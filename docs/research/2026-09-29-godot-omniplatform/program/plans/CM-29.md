## 0. Binding decisions

**Owner, 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08 (not reopened).**

1. Commerce is a service: slug `commerce`, `requires: ["license"]`. `defaultEnabled: false` is the
   lead's decision, since no product sells today (D2).
2. The 0.9 line removes instead of deprecating (`tracks.md` rule 6). The only windows are the
   two-release DB contracts, break-glass, and native-binary paths until DJDL ships a 0.9 build.
3. Store notifications are never lost.
4. Commerce has its own red-orange accent. The distinctness floor drops only as far as it needs.
5. The wire change rides W-LX, with CM-29 as its first member, ahead of LX-18.

**The requirement rule (ST-38).** A service can be on only while its requirements are on. Turning
a requirement off turns its dependents off in the same audited batch, and the confirmation names
them (`?dryRun=1`, `core/servicesAdmin.ts:207-229`). Turning it back on does not restore them. A
manifest that turns a dependent on with its requirement off fails validation. The console stops
pulling requirements on (`Services.tsx:187-212`).

**D1. The store hooks are canonical and stay put.** Play's endpoint is a Pub/Sub push subscription
in the developer's GCP project, which Polaris Key cannot edit, and Apple's URL passes only the ASC
write gate, which admits this URL alone. Moving either would be a human step per product.

**D2. The device routes get no native-binary window.** Every `binding` call inserts a row
(`commerce/state.ts:253-280`). On 2026-10-07, production had 0 bindings, purchases, store products
and commerce settings on all four products, so no installed binary has called these routes. §3.5
re-checks this immediately before the deploy.

**D3. The layout follows CQW-14.** The code lives in `services/commerce/`, with per-store facets in
`services/commerce/stores/<store>/`. The shared client, credentials and events live in
`core/stores/<store>/` (P0-27a).

## 1. Summary

- `tools/services.json` gains `commerce`; every SDK's capability map gains it. The code moves to

- The device routes become `/<p>/commerce/{binding,claim}` in the release that moves all six SDKs.
  The store hooks keep their canonical URLs.

  answer; a store claim needs that store's channel (§3.3).

## 2. Contract

CM-29 writes three sentences as a **Commerce (CM-29)** bullet in `WIRE-CONTRACT-V4.md` §5, plus an
amendment clause in the header. LX-18's store-commerce section (CM-20 §3.3) points to them.

1. **Discovery.** `services.commerce` is `{"enabled":false}` unless the product runs Commerce. When
   on, it is `{"enabled":true,"endpoints":{"binding":"<base>/commerce/binding","claim":"<base>/commerce/claim"}}`.
   It never names a store, product id or count, and a client that does not know the slug ignores it.
2. **Device routes.** `GET /<p>/commerce/binding` and `POST /<p>/commerce/claim` take a device token
   and P6-01's bodies. While Commerce is off, both answer `404 {"error":{"code":"not_found"}}`,
   byte-identical to an unknown route (`core/registry.ts:368-370,385`).
3. **Store hooks.** `POST /<p>/distribution/hooks/app-store` and `…/play-rtdn` are Commerce's
   canonical, store-facing routes. No SDK calls them, and `/<p>/commerce/hooks/*` does not exist.

§9 gains: "CM-29 adds the unsigned discovery member `services.commerce` and moves two device
routes. Every counter is unchanged, because an SDK that predates it ignores the member."
`shared-protocol`, `shared-jws` and `client-core` do not change. LX-18 adds the
`@polaris-key/protocol/commerce` subpath for CM-20's types.

### 3.2 Layout, routing and ownership

### 3.3 Readiness

resolves and its channel is set up (a live outlet of its kind in `hooks.delivery()?.outlets()`,
which is null while Distribution is off). The channel is a readiness fact, not a requirement.
With Distribution off, Commerce still serves manual and external sources (LX-41) and Polaris Key
checkout (CM-01 onward), and its hooks answer.
A store claim needs that store's channel once CM-21 gates claims on Ready (CM-20 D14); until then,
claims keep today's per-store gate. Hooks are never gated on readiness. Commerce on with no
storefront Ready opens ST-38's not-set-up component, which names each store's missing fact

## 8. Risks and open questions

**Decided.** Q1: the owner chose a ninth accent (CM-29b). Q2: CM-29 is W-LX's first member
(`tracks.md`). Q3: NO. Q4 stands: a disabled service runs no code, and no hook answers while
Commerce is off.

**Q5. How does P0-27's dependency narrow?** CM-29 needs only the store-client move. **Recommend
splitting it out as P0-27a**, "Store clients and events to `core/stores/`", which depends on P0-17.
Its acceptance is that commerce imports only `core/` and its own directory. P0-27 then depends on
A-19 and P0-27a, and A-19 leaves CM-29's chain. The alternative, CM-29 doing the move itself, adds
about half a week to CM-29.

**Q6. Which App Store catch-up does CM-22 use?** Either it re-verifies every active App Store
purchase, or it reads Apple's notification history from Commerce's last tick.
**Lead decision (2026-10-08): notification history first, re-verify as the fallback.** Apple's Get
Notification History returns the last six months of version 2 notifications (verified 2026-10-08),
so a gap of up to six months replays exactly what was missed, refunds and renewals included. A
longer gap falls back to re-verifying every active App Store purchase with CM-22's action.

**Risks.**

- **Vendor figures (verified 2026-10-08 against Apple's and Google's docs):** Apple ASSN v2 retries
  five times at 1, 12, 24, 48 and 72 h, production only; Apple's notification history covers six
  months; Pub/Sub keeps unacknowledged messages 7 days and deletes a subscription after 31 days
  without a successful push; Play's voided-purchases list reaches back 30 days.
- **A Pub/Sub subscription deleted while Commerce is off** is not recovered by the catch-up.
  While Commerce is off every push fails, so after 31 days Pub/Sub deletes the subscription. Three
  guards: CM-21 creates or updates the push subscription with expiration **never** where it
  provisions it, and its setup steps tell the developer to set it where it doesn't; the Commerce
  off confirmation warns that Play notifications stop being kept after 7 days and the subscription
  may be deleted after 31; and CM-21's notifications check shows a missing subscription, which the
  developer recreates.
- **A manifest-sourced product that sells** must declare `modules.commerce` (§3.5). Production has
  none.
