## 0. Decisions

1. **No signed-document change.** Every new behaviour is expressed through members SDKs already
   enforce or ignore: the enforced entitlements `app.maxVersion`, `channels`, `license.tier` and
   `license.tierLabel` (WIRE-CONTRACT-V4.md:103) and the envelope's `graceUntil` (§3.6, :577). The
   build gate stays server-side (:648; the client only reflects the 403, `client-core/src/gate.ts:1-4`).
   What old SDKs cannot do is _explain_ a lapse; that is LX-18's `term` member (§2.5).
2. **Durations live on the two templates and are stamped at sale.** A tier carries
   `policyExpiryDays` and the new `onExpiry`. Every issuance and re-tier copies both onto the licence
   through `tierDuration`: admin create and PATCH, batches, enrolment, OIDC issuance and the enrolment
   claim, a subscription with `tierId`, and trial conversion. This is how `tierExpiresAt` already
   copies the expiry (`core/authz.ts:231-236`). The sign-in upgrade is not a sale and stamps
   nothing (§2.3). An add-on's duration is its grant's own `expires_at` and always stops. Stamping
   keeps `licenseState` a pure function of one row, so the ~30 `licenseUsable` call sites need no
   tier read, and a tier edit never rewrites contracts already sold (Q1).
3. **`licenseState` replaces the body of `licenseUsable`, not its call sites.**
   `licenseUsable(l, now)` (`core/devices.ts:196-203`) becomes `licenseState(l, now).usable`, so every
   gate gets the new states at once. The one gate that differs (package feeds) calls
   `licenseState` itself (§2.2, Q2).
4. **Transitions are written by a tick, never by a read.** The License service gains a `scheduled`
   hook, which runs every 15 minutes per product (`core/registry.ts:314-320`, `scheduled.ts:505`,
   `wrangler.toml:98`). Between an expiry and the next tick a keep-version licence is usable and
   uncapped and a trial is still on its tier: the gap fails open, in the customer's favour, for at
   most one tick.
5. **The cap is one clamp in the limits resolver, and it binds only while the licence is lapsed.**
   The resolver reads `licenseState(l, now).versionCap`, never the raw column. That value is a
   version only on §2.2's fourth row: `status` `active`, `on_expiry` `keepVersion`, a non-NULL
   `expires_at` at or before `now`, and a non-empty `fallback_version`. Then `app.maxVersion`
   becomes `tighterMax(windowMax, versionCap)` (`core/entitlements.ts:83-90`) and the base channel
   set is `["stable"]` (add-ons still add, C-04). It binds wherever a version window binds today:
   the licence document's build gate, `entitled` delivery, and portal downloads under `entitled`
   release access.
6. **The fallback comes from the release catalog, else from devices.** The source is the newest
   `stable` release of the `app` deliverable (`APP_DELIVERABLE_ID`, `shared-manifest/src/index.ts:1100`).
   It must be published at or before `expires_at`, not yanked, and inside the licence's window, and
   it is read through `releaseCatalog().channelReleases(APP_DELIVERABLE_ID, "stable")`
   (`core/hooks.ts:533-536`). Release may be off, or the catalog may hold no stable `app` release at
   all (a store-only distributor). Then the source is the highest `devices.app_version` the licence's
   devices reported inside the window. If nothing is found, the licence ends like `stop`.
7. **A trial is `onExpiry: tier:<id>`, one hop, converted in place.** The licence keeps its id, keys
   and devices; only its tier, expiry and `onExpiry` change, through the re-tier path that R3-06
   made authoritative (`core/authz.ts:219-230`).
8. **One Core `subscriptions` table** for `manual`, `external`, `app-store`, `play` and `polaris-key`,
   states `trialing`, `active`, `past_due`, `canceled` and `expired`, targeting a licence or a grant,
   with one live subscription per target. A subscription moves its target's `expires_at` to the
   period end plus a 24-hour constant buffer (S-22's `renewalBufferHours` is not created); dunning
   adds `dunningGraceDays`. Commerce keeps provider references only (C-16).
9. **Two planes, one module.** The console manages `manual` subscriptions; a developer's billing
   system reports `external` ones through one idempotent CI-plane upsert authenticated by a
   `pkeyci_` token with a new opt-in scope `license:subscriptions` (C-28: machines use `pkeyci_`).
   Both call `core/licensing/subscriptions.ts`, which LX-23 and CM-08 call too. A static
   `pkeyci_` token lives at most 90 days (`STATIC_CI_TOKEN_MAX_TTL_SECONDS`, `core/publisher.ts:86`),
   so the integration has to rotate it (§2.4, Q4).
10. **The grace clamp is null for `keepVersion` only** (`core/graceClamp.ts:112-124`): a lapsed
    licence keeps working offline at its version. Trials and subscriptions keep the clamp.
11. **Emails only for transitions the tick writes**: trial converted, updates ended (keeps a
    version), ended (nothing to keep). The guarded write that changed one row is the dedupe, so each
    is sent at most once and needs no new state.
12. **Split by lane.** LX-41 is the worker; LX-41b the console. The W-LX critical path
    (LX-41 → LX-18 → LX-19) then never waits on LX-44 or ST-07.

## 1. Summary

- Tiers and licences gain `onExpiry`: `stop` (today), `keepVersion` (after lapse the licence runs
  the last version released while it was active, JetBrains-style) or `tier:<id>` (a trial that
  converts, for example 14 days of Pro, then Free).
- `licenseState(license, now)` gives `active`, `lapsed`, `converting`, `expired`, `ended`,
  `suspended` or `none`, with `usable`, `versionCap` and the 401 `reason` LX-18 will send.
- A Core `subscriptions` table and `core/licensing/subscriptions.ts` make renewing licences work
  with Commerce off: the console's Renew…, the developer's own billing through one route, and later
  App Store and Play (LX-23) and Stripe (CM-08).
- "1.x forever, 2.x subscription" is one licence per major line, each with its own duration; LX-41
  ships the duration half and its fixture, LX-33 the anchor-only window, LX-10 the device moving
  between lines, LX-43 the "1.x" helper and presets.
- No wire, corpus or SDK change. Existing licences have `on_expiry` NULL (= `stop`) and no
  subscription, so every document and transcript is byte-identical.

### 2.3 The cap, the fallback and conversion

- **Clamp.** The clamp lives in `resolveLicenseTerms` (LX-32 and LX-33; today `injectAdminPolicy`,
  `core/entitlements.ts:182-234`). There, `licenseState(l, now).versionCap` caps `app.maxVersion`
  and sets the base channels to `["stable"]`, so the tier no longer grants `dev`. The resolver gains
  a `now` parameter. Its six callers already hold one (`core/authz.ts:150,192`,
  `core/entitledAccess.ts:184,373,414`, `core/syncAccess.ts:77`). The entries keep
  `updatedAt = licenses.modified_at` (`:189`). The `0.0.0-dev*` bypass (`core/gate.ts:95-97`) still
  opens for a product with `allowDevBuilds` or for an add-on that grants `dev`. That gives nothing a
  modified client cannot already get, because `X-PKey-Version` is client-asserted (THREAT-MODEL §6).
- **Clearing.** Every write that sets `expires_at` or `on_expiry` goes through
  `setLicenseExpiryStatements({product, licenseId, tierId?, expiresAt, onExpiry, now, guard})`.
  It writes `tier_id` (when given), `expires_at`, `on_expiry` and `modified_at`, plus:

  ```sql
  fallback_version = CASE
    WHEN expires_at IS :e AND on_expiry IS :o AND :e IS NOT NULL AND :e <= :now
    THEN fallback_version ELSE NULL END
  ```

  The frozen version therefore survives only a write that leaves the term exactly as it was and
  still lapsed. A NULL (lifetime) expiry, a future expiry, or
  any change to `expires_at` or `on_expiry` clears it. SQLite evaluates the `CASE` against the row's
  current values, so no stale read is involved. Clearing and the lapsed-only clamp together uncap a
  licence at once when it is made lifetime (PATCH `expiresAt: null`,
  `services/license/admin/licenses.ts:563`), re-tiered to a lifetime tier (`tierExpiresAt` → null,
  `:573`), or converted or claimed onto one.

- **Writers.** `setLicenseExpiryStatements` is called by:
  - the admin PATCH and re-tier: `patchLicense`'s `fields` type (`admin/repo.ts:309-344`) loses
    `expires_at` and `tier_id`, so its dynamic `SET` cannot write them;
  - subscriptions (§2.4);
  - `convertTrial`;
  - the enrolment claim: `claimEnrolledLicense` (`src/repo.ts:1010`) drops `tier_id` and
    `expires_at` from its `UPDATE` (`:1024-1027`) and is batched with the statements built from
    `identityTier`'s `tierDuration` (`services/identity/oidc.ts:836`).

  The inserts (`stmtInsertLicense`, `src/repo.ts:956`; `services/license/batches.ts:135`) write
  `on_expiry` from `tierDuration` and `fallback_version` NULL. `freezeFallback` and the PATCH's
  `fallbackVersion` correction build their statements in the same file.

  **Left out on purpose:** the sign-in upgrade (`updateLicenseOnSignIn`, `services/identity/oidc.ts:1198`,
  `syncTierOnSignIn: upgradeOnly`) moves `tier_id` alone. LX-02 decided that a sign-in never moves a
  licence's term (`oidc.ts:1083-1087`), so it writes none of `expires_at`, `on_expiry` or
  `fallback_version`. A lapsed licence that it upgrades stays lapsed at its frozen version,
  intersected with the new tier's window. A trial that it upgrades still converts at its end, where
  today it would stop, and the next sign-in upgrades it again.

  P0-18's writer-location test is per table, so LX-41 adds a per-column test,
  `test/durationWriters.test.ts`. Every SQL string in `src/` that assigns `licenses.expires_at`,
  `on_expiry`, `fallback_version` or `tier_id` must live in `core/licensing/duration.ts` or in the
  two inserts. The one named exception is the sign-in upgrade's `tier_id`.

- **Freeze** (`freezeFallback`, in the tick) covers each `active` licence with
  `on_expiry = 'keepVersion'`, `fallback_version IS NULL` and `expires_at <= now`. The query also
  states `on_expiry IS NOT NULL AND on_expiry <> 'stop'`, because SQLite uses the partial index
  `idx_licenses_duration_due` only when the query repeats its terms, and `= 'keepVersion'` alone does
  not imply them. For each row it computes decision 6's fallback. The window is the limits
  resolver's, intersected with the product's compat range (`versionWindow`,
  `core/entitlements.ts:119-131`; `compareSemver`). Releases with a NULL `publishedAt` are skipped,
  and `channelReleases` is read once per product per tick. It writes
  `fallback_version = <version or ''>, modified_at = now`, guarded by
  `fallback_version IS NULL AND expires_at = <read value>`. An operator can correct the value
  through the licence PATCH (`fallbackVersion`: a version inside the window, lapsed licences only,
  audited).
- **Convert** (`convertTrial`, in the tick) covers each `active` licence with
  `on_expiry GLOB 'tier:*'` and `expires_at <= now`, with the same index terms. Through
  `setLicenseExpiryStatements` it sets `tier_id` to the target and `expires_at` to the old expiry
  plus the target's `policy_expiry_days` (or NULL). It sets `on_expiry` to the target's and clears
  `fallback_version`, guarded by the old `on_expiry` and `expires_at`. A deleted target writes
  `on_expiry = 'stop'` (the licence then ends) and audits `license.duration.convert_failed`. Licence
  overrides (channels, window, seats) survive.
- **Re-tier with a live subscription** keeps the subscription's `expires_at` and stamps the new
  tier's `on_expiry`. Today `services/license/admin/licenses.ts:573` would re-derive the expiry from
  the tier.
- `tierExpiresAt` becomes `tierDuration(tier, from) → {expires_at, on_expiry}` at every issuance
  site: `services/license/admin/licenses.ts:366,573`, `admin/batches.ts:311`, `enroll.ts:90`, and
  `services/identity/oidc.ts:836` (OIDC issuance and the claim). If P0-20 has landed, the site is
  its `core/licensing/issue.ts`.

### 2.4 Core subscriptions

| Transition                                  | Called by                                                       | Target's `expires_at` (one D1 batch with the row and one audit row)                |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| none → `trialing` or `active`               | console start (`manual`), the upsert (`external`), LX-23, CM-08 | period end + 24 h; re-tier to `tierId` if given; fallback cleared                  |
| live → `active`, later period end           | renew                                                           | period end + 24 h; fallback cleared                                                |
| `trialing`/`active` → `past_due`            | a failed payment                                                | max(period end + 24 h, period end + `dunningGraceDays` d, a store's own grace end) |
| `trialing`/`active`/`past_due` → `canceled` | cancel at period end                                            | unchanged; the term ends there and follows `onExpiry`                              |
| `canceled` → `active`                       | resume, before the end                                          | unchanged                                                                          |
| live → `expired`                            | cancel now                                                      | now                                                                                |
| live → `expired`                            | the tick, once the target's expiry has passed                   | unchanged                                                                          |
| `expired` → `active` (same ref)             | a late `external` renewal                                       | as start, if no other live subscription holds the target                           |

Bounds: `period` matches `^P[1-9][0-9]{0,2}[DWMY]$`; a period end is in the future and at most
1100 days ahead. Months add in UTC with the day clamped. An `external` ref is 1–256 printable ASCII
without `@`. Every source stores its reference only as `sha256Hex("<source>:" + ref)`, the
`purchaseKeyHash` convention (`services/distribution/commerce/state.ts:52-55`, which CM-29 moves
to `services/commerce/state.ts`), so a store row joins the purchase ledger on it
(`plans/CM-20.md` §2.4). Every function takes the row's `modified_at` as an optimistic guard, so
two callers cannot both win a transition. The dunning setting is read through one constant,
`DUNNING_GRACE_SETTING` (today `licensing.dunningGraceDays`; LX-40 renames it).

- **`tierId`.** A start or upsert with `tierId` re-tiers through `tierDuration` and stamps the new
  tier's `on_expiry`. The subscription's period end plus 24 h replaces the tier's
  `policy_expiry_days`. Without `tierId`, the licence keeps its tier and `on_expiry`. A trial
  licence therefore keeps its `tier:<id>`, which applies when the subscription lapses.
- **Resubscribing.** A start or upsert from the same source may hit a target whose live row is
  `canceled`. The new subscription supersedes it: one batch sets the old row to `expired` and
  inserts the new one, so the one-live index never sees two live rows. A live row that is
  `trialing`, `active` or `past_due`, or a `canceled` row from another source, answers 409
  `subscription_exists`. A known `externalRef` that arrives with a different `licenseId` answers
  409 `subscription_target` and changes nothing.
- **Token lifetime.** A static `pkeyci_` token lives at most 90 days (`core/publisher.ts:86`). Once
  it expires, the upsert answers 401, and every licence the integration renews lapses at its period
  end plus 24 h. Four things cover this:
  - the upsert's 200 carries `token: {expiresAt}`;
  - `services/license/subscriptions.md` and the RUNBOOK give the rotation (mint the successor,
    deploy it, revoke the old token; several tokens can be live at once);
  - LX-41b shows the expiry on the subscription panel;
  - ST-44's attention read gains a kind for a `license:subscriptions` token that expires within 14
    days while the product has a live `external` subscription (§13, Q4).

### 2.5 What LX-18 carries

`licenseTerm(row, tier, subscription) → LicenseTerm` is the one summary for the console, the portal,
LX-13's `subjects/<s>/entitlements` and LX-18:

```ts
export interface LicenseTerm {
  endsAt: number | null;
  onExpiry: "stop" | "keepVersion" | "tier";
  nextTier: { id: string; label: string } | null;
  state: "active" | "lapsed" | "converting"; // a device gets no document in any other state
  fallbackVersion: string | null;
  trial: boolean; // onExpiry tier:<id>, or a trialing subscription
  renews: {
    at: number;
    source: SubscriptionSource;
    status: SubscriptionStatus;
  } | null;
}
```

Recommendation to LX-18's plan: serialise it as the `term` member outside the claims
(WIRE-CONTRACT-V4 §3.2), never with a subscription id or a provider reference. The 401 `reason` is
`licenseState().reason`. `term` takes the place of the `licenseExpiresAt` member that W3 planned
and that no Worker has sent (`sdk-node/src/license/client.ts:148`). That member is therefore never
added, so nothing has to be removed later (rule 6). LX-18 owns the encoding, the corpus rows and
the transcripts.

### 2.6 Clients and Workers already deployed

- **Old SDKs.** Nothing changes for an existing licence. On an opted-in licence they show their
  generic blocked state for a build above the cap (with `allowedRange`), a tier change after a trial,
  and today's 401 after an expiry. No state is lost.
- **New SDKs on an old Worker** (after LX-19): no `term`; they behave as old SDKs.
- **The old Worker during the deploy window** never names the new columns or table, and the
  `on_expiry` triggers pass its inserts (NULL). It would refuse a lapsed `keepVersion` licence and
  not convert trials; none exists until an operator opts in after the deploy.

- **WIRE-CONTRACT-V4 §3.6** (informative only). The LX-07 note (`:577`, "on every document the
  licence grants") gains "except a licence whose term ends in `keepVersion`". No normative text,
  corpus file or `PROTOCOL_VERSION` changes.
- **THREAT-MODEL** gets a new subsection, "Durations and Core subscriptions (LX-41)", after LX-07's
  (`THREAT-MODEL.md:7525`). LX-07's "The clamp" bullet (`:7533-7538`) and §6 (`:7786`, `:7793-7796`)
  gain the `keepVersion` exception: such a licence outlives its term by design, at its frozen
  version.
  - **Cap.** Enforced server-side by the build gate and `entitled` delivery. `X-PKey-Version` is
    client-asserted, so a modified client can claim an old version, or a `0.0.0-dev*` build where
    `allowDevBuilds` or a `dev` add-on opens the bypass. That is today's §6 promise.
  - **Freeze.** Server-side, frozen once by a guarded write, audited. With a stable `app` release in
    the catalog it is never taken from a client. Without one, it is the highest version the
    licence's devices reported inside the window. `devices.app_version` holds only each device's
    latest report, which may postdate the end (between the expiry and the tick, the licence is
    uncapped), so this source has the build gate's client-asserted trust. An operator correction is
    audited.
  - **What a lapse keeps.** A lapsed `keepVersion` licence is `usable`, so it keeps config secrets,
    edge-minted tokens (`services/config/mint.ts`), attestation and Cloud Sync storage with no end
    date. An operator who sells backend access sells it as an add-on with its own term, and an
    add-on's grant `expires_at` always stops.
  - **Trials** convert only to an operator-declared tier, one hop, validated on write.
  - **Grace.** `keepVersion` documents keep the full offline window; trials and subscriptions keep
    the LX-07 clamp.
  - **Upsert.** A `license:subscriptions` token can extend any licence of its product, the same
    power as an operator's comp: opt-in scope, product-bound, `external` rows only, a bounded period
    end, a hashed ref, audited as `ci:<subject>`, rate-limited, revocable and expiring after at
    most 90 days. An expired token fails closed for renewals: licences lapse at period end plus
    24 h (§2.4 surfaces it). One live subscription per licence (`REQUIRED_INDEXES`) stops two
    sources racing its expiry. Dunning extends a term by at most 30 days.
  - **Tick.** Idempotent guarded writes. A stalled cron fails open for its lag; the Operations page
    shows the job runs.
- **PRIVACY.md** ("Where it lives", `:176`), two rows:
  - `subscriptions`: the licence or grant, source, a hashed provider reference, status, period,
    period end, times and who changed it; no payment data and no person. Kept while the licence or
    grant exists; deleted with it and with the product.
  - `licenses.on_expiry` and `fallback_version`: a licence term, derived from the release catalog
    or from app versions its devices already report ("Software facts"). The duration emails go to
    the holder's existing address, once per transition.

## 11. Risks

- **Every gate reads `licenseState`.** Mitigated by the per-gate table, the property test and
  `lapsed` being reachable only after an opt-in.
- **Fallback wrong** (a prerelease, a yanked release, another channel): stable only, yanks removed by
  `channelReleases`, published at or before the end, inside the window, frozen once, correctable.
- **Update feeds** list releases above a cap, as they already do above any licence window; the
  download and the build gate refuse them. Unchanged behaviour, noted in the docs.
- **Cron lag** extends a trial or an uncapped lapse by at most the lag (decision 4).
- **An expired external billing token** stops renewals reaching the Worker, and the customers'
  licences lapse. Mitigated by the 90-day limit being visible in the upsert's answer, the panel, the
  docs and ST-44's attention kind (§2.4, Q4).

## 14. Review notes

- **No rule-9 warning for `keepVersion` or `tier:<id>` on a tier without `policyExpiryDays`.** The
  review suggested one, saying such an `onExpiry` never takes effect. That is not so: a
  subscription sets `expires_at` to its period end plus 24 h (§2.4) whatever the tier says, and
  fixture 1's Pro tier is exactly this case. A warning would fire on every subscription tier.
- **Why the clamp now takes `now`.** The earlier text said the clamp "reads no clock", which was
  safe only if every writer cleared the version, and a NULL expiry did not clear it. §2.3 now uses
  both guards: a clearing rule in SQL, and a clamp that binds only while the licence is lapsed.
