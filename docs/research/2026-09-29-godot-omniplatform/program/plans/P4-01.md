## 1. Summary

- **A pack release is one CI-signed `pkey-release+jws` record with `kind: pack`**, variants
  inside. Every object it names is pinned by an object ref `{sha256, bytes, size, codec}` over the
  stored bytes, and per-file detail lives in hash-pinned side objects: the `pkey-files/1` index,
  one zstd frame with a blob ref per file, and for the per-entry delta set a `pkey-patch/1`
  descriptor plus one packed data object. A record with three variants and two deltas each is
  4,132 bytes, whatever the entry count (§2.3).
- **App records fill the reserved `content: {contentApi, pins[], expects[]}`, and builds gain
  `embeds[]`.** The running build learns its own pins from an embedded content stamp
  (`pkey-content/1`) and its embedded packs from markers (`pkey-marker/1`, the compact pack record
  beside the payload), so required-set checks are local and offline (CONTENT F11). Both reach the
  record through the release descriptor, which `descriptorToRecord` moves (decision 37). No new
  `typ`, feed field or device route: `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 4, and a v4 SDK that predates packs
  keeps every verdict it acts on (§2.1).
- **Claims stay forward-compatible and are pinned one by one.** They check structure, patterns,
  presence, uniqueness and the integer rule, and no claim relates two integer members. A value
  outside an extensible vocabulary (type, method, scope, codec, layout, activation, delivery, axis)
  only makes a variant, delta or pack unusable on an SDK that lacks it, never the record invalid,
  so v2 and v3 records verify in v1 SDKs (§2.2). Each of the 80 claim checks has a negative case
  (§4.6).
- **Corpus.** `conformance/corpus/v2/content/` (`cases.json` and `blobs/`, about 2.7 MB, kept in the
  source tree only), `plan-matrix.json` (A7's 23 rows plus 2, `variantCases`, `targetCases`),
  `cases.json` gains `packRecordCases` and `markerCases`, and `stage-matrix.json` goes to version 3
  (consent, progress and a playable offline). `tools/gen-content-corpus.ts` regenerates everything
  from committed inputs, the blobs and `content/blobs/refs.json` (the refs of the objects the set
  does not ship), decoding with the decoder-only libzstd WASM that P4-21 lands as
  `@polaris-key/zstd-wasm` (§4). Every applier reads a delta frame's header and refuses a window
  above its limit before decoding, because no decoder parameter does so reliably (§2.7).
- **The operator gates a pack.** The pack's own `dist_access` row (P2b-04, operator-owned) holds its
  licence flag; CI signs that value into the record, and the stage round and ingest refuse anything
  that differs. `.pkey/release` may only assert it (decision 35).
- **Order.** P4-21 (P3-02 has merged) → P4-02 and P4-03 (P3-03 has merged) and P4-04 → P4-05;
  SDKs P4-06, then P4-07 and P4-08. CI publishes a pack in 256-object stage rounds and then one
  record submit, and keeps its own copies of delta bases; the Worker decodes one files index at a
  time, at most 8 MiB, under a third of an isolate (§6). `packs` stays a reserved decision name,
  which P4-13 adds with the rows that produce it; v1 adds no `update-matrix.json` row. Error codes
  are A7's registry, spelled in kebab case (§2.9).

### 2.2 What a claim refuses, and what only makes a thing unusable

Beyond V4 §2.5's common claims, a P4 verifier refuses a record at step 14 (`claims`) only for the
80 checks of §4.6's table and the integer rule (V4 §3.1) at §2.5's paths, which no later package
relaxes. The checks are of these kinds: a wrong JSON type, a pattern miss, a missing required
member or a present `null` where the type has none (V4 §3.3 rule 3), a duplicate where uniqueness
is stated, a count or length outside its bound, and these structural invariants:

- `codec: none` means `bytes === size`, within one object ref;
- a `container` variant has `gaps` and a `tree` variant has none; a `payload` delta is refused on a
  `tree` variant. For any other layout `gaps` is optional (an object ref when present) and a
  `payload` delta is allowed, because the variant is unusable anyway (`pack-valid-unknown-layout`);
- a `payload` delta has `artifact` and a `files` delta has `patch` and `data`; a delta of another
  scope requires neither, and a member of the other scope is ignored, not refused
  (`pack-valid-foreign-delta-members`);
- every variant of a record declares the same axis names, and variant keys are unique;
- a pack id is never `app`.

**No claim relates two integer members**, so every integer path is broken alone by its own cases
(§2.5). In particular `full.size` need not equal `payload.size` for a record to verify
(`pack-valid-full-size-differs`): such a `full` object is unusable, `planTarget` maps it to null
and `applyFull` refuses it before decoding (`full-corrupt`), and the CLI and ingest never publish
one (§6). §2.3 applies to `kind: pack` and §2.4 to `kind: app`; a record of any other kind keeps
the common claims only, and a `content` or `embeds` member on it is ignored
(`content-valid-ignored-on-unknown-kind`).

Everything a later package extends is checked only for its pattern. A value the SDK does not know
makes the governed thing unusable on that SDK, and the record still verifies:

| Field                    | v1 values                                                                   | An unknown value makes                                                                                         |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `type`                   | `godot.pck`, `files.tree`                                                   | the pack unusable (`pack-type-unsupported`)                                                                    |
| `formatVersion`          | per handler (`godot.pck`: the PCK header's format version; `files.tree`: 1) | the pack unusable (`pack-type-unsupported`)                                                                    |
| `handler.activation`     | `restart`, `hot`                                                            | the pack unusable (`pack-type-unsupported`)                                                                    |
| `files.format`           | `pkey-files/1`                                                              | the variant's index unusable: no `file` strategy or `files` delta; a tree variant is unusable and not selected |
| `files.layout`           | `container`, `tree`                                                         | the variant unusable: `selectVariant` skips it                                                                 |
| any object ref's `codec` | `zstd`, `none`                                                              | that object unusable (§2.9's `planTarget` rules)                                                               |
| `deltas[].method`        | `zstd-patch-from`                                                           | the delta infeasible (the planner's method check)                                                              |
| `deltas[].scope`         | `payload`, `files`                                                          | the delta dropped by `planTarget`                                                                              |
| variant axis names       | `texture`, `locale`, `quality`                                              | the variant ineligible unless the host supplies preferences for that axis                                      |
| `expects[].delivery`     | `essential`, `prefetch`, `on-demand`                                        | the pack read as `on-demand`                                                                                   |

Cross-record rules (every expected pack is pinned, `embeds` ⊆ pins, a required pack has no
entitlement, a pack record's `entitlement` is the pack's delivery gate, a variant's axes are
declared) are publish rules, enforced by the CLI and by ingest, never by a client: P4-12 makes
unpinned `expects` valid, and a v1 client must verify the app records that P4-12-era CI signs.

A **pack id** is a string matching `DELIVERABLE_ID_PATTERN` `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]*(\.[a-z0-9-]+)*$`,
at most 64 bytes, and not `app` (`isPackId` in client-core). It is the rule for a pack record's
`deliverable`, and for `pins[].pack`, `expects[].pack` and `builds[].embeds[]` (§2.4).

An **object ref** is an object `{sha256, bytes, size, codec}`: `sha256` 64 lowercase hex and
`bytes` an integer ≥ 0, the SHA-256 and length of the stored bytes (so the blob route's `ETag` and
`Repr-Digest` equal them, README §3.5); `size` an integer ≥ 0, the decoded length (a row may raise
either minimum to 1); `codec` a string matching `VOCAB_TOKEN_PATTERN`. `zstd` means exactly one
zstd frame with its content size; `none` means stored raw and is refused unless `bytes === size`.
All four members are required, even for `none`, because Godot's `decompress` needs the size (A7
§5). Every SDK checks object refs with one function, `objectRef(value, pointer, minBytes,
minSize)`, called at `full`, `files`, `gaps` and `patch`; §4.6 relies on that for its cases.

The **variant key** is the canonical string of a variant: its `axis=value` pairs sorted by axis
name bytes and joined with `;` (`locale=fr;texture=astc`), and the empty string for `{}`.

**Size (brief item 3).** The record names no file, so the entry count never enters it. Measured on
compact JSON built to these shapes (the example's members, 64-hex hashes): the envelope, every
member but `variants`, is 453 bytes; `,"variants":[` and `]` add 14; a variant without `deltas` is
600; a `payload` delta 244 and a `files` delta 364; `,"deltas":[`, the comma between the two
deltas and `]` add 13 per variant; and two commas separate three variants. The acceptance worst
case, a Diceroll-sized pack of 625 entries with three variants and one delta of each scope per
variant, is 453 + 14 + 3 × (600 + 244 + 364 + 13) + 2 = 453 + 14 + 3,663 + 2 = **4,132 bytes**,
6.3% of the 65,536-byte cap. As a compact JWS it is 94 + 1 + 5,510 + 1 + 86 = 5,692 bytes (header,
dot, ⌈4,132 × 4 / 3⌉, dot, signature), inside `MAX_RECORD_JWS_BYTES`. The 625 entries live in the
files index: about 263 bytes each raw with the blob ref, 164 KB, and about 52 KB as one zstd frame
(measured 3.1×). The bounds alone do not keep a record under the cap (32 variants of 16 `files`
deltas would be about 207 KB), so the cap is the limit, as for app builds: the CLI refuses to sign
and ingest refuses to store a record over it (P3-01 §2.4). A long `notes` member counts against the
same cap. `MAX_RECORD_JWS_BYTES` is unchanged.

**How they reach the record (decision 37).** An app record is the release descriptor moved into a

### 2.5 New integer claims and their minimums (V4 §3.1)

Every new integer is an integer claim under P3-01 §2.2's rule (a plain integer token from its
minimum to 2^53 − 1, decided from the token through the pointer sets), and gets a token case, a
bound case and a minimum case (P3-01 §4.3). None is implied by another check: no claim relates two
integer members (§2.2), and every integer case's object refs use codec `zstd`, so `codec: none`'s
`bytes === size` never ties two paths together. Each case therefore breaks its path alone, and
P3-02's per-path self-check (§4.2) applies unchanged.

| Path (`*` is any index)                                               | Minimum |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| `/formatVersion`                                                      | 1       |
| `/handler/mountOrder`                                                 | 0       |
| `/variants/*/payload/size`                                            | 0       |
| `/variants/*/full/bytes`, `/variants/*/full/size`                     | 0       |
| `/variants/*/files/bytes`, `/variants/*/files/size`                   | 1       |
| `/variants/*/files/gaps/bytes`, `/variants/*/files/gaps/size`         | 0       |
| `/variants/*/deltas/*/memBytes`                                       | 1       |
| `/variants/*/deltas/*/artifact/bytes`                                 | 1       |
| `/variants/*/deltas/*/patch/bytes`, `/variants/*/deltas/*/patch/size` | 1       |
| `/variants/*/deltas/*/data/bytes`                                     | 1       |
| `/content/contentApi`                                                 | 1       |
| `/content/pins/*/release/seq`                                         | 1       |

That is 16 paths: 16 token, 16 bound and 16 minimum cases, 48 in all (§4.6). A missing or
non-number value at a path is a presence-and-type failure, which has its own check and case in
§4.6's table (`formatVersion`, `ref.bytes`, …). The side objects (§2.7), the marker and the stamp
are parsed with V4 §1.2's strict JSON, and every integer member in them is decided by the same
token rule, minimum 0 unless stated; a failure there is the side object's own code, not a claims
step.

### 2.6 Verification order

**Pack records.** The device fetches a pinned pack record by its pin's `sha256` from
`release.endpoints.record` (P3-03's `GET /{product}/release/records/{sha256}`, which serves every
stored record), and runs V4 §3.5's steps 12–15 with `pin: {kind: "pack", deliverable: <pack id>,
version, seq}`. Step 15 becomes: "`kind` equals the pin's `kind`, `app` when the pin names none;
`deliverable`, `version` and `seq` equal the pin's". The refusal codes stay P3-01's
`record-rejected` (`hash`, `jws`, `claims`) and `record-mismatch`. Pack records verify against the
pinned release keys only, as app records do; P4-19's content keys come later.

**Markers (V4 §3.7, [C] via `markerCases`).** The marker's `release` member is a compact pack
record:

1. The marker file's bytes pass V4 §1.2's strict JSON (`format`).
2. `format === "pkey-marker/1"`; `packId` is a pack id (§2.3); `version` matches `VERSION_RE`;
   `release` is a string (`format`).
3. Steps 12–14 with `release` as the body and its own SHA-256 as the pin hash: the 88,844-byte
   ASCII bound (`hash`), key selection from the pinned release keys and `verifyJws` (`jws`), the
   record claims (`claims`).
4. Cross-check: `kind === "pack"`, `deliverable === packId`, `version === version`
   (`cross-check`).

It returns `{record, recordSha256}`. The host then matches its bytes (unit tests in P4-06 to P4-08,
not corpus): a single-file payload's SHA-256 and size equal some variant's `payload`; a tree's
`treeDigest`, computed from the files under its directory minus `.pkey/`, equals some variant's
`payload.sha256`. When the content stamp pins that pack, `recordSha256` must equal the pin's
`sha256`, or the embedded copy is not the pinned release and is not used. A refusal is the client
code `marker-rejected` with `detail` set to the step.

**The content stamp** (§2.8) is parsed with strict JSON; `format === "pkey-content/1"`; its
`contentApi`, `pins` and `expects` pass §2.4's `content` claims. Otherwise `content-stamp-invalid`.

### 2.7 Side objects and byte rules (V4 §2.6, [C] via the content corpus)

**Where bytes come from.** Every pack object is a blob of the product's blob store,
`blobs/sha256/<sha256>` (or under `gated/` for an entitled pack), named by the SHA-256 of its
stored bytes, served by `distribution.endpoints.blobs` with `{sha256}` substituted (P2b-04's
`GET|HEAD /{product}/distribution/blobs/sha256/{sha256}`). P2-01's `deltas/<from>/<to>.<method>`
keys are not used in v1. A device checks the stored SHA-256 and length against the object ref
before it decodes anything, and the decoded length against `size`.

**`pkey-files/1`** (A7 §3.3, plus one member per entry):

```jsonc
{
  "format": "pkey-files/1",
  "layout": "container", // or "tree": no offsets, no gaps
  "payload": { "size": 5256232, "sha256": "b994…" }, // equal to the variant's payload
  "files": [
    {
      "path": ".godot/imported/t512_05.png-….s3tc.ctex",
      "offset": 1234, // container only; see the layout rule
      "size": 174828,
      "sha256": "…", // of the file's bytes
      "blob": { "sha256": "…", "bytes": 61230, "codec": "zstd" }, // the stored object of this file
    },
  ],
}
```

- **Members.** Top level: `format`, `layout`, `payload {size, sha256}` and `files`, an array of at
  most `MAX_INDEX_FILES` (100,000) entries. Each entry is an object: `path` a string (the path
  rules below); `size` an integer ≥ 0; `sha256` 64 lowercase hex; `blob` an object with `sha256`
  (64 lowercase hex), `bytes` (an integer ≥ 0) and `codec` (`zstd` or `none`); and in a container
  `offset`, an integer ≥ 0 (ignored in a tree). Unknown members are ignored. A `none` blob has
  `bytes === size` and `blob.sha256 === sha256`. Any other codec is a parse failure here, not an
  unusable object: a new codec inside an index comes with a new `format` string, which makes the
  whole index unusable instead (§2.2).
- CI publishes a blob for every file of every release, deduplicated by hash, so the `file`
  strategy works from any older release (brief item 5).
- **Layout.** Container entries are in non-decreasing offset order, and each starts at or after
  the end of the one before (`offset[i] ≥ offset[i−1] + size[i−1]`), so a zero-size entry may
  share its offset with the next, as in a real PCK (A7 §3.4); the last ends at or before
  `payload.size`; and the bytes no entry covers, `payload.size` − Σ `size`, equal `gaps.size`. Tree
  entries are in strictly ascending path byte order, and their sizes sum to `payload.size`.
- `parseFilesIndex(stored, ref, variant)` returns the first failure, in order:
  1. `ref.size` is above `MAX_FILES_INDEX_BYTES` (32 MiB, 33,554,432), checked before anything is
     fetched or decoded; the stored SHA-256 or length differs from the ref; the decode fails; or
     the decoded length differs from `ref.size` → `files-index-invalid`. The limit is not a claim:
     a later version may raise it, and a v1 SDK then finds the larger index unusable (§2.9);
  2. strict JSON or the integer rule fails → `files-index-invalid`;
  3. `format` is not `pkey-files/1`, `layout` differs from the ref's, `payload` differs from the
     variant's, or `files` or an entry breaks the member rules above → `files-index-invalid`;
  4. the path rules below, in index order → `files-unsafe-path`, `files-duplicate-path`,
     `files-case-collision` or `files-path-conflict`, with `path`;
  5. a container that breaks the layout rule → `files-layout-mismatch`; a tree whose paths are not
     strictly ascending, whose sizes do not sum to `payload.size`, or whose `treeDigest` differs
     from `payload.sha256` → `files-index-invalid`.
- The Worker decodes indexes too, at ingest and in the hook's `packFiles`, but only up to
  `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES` (8 MiB, 8,388,608), which one Workers isolate can hold beside its
  other work; the CLI publishes no larger index (§6). `MAX_FILES_INDEX_BYTES` stays the client
  limit, so a later Worker can raise its own bound without a client release.

**Path rules** are A7 §3.3's, with one addition: a first segment equal to `.pkey` (any case) is
`files-unsafe-path`, because a tree's marker lives there.

**`treeDigest`** is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the UTF-8 text made of one line
`<sha256hex> <size> <path>\n` per file, sorted by path bytes (A7 §3.3); size is decimal without
leading zeros, and an empty tree hashes the empty string.

**`pkey-patch/1`**, the descriptor of a `files`-scope delta set, packed now (brief item 7):

```jsonc
{
  "format": "pkey-patch/1",
  "scope": "files",
  "method": "zstd-patch-from",
  "from": "088b…", // the base release's payload.sha256 (a tree's treeDigest)
  "to": "b994…", // this variant's payload.sha256
  "data": { "sha256": "8898…", "bytes": 314899 }, // equal to the record's data
  "entries": [
    {
      "path": "…/t1024_03.png-….ctex",
      "op": "delta",
      "from": "<base file sha256>",
      "to": "<file sha256>",
      "size": 349656,
      "offset": 0,
      "length": 21313,
    },
    {
      "path": "assets/data/level_120.json",
      "op": "blob",
      "to": "<file sha256>",
      "size": 4410,
      "codec": "zstd",
      "offset": 21313,
      "length": 1203,
    },
  ],
}
```

- **Members.** `format` is `pkey-patch/1` and `scope` is `files`; `method`, `from` and `data`
  equal the record's delta, and `to` the variant's `payload.sha256`; `entries` is an array of at
  most `MAX_INDEX_FILES`. Each entry is an object: `path`, a path of the target index, at most once
  per descriptor; `op`, `delta` or `blob`; `to` and `size`, equal to that target entry's `sha256`
  and `size`; `offset` and `length`, integers ≥ 0; on a `delta` entry `from`, 64 lowercase hex;
  on a `blob` entry `codec`, `zstd` or `none` (`none` means `length === size`). Unknown members are
  ignored.
- Entries cover exactly the target files whose hash is not in the base: an `op: "delta"` entry for
  a path the base has with another hash (a bare `--patch-from` frame against the base file), an
  `op: "blob"` entry for an added file. Removed files vanish. A frame that is not smaller than the
  file's blob is published as a blob entry. Entries are in target index order, and their byte
  ranges follow the container layout rule: each starts at or after the end of the one before, and
  the last ends at or before `data.bytes`.
- `parsePatch` refuses with `delta-artifact-mismatch` any failure of the descriptor or the data
  object against its ref (as `parseFilesIndex` step 1, `MAX_FILES_INDEX_BYTES` included), of strict
  JSON or the integer rule, or of these member rules.
- Packing turns A7's 33-object per-file set into four requests (index, gaps, descriptor, data): on
  the measured set the data object is 314,899 bytes (35 frames, 179,951; 9 added blobs, 134,948)
  and the descriptor 4,009 bytes as one zstd frame.

**Codec, publish and decode rules** (A7 §3.2; brief items 4 and 8):

1. Every compressed object is exactly one zstd frame with the content-size flag, or stored raw
   (`codec: "none"`). CI stores raw when the frame is not smaller. There is no second codec
   (README decision 21).
2. `zstd-patch-from` frames are bare `zstd --patch-from=<base> <target>` output: content size and
   checksum on, the whole base as a raw-content prefix. The stored artifact is the bare frame; an
   edge may add the RFC 9842 `dcz` header later (P4-18).
3. Decoders apply them with raw-content prefix semantics: the whole base is the frame's prefix
   (`ZSTD_DCtx_refPrefix`, `ZSTD_dct_rawContent`, Python's `as_prefix`, `zstandard`'s
   `DICT_TYPE_RAWCONTENT`). A decoder that detects the dictionary type itself (A7 trap 2:
   `node:zlib`'s `dictionary`, zstd-jni, ZstdSharp's `LoadDictionary`) is allowed only because
   rule 5 keeps every published base from starting with the dictionary magic: on such a base it
   reports `delta-apply-failed`. No corpus case uses such a base; P4-06 to P4-08 unit-test it.

   **The window check, one rule for every SDK and for `decodeWithPrefix`.** Before it decodes a
   `zstd-patch-from` frame, an applier computes `frameWindow(frame)` (below) and refuses the frame
   as `delta-apply-failed` when that is null or above 2^`windowLogMax`, where
   `windowLogMax = max(10, min(P, ⌈log2(memBytes)⌉))`. ⌈log2(m)⌉ is the smallest integer n ≥ 0 with
   2^n ≥ m, computed in integers as the bit length of m − 1 (the bit length of m is one too high at
   every power of two, and a floating `log` rounds 2^29 and 2^31 up, measured in Python and in
   GDScript, whose only `log` is floating). P is 31 for a 64-bit decoder and 30 for a 32-bit or
   wasm32 one (libzstd's `ZSTD_WINDOWLOG_MAX_64` and `_32`). The check is the rule, and no decoder
   parameter replaces it: libzstd 1.5.7 enforces `ZSTD_d_windowLogMax` only on its buffered
   streaming path. Measured on a 5,001,000-byte target over a 5,000,000-byte base, a single-segment
   frame whose window is its content size, with the limit set to 22 (4 MiB): `ZSTD_decompressDCtx`
   with the prefix decodes it, and so does `ZSTD_decompressStream` when the output buffer holds the
   whole content (libzstd's single-pass shortcut); 64 KiB output chunks and Python's
   `compression.zstd` refuse it; `node:zlib` refuses it with its default chunk size and decodes it
   with an 8 MiB `chunkSize`. A7's `zdec.c`, the generator's decoder, calls `ZSTD_decompressDCtx`
   and sets no limit, and Godot's engine decoder takes none (A7 §6). Without the check, one corpus
   verdict would depend on each SDK's buffering. An SDK may still pass the limit to its decoder
   (`ZSTD_d_windowLogMax`, Python's `window_log_max`, `zstandard`'s `max_window_size` as
   2^windowLogMax); nothing relies on it. The floor of 10, `ZSTD_WINDOWLOG_ABSOLUTEMIN`, keeps that
   optional parameter in range (libzstd, `compression.zstd` and `node:zlib` refuse 9; measured, the
   range is 10 to 31), and keeps its verdict equal to the check's, because libzstd's streaming path
   raises a window under 1,024 to 1,024 before it compares. A 32-bit or wasm32 SDK sets
   `caps.memBudget` ≤ 2^30, so the planner never chooses a delta that needs 31. `--patch-from`
   frames are single-segment, so an honestly published frame's window is its content size, never
   more than `memBytes`.

   **`frameWindow(bytes)`** reads RFC 8878 §3.1.1's frame header from the bytes alone, the same way
   in every SDK (Python's `compression.zstd` exposes no window, and Node and Godot no header
   parser). It is null unless bytes 0–3 are `28 B5 2F FD`, bit 3 (`0x08`, reserved) of the
   descriptor at byte 4 is clear, and the bytes hold the whole header: those 5 bytes, one
   Window_Descriptor byte unless Single_Segment_flag (`0x20`) is set, 0, 1, 2 or 4 Dictionary_ID
   bytes by the descriptor's low two bits, and 0 (1 when single-segment), 2, 4 or 8
   Frame_Content_Size bytes by its top two. With Single_Segment_flag set, the window is
   Frame_Content_Size (little-endian, plus 256 for a 2-byte field); otherwise, from the descriptor
   byte `w`, it is `b + (b >> 3) × (w & 7)` with `b = 2^(10 + (w >> 3))`. A window of 2^32 or more
   counts as 2^32, which no `windowLogMax` reaches, so no SDK needs an integer above 2^53 or one
   wider than 64 bits. `frameWindowCases` (§4.4) pins it.

4. `memBytes` is the most memory one frame needs: a `payload` delta's base size plus the payload
   size; a `files` set's largest entry, base file plus new file (a blob entry counts its size).
   The planner drops a delta whose `memBytes` exceeds the device's budget.
5. CI never publishes a `zstd-patch-from` delta whose base (payload or file) starts with
   `37 A4 30 EC`; it skips that delta and reports it. A device that meets one anyway reports
   `delta-apply-failed` (rule 3), and the host falls back to the next strategy.

### 2.8 The marker and the content stamp

**Marker** (`pkey-marker/1`; no new `typ`: the signature is the pack record's own):

```json
{
  "format": "pkey-marker/1",
  "packId": "diceroll.core3d",
  "version": "1.4.0",
  "release": "eyJhbGciOiJFZERTQSIsInR5cCI6InBrZXktcmVsZWFzZStqd3MiLCJraWQiOiJkaWNlcm9sbC1yZWxlYXNlLTIwMjYifQ.…"
}
```

It sits **beside** a single-file payload `X` as `X.pkey.json` (so `diceroll.core3d.pck` and
`diceroll.core3d.pck.pkey.json`; inside, it would change the hash it pins), and **inside** a tree
payload rooted at `D/` as `D/.pkey/pack.json`, which no files index lists. One marker covers every
variant: the device picks the variant whose `payload` its bytes match. A detached JWS was rejected:
it needs a new `typ` and a `PROTOCOL_VERSION` event for what the record's signature already gives.
CI writes markers for every embedded pack, and platform transports (P5-08) ship them in the
delivered unit.

**Content stamp** (`pkey-content.json`, `pkey-content/1`): the app record's `content`, written by
CI before the export and embedded in every build. The running build reads its pins, `required`
and `delivery` from it, never from the network: a store build lagging behind its channel cannot
learn its own record's hash from the feed (the feed pins the channel head, and an outlet's `live`
carries no hash), and the record cannot be embedded in the build it hashes. The build is the
running code, so an unsigned stamp inside it is as trustworthy as the code. `pkey release publish
--deliverable app --content-stamp <file>` signs that same object as the record's `content` (§6).

```json
{
  "format": "pkey-content/1",
  "contentApi": 4,
  "pins": [
    {
      "pack": "diceroll.core3d",
      "release": { "sha256": "…", "seq": 12, "version": "1.4.0" }
    }
  ],
  "expects": [
    { "pack": "diceroll.core3d", "required": true, "delivery": "essential" }
  ]
}
```

Where it lives: in Godot, `res://pkey_packs/pkey-content.json` beside the embedded payloads and
markers (P4-08); in Node, Python and Swift, a file the host ships and names in the client options
(P4-06, P4-07), which must be among the app's own read-only resources, never a user-writable path
(the threat-model entry, §4.8). A host without a stamp has no packs.

### 2.9 Selecting, planning and applying (V4 §11.4, informative; the corpus pins it)

**Usability.** Three predicates decide what a v1 SDK can use, from the record alone:

- an object ref is _usable_ when its `codec` is `zstd` or `none`;
- the index is _readable_ when `files.format` is `pkey-files/1`, the `files` ref is usable and
  `files.size` ≤ `MAX_FILES_INDEX_BYTES`; for a container, rebuilding also needs a usable `gaps`
  ref, and the index and gaps together are _rebuildable_;
- a variant is _usable_ when its `layout` is `container`, or `tree` with a readable index.

**`selectVariant(variants, {engine, axes})`** (`variantCases`). A variant is eligible when it is
usable, its `requires.engine` is absent or equals the host's `engine` (`godot-<major>.<minor>`,
null outside Godot), and every axis it declares has a host preference list (`axes.texture`,
`axes.locale`, …, in preference order) that contains its value, compared by bytes. Among eligible
variants the lowest tuple of preference indexes over the axis names in byte order wins; the
record's single axis set makes the order total. None eligible → `pack-no-variant`.

**`planTarget(variant, recordSha256, filesIndex | null)`** (`targetCases`) maps a variant onto A7
§4.1's planner input. A variant that is not usable maps to `full: null`, `files: null` and
`deltas: []`. Otherwise:

| Planner member | Value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `release`      | `recordSha256`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `payload`      | the variant's `payload`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `full`         | when the `full` ref is usable and `full.size === payload.size`: `{bytes: full.bytes, requests: 1}` for a container, and `{bytes: full.bytes + files.bytes, requests: 2}` for a tree, which needs its readable index to split. Otherwise null                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `platform`     | null (P5-08 sets it for a platform-bound pack)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `chunks`       | null (P4-10, P4-11)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `files`        | when `filesIndex` is given and the index is readable (a container: rebuildable): `{indexBytes: files.bytes, gapsBytes: gaps?.bytes ?? 0, files: [{sha256, blobBytes: blob.bytes}]}` in index order. Otherwise null                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `deltas`       | in record order: a `payload` delta on a container as `{id: artifact.sha256, method, from, memBytes, artifacts: [artifact]}`; a `files` delta, when `files` above is not null and its `patch` ref is usable, as `{id: patch.sha256, method, from, memBytes, artifacts: [files, gaps (container), patch, data]}`, each as `{sha256, bytes}`. The client fetches the target files index before planning when the layout is `tree`, or when any
release of the pack is installed (its index is kept at install); a first install of a container
plans with a null index and takes `full`. The planner counts the index in every candidate that
needs it, fetched or not, so every SDK computes the same costs.

**`plan(input)`** is A7 §4.2 exactly, `REQUEST_WEIGHT` = `PLAN_REQUEST_WEIGHT` = 16,384, with one
generalisation: `full` is `{bytes, requests?}` and costs `requests ?? 1` requests. A7's 23 rows keep
their inputs. v1 capabilities never list `chunk` (P4-06); the planner still costs chunk candidates
from inline records, so P4-11 never touches it. Its results (`plan-transport-unsupported`,
`plan-insufficient-disk`, `plan-no-strategy`) are verdicts it returns, never throws (brief item
12); a pipeline raises them only when it cannot proceed.

**Appliers** (A7 §3.4; the first failure is the verdict; counters are part of it):

- `applyFull`: before decoding, the `full` ref is usable, `full.size === payload.size`, and the
  stored SHA-256 and length equal the ref → else `full-corrupt`; decode to exactly `full.size` →
  `full-corrupt`; a container's SHA-256 equals `payload.sha256` → `full-corrupt`. A tree first
  validates its index (§2.7's codes), then splits the bytes by entry sizes in index order and
  checks every file's SHA-256 → `full-corrupt`.
- `applyDelta` (`payload` scope): the artifact ref → `delta-artifact-mismatch`; the base's SHA-256
  equals `from` → `delta-base-mismatch`; §2.7 rule 3's window check, then the raw-prefix decode, its
  length and its SHA-256 against `payload` → `delta-apply-failed`.
- `applyFile`, for the `file` strategy and for a `files`-scope set: the target index (§2.7), the
  gaps ref of a container (a failure is `files-layout-mismatch`, as A7's `file-gaps-short`), the
  descriptor and data of a set (`delta-artifact-mismatch`). Then for each target file in index
  order: reuse an installed file with the same SHA-256; else the set's `delta` entry (no installed
  file with its `from` → `delta-base-mismatch {path}`; the window check against the set's
  `memBytes`, then the decode → `delta-apply-failed {path}`); else its `blob` entry, or for the
  `file` strategy the file's own blob by its ref (ref, decode or hash → `file-corrupt {path}`); else
  `file-source-missing {path}`. A container's payload SHA-256 → `payload-hash-mismatch`; a tree
  checks every file, reused ones included (`file-corrupt {path}`), and reports its `treeDigest`.
  Counters: `reusedFiles`, `deltaFiles`, `blobFiles`, and `downloadedBytes`, the bytes of the
  objects fetched for the strategy except the index and gaps (the file blobs, or descriptor plus
  data).

**`packSetId(entries)`** (`packSetIdCases`; brief item 11): the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the UTF-8
lines `<packId> <releaseSha256>\n` sorted by pack-id bytes, where `releaseSha256` is P3-01's
record hash. An invalid pack id, a release that is not 64 lowercase hex, or a pack listed twice
gives null; the empty set hashes the empty string. A device reports the id of its **active set**:
the pack releases activated in the running process (restart packs mounted at this boot, hot packs
active, embedded baselines included). P4-12 stores the same function's value for resolved sets.

### 2.12 The feed and the update decision (brief item 14)

Pinned packs need no feed entry (CONTENT §6.3), so v1 adds no feed field: `packSets`,
`packFloors`, `revocations`, `deltas` and new `selector` keys stay reserved for P4-12, P4-13 and
P4-17, and a v4 or P4 client never asks for one. CONTENT §16's v1 row says the same ("pinned packs
come from the app record, so v1 needs no feed entry").

**`packs` stays reserved.** P3-01 asked this plan to add `packs` to `UPDATE_ACTIONS`, the
`updateAction` enum, `vocabulary.actions` and `UpdateDecision` together with the rows that produce
it. In v1 nothing produces it: the running build's set is fixed by its stamp and only an app
update changes it, which `binary`, `store` and `platform` already decide; first fetches are the
boot's FETCH, background prefetch and `ensure()`. A listed value no row produces would fail P3-01's
coverage self-check (§4.9 there). So P4-13, which brings the feed's pack sets, adds `packs` with
`prestage`, `content-floor` and `revoked-content`, and `update-matrix.json` is unchanged here.

### 4.2 The generator (brief item 19)

- **`tools/gen-content-corpus.ts`** (new), a TypeScript port of A7's `gen/gen.py`, `planref.py`
  and the reference applier in `runners/python/pkey_content.py`, called from
  `tools/sign-corpus.ts`'s `main`, so `pnpm gen corpus` and `--check` cover both corpora. It
  imports nothing it checks (P3-02's rule): its reference `parseFilesIndex`, appliers, planner,
  `selectVariant`, `planTarget`, `packSetId`, path rules and content claims are its own.
- **Blobs are inputs.** zstd output is not stable across libzstd versions, so normal and `--check`
  runs never compress: they read `content/blobs/`, decode with `@polaris-key/zstd-wasm`, hash with
  `node:crypto`, and rebuild `content/cases.json` (its `blobs` table included) and
  `plan-matrix.json`. Only the WASM decoder gives one answer everywhere: Node 22 before 22.15 has
  no `node:zlib` zstd, 22.15–22.18 ignore its dictionary (A7 trap 1), and the local Node (22.13.1
  here) differs from CI's latest 22. `--check` also fails on a missing blob, a blob not in the
  table, and a stray file in `content/` or a `content/` directory in a mirror.
- **`content/blobs/refs.json` is an input too.** Some values the records and plan rows must carry
  are the stored `{sha256, bytes}` of frames the set does not ship, and only a compressor can
  produce them (§4.3). `refs.json` holds exactly those object refs, keyed by the blob name each
  would have, as `{"zstd": "1.5.7", "refs": {<name>: {sha256, bytes, size, codec}}}`. It sits in the
  `blobs` table like any blob, so its hash is checked, the stray-file rule covers it, and
  `.prettierignore` and `.gitattributes` already match it; normal and `--check` runs read it and
  never write it. A case that needs the bytes of a `refs.json` object throws.
- **`--rebuild-content-blobs`** (explicit, never in the gate) rebuilds every blob from the decoded
  payloads (v1 from its full blob, v2 by the `file` strategy) with the zstd CLI, and refuses unless
  `zstd -V` reports exactly 1.5.7. It is the only writer of `refs.json`: it compresses each
  unshipped object by §2.7's rules, records its ref and keeps none of those frames.
- **Seeding** (once, in P4-04): run A6's `prototype/patching/build.sh` and A7's
  `gen/gen.py small`, re-encode the result to §2.7's formats with the rebuild mode, and commit.
  The figures here come from S-04's regeneration of that set; P4-04's exact bytes will differ and
  its PR lists them.
- **The JWS side.** `sign-corpus.ts` signs the pack records, app records and markers of §4.6 with
  P3-02's `djdl-release-test-2026` (and `-2027` for rotation), so no key enters `content/` (A7
  §11.5). P4-21 lands them before the content set exists, pinning a fixed table of object refs in
  `sign-corpus.ts` (each `sha256` the SHA-256 of its blob name, sizes as §4.3's table); claims never
  fetch an object, so every verdict holds. P4-04 then takes the content set's hashes from the
  content generator instead, so the corpora join by hash: the valid records, their twins, the
  markers and the two rewritten P3-02 cases change bytes, never ids or `expect` (whose
  `expectedHash` and `recordSha256` the generator recomputes). The per-integer-path and per-check
  self-checks below are P4-21's; the `refs.json` and join self-checks are P4-04's.
- **Self-checks** (each throws, so `gen corpus` fails):
  - every positive apply case is `ok` and every negative one fails with the code it names; the v2
    payloads rebuilt by `delta-whole-v1-to-v2`, `file-v1-to-v2` and `file-delta-v1-to-v2` equal each
    other byte for byte and match v2's index `payload`; `file-delta-tree` rebuilds v2's

    sizes);
  - **per integer path**, P3-02's `PER_CLAIM` loop extended with §2.5's 16 paths, each with a token,
    a bound and a minimum case and no `null` entry: a token or bound case's non-wire scan is exactly

**P4-02** (worker lane; imports P4-21's `client-core` claims and files functions and
`@polaris-key/zstd-wasm`):

- **The release descriptor** (decision 37): `ReleaseDescriptor` gains `content?` and
  `builds[].embeds?`; `descriptorToRecord` moves both unchanged; `validateReleaseDescriptor` gains
  `invalid_descriptor_content` and `invalid_descriptor_embeds` with their schema properties and

  `descriptorManifestOf` and `planDescriptorIngest` (`readPackDeliverableIds`) fill (§3);
  `test/descriptorRecord.test.ts` gains a descriptor with both members whose record carries them
  byte for byte, and one without either whose record is unchanged from today's. Ingest needs no new
  comparison: P3-03's `descriptor-mismatch` covers them.

- **Manifest sync** writes pack rows to `release_deliverables` (`kind` `pack`, `pack_type`,
  `def_json`, `def_source`, the operator guard), at most 64 (§3).
- **The delivery gate** (decision 35). P4-02 adds `entitlement(deliverable)` to the `Delivery` hook
  (`core/hooks.ts`, implemented in `services/distribution/delivery.ts`). It answers the
  `entitlement` of the deliverable's own `dist_access` row, never the `app` row's, or null when the
  deliverable is ungated. Release's publish routes read it there, as Update reads `accessMode` (rule
  6: the hook stays read-only and keyed by deliverable, and `core/hooks.ts`'s consumer list gains
  P4-02). It reads `dist_access` alone and never `releaseCatalog`, so the two hooks never call each
  other. The hook is null while Distribution is off for the product; `uploads`, the stage round and
  the submit then refuse every pack (`distribution_disabled`), which could not be served anyway.
  `uploads` gains a **preflight**: a request with a non-empty `releases` may omit `objects`, and
  then issues no ticket and answers only `seqs[]` (an `objects` array that is present stays
  non-empty, as P2-02 requires). Each `seqs[]` entry of a pack carries `entitlement`, the gate,
  beside P3-03's `seq` and this plan's `recordSha256`. The access route's literal pattern
  (`services/distribution/admin.ts:361`) becomes an import of `ENTITLEMENT_PATTERN`, so the column
  and the claim accept the same strings.
- **The index bound** (decision 36). A Workers isolate has 128 MB (notes/E5, E7), shared by the
  requests it serves at once. Measured on Node 22.13.1, whose V8 lays objects out as workerd's does:
  parsing a 32 MiB, 108,095-entry `pkey-files/1` index adds 72.4 MiB of heap, beside its 32 MiB of
  decoded bytes and the WASM memory that decoded them, about 140 MiB in all; an 8 MiB, 27,079-entry
  index adds 18.3 MiB, about 37 MiB with its bytes and the decoder's memory. So ingest and the
  hook's `packFiles` hold one index at a time, at most `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES` = 8 MiB, under a
  third of the isolate, so the bound holds even if workerd's figure were twice Node's; the CLI
  publishes no larger one; and `@polaris-key/zstd-wasm`'s workerd entry instantiates per decode, so
  no decode's linear memory outlives it. A Diceroll desktop index (6,733 entries) is about 2.1 MB
  with its blob refs, a quarter of the bound. `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES` and
  `MAX_INGEST_INDEX_BYTES` live in `@polaris-key/manifest` beside `MAX_PACK_DELIVERABLES`, for the
  CLI and the Worker. P4-02 repeats the measurement in its PR, in Node 22 as here, and its
  `test:workerd` case ingests a record whose index is exactly `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES` and
  refuses one that declares a byte more (`pack-index`, before any read).
- **Publishing a pack: stage rounds, then one record submit** (decision 28). The submit verifies
  and promotes inside one request, and each object costs one R2 `head` in the verify pass, then
  `head`, `get`, `put` (a second `head` when the key exists) and a D1 `recordObject` insert in
  `promote` (`services/release/publish.ts:440-520`, `core/blobs.ts:313-420`): at most five R2
  calls and one D1 query. Workers Paid allows 10,000 subrequests per invocation (notes/E5, E7), so
  one request must promote a bounded slice, not a whole pack:
  - `uploads` keeps its caps, at most `MAX_TICKET_OBJECTS` = 256 objects and
    `MAX_UPLOADS_BODY_BYTES` = 64 KiB per ticket (`core/publisher.ts:107`, `publish.ts:77`), and
    gains only the preflight above.
  - **Stage round**, `POST …/publish/stage` `{ticket, deliverable}`, with the same `pkeyci_` token
    and `release:publish` scope as `uploads` and `submit`: `deliverable` must be a declared pack
    (else `unknown_pack_deliverable`). It verifies every ticket object the product does not already
    reference (one `head`: the CLI uploads with `x-amz-checksum-sha256`, so R2 holds the checksum),
    refuses an object whose `gated` flag differs from the pack's gate (`gated` exactly when the gate
    is set; `gated_mismatch`), claims the ticket, promotes each object and writes one `blob_refs`
    row per object, `(product, key, "pack-upload", <packId>)`, in one D1 batch of multi-row inserts.
    It answers `{staged, present}`, with P2-02's refusals for the rest (`object_not_in_ticket`,
    `staged_object_missing`, `staged_object_mismatch`, `ticket_redeemed`, `promote_failed`). A
    failure gives the claim back; objects already promoted keep their refs, because `promote` is
    idempotent. A round of 256 objects costs about 1,550 subrequests (six per object and a few for
    the ticket and the batch) and 260 D1 queries, about a sixth of the limit.
  - **Record submit**, `{ticket?, record, dryRun?}` with no descriptor (the record is the whole
    truth, and every pack object is a blob); the body is at most `MAX_RECORD_JWS_BYTES` + 4 KiB.
    The optional ticket (at most 256 objects) is promoted as a round, so a small pack, or an
    update whose new objects fit, publishes in one request. A dry run runs every check without
    promoting and lists in `unverified` the objects not yet referenced, as P2-02's dry run lists
    unstaged ones.
  - A Diceroll-sized first publish (625 entries in three variants, at most about 1,900 objects) is
    eight rounds and a record. An update uploads only new objects: S-03 §4.6 measured 20 to 124
    changed entries per Diceroll release, so most updates are one round or the record's own ticket.
- **Ingest**, refusing with P3-03's `release_record_rejected` and a `reason`: P3-03's checks (`typ`,
  `kid`, `product-key`, `signature`, `claims` with the extended claims, `scheme` under the pack's
  scheme, `descriptor-mismatch` for an app record, whose `content` and `embeds` it now covers, and
  `seq`), then `pack-unknown` (not a declared pack), `pack-type` (type differs from the
  declaration), `pack-variant` (a variant outside the declared axes and values), `pack-entitlement`
  (the record's `entitlement`, absent read as none, differs from the gate the hook answers at
  submit, or from the `entitlement` the pack's `def_json` asserts), `pack-index` (a variant's
  `files.size` or `files.bytes` is above `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES`, refused before anything is
  read; or an index fails `parseFilesIndex`, read from the blob store and decoded with the WASM
  decoder one at a time, each decoded, parsed, its objects checked and dropped before the next; or
  the record's indexes decode to more than `MAX_INGEST_INDEX_BYTES` = 64 MiB in all, which bounds
  the submit's CPU, not its memory), and `pack-object` (an object named by the record or by a files
  index is not stored with that size under the pack's prefix, `gated/` exactly when the record
  carries an `entitlement`, or the product holds no ref to it, or a variant's `full.size` differs
  from its `payload.size`; a `files` set's added blobs live inside its data object, so ingest never
  reads a patch descriptor). The ref check is P2-01's possession rule: every object was promoted

_Amendment (P4-02 as implemented; no name above changed)._ A pack release's id is
`<packId>@<version>`, never the record's `tag`. Git allows `@` in a tag, so a GitHub release can
carry the same id; the truth-store sync is guarded instead: it skips such a release and reports
it as a conflict, and its writes never touch a non-app row. Its objects' `release_artifacts` ids are
`<build_id>/<role>[-<n>]`. The stage round answers `{ok, deliverable, staged, present}` (target
keys). `pack-unknown` runs before `scheme`, whose scheme is the pack's; a different record for an
existing pack version is P2-04's `release_exists` (409). The pack code is
`services/release/packs/` (`deliverables.ts`, `ingest.ts`, `content.ts`, `catalog.ts`,
`publish.ts`), and the hook's pack readers are `ReleaseCatalog.packDeliverables`, `packRelease`,
`packFiles`, `pins`, `pinnedBy` and `embeds` with the `Catalog*` shapes in `core/hooks.ts`.

**P4-03** (CLI; no Worker change): `--deliverable <packId>` builds, per variant, the stripped
payload, the files index, every file blob, the gaps object, the whole and the packed `files`
deltas, the marker and the record; self-checks with `releaseRecordClaims`, `parseFilesIndex` and a
byte-for-byte rebuild, refusing an index above `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES`; and publishes in stage
rounds of at most 256 objects that are not `present`, then the record submit, retrying a failed
round with a new ticket.

- **The gate comes from the preflight.** The CLI's first `uploads` call is the preflight (§6,
  P4-02): the cached versions and the new one in `releases`, no objects. Its answer gives the new
  `seq`, the cached records' hashes and the pack's gate before any delta is built. The CLI stops,
  before uploading anything, while `.pkey/release` asserts an `entitlement` the gate does not equal
  ("set it under Distribution → Access first"); otherwise it requests every object with `gated` set
  exactly when the gate is, and signs the gate as the record's `entitlement`. `--dry-run` prints the
  gate.
- **Delta bases come from CI's own earlier output, never from the Worker.** CI cannot read stored
  objects: its ticket credentials grant `PutObject` and `HeadObject` only ("CI never reads",
  `core/publisher.ts:111-121`), and the byte routes want a licensed device under any mode but
  `public` (`core/entitledAccess.ts:274-316`) and refuse `gated/` keys outright
  (`services/distribution/bytes.ts:428-445`). So `--out <dir>` writes each publish's record and
  payloads to `<dir>/<packId>/<version>/` (`record.jws`, and one `<variant key or "default">/`
  directory holding the payload file or tree); the workflow keeps `<dir>` between runs (the
  Action documents an `actions/cache` step keyed by pack id); and `--bases <dir>` reads it back.
  The CLI lists the cached versions in the preflight's `releases`, takes the
  `patch.deltaBases` highest-`seq` ones the answer reports as stored, and checks each cached
  record's SHA-256 against the answer's `seqs[].recordSha256` and each cached payload against
  that record's variant `payload` (a tree by its `treeDigest`). A base that is missing or fails a
  check gets no delta and a warning, never a failure: deltas are optional, and A6's rule ("never
  re-export") still holds, because a base is the exact bytes published, proven by hash.
- `pkey release content-stamp`, with `--embedded <dir>`, any number of
  `--pin <packId>@<version>` and `--out pkey-content.json`, writes the stamp from the markers in
  `<dir>`, the pins and `.pkey/release`; `--deliverable app --content-stamp <file>` signs it as the
  record's `content`, with each build's `embeds` from the artifact map: both go into the descriptor,
  never into the record directly, and `descriptorToRecord` moves them (decision 37), so the record
  the CLI signs is the one ingest rebuilds. `--dry-run` prints all of it and runs no round.

**P4-05**: serves every pack object through the blob route (`Range`, `If-Range`, `ETag` = SHA-256,
`Repr-Digest`, no `Content-Encoding`), the gated prefix for an entitled pack, and availability
(`pkey-cdn` and `web` live when every object is stored; `embedded` per build from `embeds`). A blob
reaches its pack through its refs: a `pack-object` ref names the release, a `pack-upload` ref the
pack. The pack's `dist_access` row governs (decision 35): an object under `gated/` is authorised per
request by device token and a licence that holds the **current** gate of a pack holding a ref to it,
never the manifest's assertion and never a record's `entitlement`, which is a publish-time snapshot,
and is served `private, no-store`; when none of those packs is gated any more, it is served under
their strictest mode, still `private, no-store`. An object never moves between prefixes (P4-14's
rule too), so an object under `blobs/` stays on the public path under its pack's mode even after the
pack is gated: its earlier responses were publicly cacheable and cannot be recalled, and a gate
protects the bytes published after it is set. `entitledBlobRefusal`
(`services/distribution/bytes.ts:575`), which today answers not-found for any hash no release
artifact carries, gains that pack branch, and the `gated/` refusal at `bytes.ts:428-445` gives way
to it. It retires `routedDeliverables`'s pack filter (§3) and updates the R10 test. THREAT-MODEL's
"Byte delivery, delivery access and rollouts (P2b-04)" section gains the gated pack rule: no byte
under `gated/` is served without the current gate's flag while the pack is gated, the public path
never reads `gated/`, a ref of another product never counts, and a gate set after a release leaves
that release's `blobs/` objects where they were.

## 7. Rollout

1. **P3-02 has merged** (v4, its corpus, the two-key release test set; `4ed7935f`).
2. **P4-21 merges.** Contract amendment, the pack types and constants, `client-core`'s claims and
   files functions, `packRecordCases` and `markerCases`, `@polaris-key/zstd-wasm`, the rebundled
   Action. No Worker deploy is needed. A Worker built after it applies the stricter claims at
   ingest, which changes nothing in practice: pack submits arrive only with P4-02, and app records
   carry `content` only from P4-03 (a descriptor carrying `content` before P4-02 fails
   `descriptor-mismatch`, because today's `descriptorToRecord` drops it, so nothing half-lands).
   **P4-04 merges** after it, in the same lane: the content corpus, `plan-matrix.json`, stage matrix
   version 3 in the ports; SDK releases carry version 3, whose new events no host sends until P4-06
   to P4-08.
3. **P4-02 deploys** (after P3-03 and P4-21): the descriptor's `content` and `embeds`, migration,
   pack manifest rules (with the 64-pack bound), the delivery gate and the uploads preflight, the
   stage route, pack and `content` ingest under the 8 MiB index bound, `release.packs`, the report
   key. Existing `dist_access` rows keep their meaning: no pack row exists yet, and the `app` row's
   `entitlement` never gates a pack. No product in `products/` declares a pack. A product that
   declared one under the old warning now has it validated, and its sync is refused if it breaks a
   rule, more than 64 packs included; the deploy checklist runs `pkey validate` over known products
   first. Packs are still not routed.
4. **P4-03** publishes packs and stamps, refusing without `release.packs`; **P4-05** serves them and
   routes packs to outlets, which the bound makes safe. An operator gates a paid pack under
   Distribution → Access before its first publish; a manifest that asserts the flag makes CI refuse
   until then.
5. **SDKs:** P4-06, then P4-07 and P4-08; then D-04 (Diceroll).

**Old clients:** v3 untouched; v4 pre-pack SDKs act on app records as before and ignore `content`
(§2.1). **Old Workers:** a P4 SDK against a Worker without pack records finds no pinned record and

publishes a pack, and its app publishes are unchanged. **Feature flags:** none; packs exist where a
product declares them. **Corpus lane:** P4-21 now that P3-02 has merged, then P4-04; P4-10 and
P4-13 after P4-04, each rebasing and re-running `pnpm gen corpus`.

### 8.2 Risks

1. **Names move.** They follow `main` after P3-02's merge; a later rename is followed by the
   implementing package and recorded in a small amendment to this plan.
2. **zstd versions.** Blobs are inputs; only the rebuild mode compresses, with zstd 1.5.7, and it
   refuses any other version. A rebuild changes hashes, so it is a deliberate PR of its own.
3. **The WASM decoder** is a committed binary in the generator, the Worker, React and the Node
   fallback. Its recorded SHA-256 and reproducible `build.sh` guard the supply chain; other SDKs
   decode with other libraries, so a decoder bug cannot make the whole corpus agree with itself.
4. **The content set's provenance.** It is seeded once from A6's Godot exports; a regeneration
   gives other bytes (S-04's differs from A7's). After seeding, the committed blobs are the inputs.
5. **A host that ships no content stamp has no packs.** P4-06 to P4-08 make the stamp option and
   `res://pkey_packs/` the documented setup, and their docs pages say so.
6. **Consent confirms a boot.** A crash after a consented download rolls back one launch later
   (§2.10). P3-10 keeps P3-01's confirmation rule.
7. **Godot's GDDL route is engine-internal**: P4-08 advertises `zstd-patch-from` only on pinned
   engine versions, and `file` and `full` need nothing internal.
8. **Forward compatibility is a promise.** A later package that must refuse what §2.2 lets v1 accept
   changes a verdict and is a plan-mode event.
9. **The Worker parses CI-supplied indexes.** Bounded by `MAX_PUBLISHED_INDEX_BYTES` (8 MiB) per
   index, one at a time, against a 128 MB isolate, and by `MAX_INGEST_INDEX_BYTES` per record, and
   run through the same `parseFilesIndex` SDKs run; refs follow P2-01's possession rule. A pack
   whose index outgrows 8 MiB (about 27,000 entries) cannot publish until a Worker streams the parse
   and raises its bound, which needs no client change because clients accept 32 MiB.
10. **Reading the checkout from the Swift and Godot runners** is a new pattern; if the lead prefers
    full mirrors, adding `content/` to the reconcile loop is mechanical and costs ≈ 5.4 MB.
11. **Subrequest and D1 limits move.** The stage-round size assumes Workers Paid's 10,000
    subrequests per invocation (notes/E5, E7); the R10 audit findings still quotes the older 1,000. A round is
    capped by the existing `MAX_TICKET_OBJECTS`, so P4-02 can lower the stage cap alone if workerd
    refuses; the `json_each` check assumes a 750 KB bound value and falls back to 2,000 pairs.
12. **CI loses its cache.** A missing base means bigger downloads for that release, never a failed
    publish; the Action's cache step and `--dry-run` report which bases were used.
13. **The 80-check registry is long.** It is the price of five SDKs agreeing check by check; the
    one-variant twins keep the corpus growth near 500 KB.
14. **A gate changes after a release.** Objects never move between prefixes, so a pack gated later
    keeps its earlier releases' `blobs/` objects on the public path under its `dist_access` mode (so
    app releases that pin them keep working), and a pack un-gated later has its earlier `gated/`
    objects served under its mode. A record's `entitlement`, the device's hint, follows the gate at
    the next publish; until then a device may skip a pack it could fetch or try one it cannot (a
    403, which the pipeline reports as a failed fetch). P4-09 shows a gate that differs from the
    latest release's `entitlement`.
15. **The `refs.json` entries are trusted inputs.** Their sizes are checked against the shipped
    indexes, but their hashes only by `refs.json`'s own entry in the `blobs` table; a corrupt entry
    could only make a record pin bytes nobody holds, which no case fetches. The rebuild mode
    rewrites them with every other blob.
