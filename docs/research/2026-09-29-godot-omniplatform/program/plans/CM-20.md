## 0. Binding decisions (from the decision record, not reopened)

- **Licence-held only (C-07).** A purchase lands on a licence: the one whose binding it carries
  (D5, D6). There is no account-held or store-held grant (P0-20 deletes LX-08's dormant holder
  code), and no account-held binding.
- **An offer has no entitlements of its own (C-49).** It targets `tier:<id>` or `addon:<id>`, the
  target vocabulary of `license.access`, codes and comps (C-12). Every grant references an add-on
  (LX-35).
- **Subscriptions are Core's (C-16).** One `subscriptions` table owned by Licensing serves every
  source (LX-41). Commerce keeps provider references only, and `dist_subscriptions` is never
  created.
- **The mount (C-17, reversed by the owner on 2026-10-07).** The decision record's working copy now
  makes `commerce` a service that requires Licensing (CM-29, riding W-LX). Its device routes move
  to `/<p>/commerce/*` and its code to `services/commerce/`. This plan's tables, functions, members
  and transcripts do not depend on the mount. The paths here are today's
  (`/<p>/distribution/commerce/*`, `services/distribution/commerce/index.ts:117-126`). Q2 orders
  LX-11 and CM-29.
- **Store identity is derived (C-40).** A-28 reads it from the app assignment. CM-21 keeps the
  notifications setup and the test-purchase switch.
- **Refunds revoke at once (C-11).** A full refund or a chargeback revokes immediately. A partial
  refund never revokes. `refundGraceHours` goes.
- **Money is operator-only** (S-22 D7, `plans/LX-01.md` §3.1). Offers, SKUs and the restore policy
  are never manifest data. Tiers and add-ons are (LX-34, LX-35).

## 1. Summary

- **One offer, many SKUs.** An offer says what is sold, once: a target, one-time or subscription
  billing, and a quantity. A SKU is that offer's product id on one storefront, and `@app` is the
  paid app itself. Today each store mapping redefines its own grant
  (`migrations/0052_commerce.sql:29-39`, `0105_j`, `0105_k`, `0105_l:12-19`).
- **One placement rule and one revocation function.** A verified purchase lands on the licence
  whose binding it carries, as today: as an add-on grant, or as a licence minted on a tier. A
  restore from another holder follows one per-store value, `transfer` or `block`. A refund,
  chargeback or loss of ownership goes through `revokePurchase`.
- **Two ledgers.** Purchases are a read model over `dist_purchases`. Subscriptions are LX-41's Core
  table, to which LX-23 adds Apple auto-renewables and Play subscriptions. Both work with no
  storefront set up.
- **The storefront activates by itself.** It is Ready once License is on, the app is assigned and
  a verification credential resolves for it. No settings write is needed, and nothing is typed
  twice.
- **One wire event.** The binding gains `offers[]`, the claim gains `kind: "app"`, and the claim
  answer gains `offer` and `transferred`. LX-18 writes the contract, LX-11 and CM-25 record the
  transcripts, and LX-20 moves all six SDKs at once.

## 2. The model

### 2.1 Offer and SKU (LX-11)

- **D1. An offer is defined once:** `{id, label, target, billing, quantity, state}`.
  - `billing` is `one_time` or `subscription`.
  - `quantity` multiplies what the target gives: units of a consumable or of a quantity add-on.
    It is 1 otherwise. Until LX-42 adds `grants.quantity`, the offers API refuses a quantity above
    1 (§7).
  - `state` is `on_sale` or `retired`. A retired offer is not listed, but a purchase of one of its
    SKUs still grants, because the customer has paid.
  - **Why.** "What someone gets" is defined once, by the tier or the add-on, and "what is sold" is
    defined once, by the offer. Today every store row redefines a grant (commerce audit P2).
- **D2. SKUs live in a new table, `dist_offer_skus`, not in a column on `dist_store_products`.**
  This departs from the storage line in `integration.md` §1.2, for three reasons:
  1. **Rollback.** A Worker rolled back past LX-11's switch reads `dist_store_products` and grants
     its `flag` whatever the row means (`state.ts:505-559`). It never sees a row in a table it
     does not know, and it treats that purchase as unmapped, which grants nothing and records
     nothing (`state.ts:11-12`).
  2. **No rebuild for new stores.** `dist_store_products.store` is a CHECK
     (`0052_commerce.sql:31`), so a Microsoft Store or itch.io SKU (CM-27) would need a table
     rebuild. The new table's store vocabulary is a trigger, following the `0105_a:72-80`
     precedent.
  3. **No dormant columns.** Four columns the offer replaces leave with the old table at LX-16b:
     `flag`, `deliverable_id`, `grants_kind` and `base_tier_id`. They would otherwise stay
     dormant, because `grants_kind` carries a CHECK that cannot be dropped without a rebuild.
- **D3. The paid app is a SKU too.** On an `app-store`, `play` or `steam` row,
  `store_product_id = '@app'` names the app purchase itself (CM-25).
  - No store's product id can start with `@` (`state.ts:72-76`), so the sentinel cannot collide.
  - Its offer must target a tier.
  - The app's store identity comes from the pin (§2.5), never from a typed id.
- **D4. The deliverable is not asked for.** Gating already says which deliverables an entitlement
  unlocks. The legacy mapping write asks for one (`admin.ts:218-237`), but it is only echoed back
  to the claimant (`state.ts:574`). The offers API does not ask.
  - While `dist_store_products` exists, `products[]` and the claim answer echo the mapping's stored
    `deliverable_id`, so the projection stays byte-identical even for a mapping that is not `app`.
  - A SKU with no legacy row answers `"app"`.
  - The offers job lists every mapping whose deliverable is not `app`. LX-16 switches those to
    `"app"` only after the lead clears that list.

```sql
-- 00XX_commerce_offers.sql  (TABLE_OWNERS: distribution, both tables; written only by
-- services/distribution/commerce/**). Expand-only; a Worker deployed before it never names them.
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS dist_offers (
  product     TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES products(slug),
  id          TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- off_<26 base32>; the backfill writes off_<addon id>
  label       TEXT NOT NULL,
  target      TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- tier:<id> | addon:<id>            (trigger)
  billing     TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'one_time', -- one_time | subscription        (trigger)
  quantity    INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1 CHECK (quantity >= 1),
  state       TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'on_sale',  -- on_sale | retired             (trigger)
  created_at  INTEGER NOT NULL, modified_at INTEGER NOT NULL, modified_by TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (product, id));
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS dist_offer_skus (
  product          TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES products(slug),
  store            TEXT NOT NULL,             -- app-store | play | steam           (trigger)
  store_product_id TEXT NOT NULL,             -- the store's id; '@app' = the app itself (D3)
  offer_id         TEXT NOT NULL,
  modified_at      INTEGER NOT NULL, modified_by TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (product, store, store_product_id));
CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS idx_dist_offer_skus_offer ON dist_offer_skus(product, offer_id);
-- trg_dist_offers_{target,billing,state}_{ins,upd}: target GLOB 'tier:?*' OR 'addon:?*'
-- trg_dist_offer_skus_store_{ins,upd}
```

Two single-statement files (R11-04) add columns to `dist_purchases`:

- `00XX_dist_purchases_offer_id.sql`: `ALTER TABLE dist_purchases ADD COLUMN offer_id TEXT;`. It
  records the offer at the time of the purchase, so the ledger stays true after a SKU is
  re-pointed.
- `00XX_dist_purchases_moved_at.sql`: `ALTER TABLE dist_purchases ADD COLUMN moved_at INTEGER;`.
  It records the time of the purchase's last transfer (D7).

### 2.2 From a purchase to a licence (LX-11; CM-24 and CM-25 call it)

- **D5. Bindings stay per licence.**
  - `GET …/commerce/binding` keeps answering the binding of the device's licence (`bindingFor`,
    `state.ts:253-280`). The claim keeps checking a Steam ticket's identity against that same
    binding (`index.ts:415`, `steam.ts:284-296`). Both routes use one binding, and every binding
    ever issued stays valid.
  - `holderOfBinding()` replaces `licenseOfBinding()` (`state.ts:288-306`). It reads
    `dist_purchase_binding_aliases`, then `dist_purchase_bindings`. That is the one alias path A-28
    asked for, and it needs no data migration.
  - LX-08's `dist_holder_bindings` and `dist_binding_aliases` (`0105_l:25-47`) were never written.
    They stay unread, and LX-16b drops them.
  - **Why not an account binding**, LX-08's design:
    1. **Notifications.** A notification can arrive before the device's claim (Apple
       `ONE_TIME_CHARGE`, a Play RTDN). With an account binding it has no device to place by.
       With the licence's binding it lands on the buyer's own licence, as today
       (`state.ts:508-526`).
    2. **Steam.** A Steam ticket carries the binding as its identity. A binding that changed when
       a sign-in moved the licence into an account would fail the claims of every client that
       cached the old one. Godot caches it for the session (`commerce.gd:110,328`).
    3. **Nothing to gain.** Under C-07 the purchase is licence-held anyway, so an account binding
       adds nothing that D6's same-account rule does not.

- **D6. Placement.** `placePurchase()` in `commerce/state.ts` replaces "first licence wins"
  (`state.ts:17-21`). Below, **B** is the licence a purchase's binding names, **L** the licence a
  recorded purchase sits on, and **C** the claiming device's licence. A notification, the Play
  voided poll and the Steam re-check have no C.
  1. **A new purchase** lands on B, as today.
     - If B is unusable (disabled, ended or expired) and an account holds it, the purchase lands on
       C when that account holds C. Otherwise it lands on the account's first usable licence by
       `rankLicenses()` (LX-10), or on B when there is none.
     - A binding that names no licence is refused `unbound`, as today. A notification records that
       purchase as `unresolved`.
     - A claim from a device whose C is not that licence follows rule 2 or 3, as if the purchase
       were already recorded there. Where that rule refuses, nothing is recorded, as today. The
       purchase is recorded when B's device claims it, or when its notification arrives.
  2. **A recorded add-on purchase** sits on L and is claimed from C.
     - If C is L, the claim changes nothing (`changed: false`).
     - If one account holds both C and L, the grant stays on L while L is usable, and the claim is
       refused as today (`binding_mismatch` for a new purchase, `bound_elsewhere` for a recorded
       one). When L is unusable, the grant moves to C. That move
       stays inside one holder. It is audited and writes `entitlement_events`, but it sends no
       notice, is not held to the interval, leaves `moved_at` alone and answers no `transferred`.
     - Otherwise the store's restore policy decides (D7). Under `block` the claim is refused as
       today: `binding_mismatch` for a new purchase, `bound_elsewhere` for a recorded one. Under
       `transfer` the grant moves to C.
  3. **A recorded tier or app purchase** is the licence M it minted, and M never moves. A device on
     another licence joins M the way an entered key would: seat-checked, and for an M in an
     account, only when the device is signed in to that account (I-09's `license_owned`). It may
     join in two cases:
     - its licence is B: the buyer's own device, after a notification minted M first;
     - under `transfer`, it presents the store-verified purchase: a restore on a second device.

     Otherwise, and always under `block`, it is refused as in rule 2.

  4. **What it writes.**
     - **`addon:`** calls LX-35's `grantAddOn(licence, addon, source)` with the purchase as its
       reference:
       - `source` is the store, and `external_ref_hash` the purchase key hash;
       - the grant id stays `grt_s_<store>_<hash>` (`core/grants.ts:70`), so every existing grant
         stays the same row;
       - LX-35 needs this reference for its own acceptance, because store grants must call
         `grantAddOn`;
       - quantity waits for LX-42 (§7). Until then LX-11 writes none, and a Play purchase whose
         `quantity` is above 1 keeps that number in `detail_json` and grants once.
     - **`tier:` and `@app`** call `issueLicense(product, holder, via, target, device)` with
       `via = "purchase"`, which LX-11 adds to `core/licensing/issue.ts`:
       - the holder is B's account, or none when B has none;
       - the device is the claiming device, and absent for a notification;
       - the licence's `source` is the store and its `external_ref_hash` the purchase key hash, so
         `idx_licenses_ext_ref` makes the mint idempotent (`0105_m:34-36`).
     - **Origin.** A minted licence keeps `origin = 'admin'`, the column's default
       (`0011_auto_issue.sql:20`). What marks it as a purchase is `licenses.source` (`0105_e`),
       which LX-08 added for this. Three facts support this:
       - `trg_licenses_origin_{ins,upd}` accept only `admin`, `oidc` and `enroll`
         (`0015_data_integrity.sql:78-86`);
       - the console types `origin` as those three (`admin/src/api.ts:3473`);
       - LX-36's `accessFor()` re-runs touch only `origin = 'oidc'`, so a sign-in never re-tiers a
         purchase licence.

       Provenance reads `source` first (§6.3).

     - **Superseding.** When the holder is an account whose previous licence on that device has a
       lower tier rank, that licence gets `superseded_by`. The account's other devices then follow
       at their next refresh (LX-10, LX-12).
     - Both functions live in `core/licensing/issue.ts` (P0-20, LX-35). Rule 6 holds: Distribution
       imports Core only.

- **D7. The restore policy is one Advanced value per store:** `restore: "transfer" | "block"`,
  inside `distribution.commerce` (`settings.ts:9-11`), where `plans/LX-01.md` §8 Q5 put it. The
  default is `transfer` (Q1, decided).
  - **One batch.** A transfer moves the add-on grant, `dist_purchases.license_id`, `moved_at` and
    the old tables' rows together.
  - **Recorded.** It is audited, and it writes `entitlement_events` for both licences.
  - **Notice.** The previous licence's account is told by email, through P0-21's `core/notify`,
    that the purchase moved, but never to whom. A floating licence has no one to tell.
  - **Interval.** A transfer is refused `bound_elsewhere` within 24 hours of the purchase's
    `moved_at`. `TRANSFER_MIN_INTERVAL_SECONDS` is a code constant (a security bound), not a
    setting. Moves inside one account neither check nor set it (D6.2).
  - **No `transferCooldownDays`.** That setting is never created.
  - **Existing products keep today's behaviour.**
    - A P0-49 job writes `block` for them (§6.2), and N+1 ships only when every store block of a
      product with recorded purchases carries `restore`.
    - Release N's parser keeps the member. Today `parseAppStore`, `parsePlay` and `parseSteam` drop
      members they do not know (`settings.ts:75-175`).
    - A Worker older than N still drops it when an operator saves; §8 covers that case.
- **D8. Only an app claim accepts a licence-less device.** A `kind: "app"` claim (CM-25) is the
  one exception. Every other claim, and the binding, keep today's 403 `not_entitled`/`no_license`.

### 2.3 One revocation path and the purchase ledger (LX-11, CM-22)

**D9. `revokePurchase(ctx, ref, cause)`** lives in `commerce/revoke.ts` and is built by LX-11, not
CM-22.

- **Why LX-11.** LX-11 rewrites `recordPurchase` and `revokeRecordedPurchase` anyway
  (`state.ts:486-651`), and writing the revocation twice would be a copy.
- **Who calls it.** The Apple hook (`index.ts:635-643`), the Play hook (`:726-736`), the claim
  (`:469-479`), the Play voided poll and the Steam re-check (`recheck.ts:1-15`). Later, CM-28 (a
  refund reduces a consumable balance, never below zero) and Polaris Key checkout's refunds and
  disputes (CM-05, which absorbs CM-06).
- **Subscriptions.** LX-23 adds the branch that ends a store purchase's Core subscription row.
  LX-11 has no subscription to end.
- **What else it emits.** An audit row, entitlement events, the "access ended" email through
  `core/notify` (LX-11) and the developer webhook `purchase.refunded` (CM-22).

| Cause and source                                                      | Add-on grant | Licence the purchase minted               | `dist_purchases`          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Full refund: Apple `REFUND`, a Play voided purchase                   | `refunded`   | disabled, `ended_reason = refunded`       | `revoked`, `detail.cause` |
| Chargeback: a Play voided chargeback (later, a lost Stripe dispute)   | `refunded`   | disabled, `chargeback`                    | `revoked`                 |
| Revocation or loss: Apple `REVOKE`, Steam no longer reports ownership | `revoked`    | disabled, `revoked`                       | `revoked`                 |
| Partial refund, where the store reports one [I]                       | unchanged    | unchanged                                 | `detail.partialRefund`    |
| Reversal: Apple `REFUND_REVERSED`, ownership regained                 | `active`     | re-enabled when this purchase disabled it | `active`                  |

**D10. The purchase ledger is a read model** (CM-22). `listPurchases()` reads `dist_purchases`
and joins it to:

- the offer, through `dist_purchases.offer_id`;
- the grant or the minted licence;
- the holder;
- the Core subscription with the same `(source, external_ref_hash)`.

`dist_orders` joins the same model when checkout ships (CM-05). There is no new ledger table,
because `dist_purchases` already keys every store transaction by its hash (`0052:50-62`).

### 2.4 Store subscriptions on Core subscriptions (LX-23, under a short plan of its own)

- **D11. A SKU whose offer bills `subscription`** is an Apple auto-renewable subscription or a
  Play subscription. It is placed as in D6.
- **One Core row per purchase.** LX-23 also writes one `subscriptions` row for each, only through
  LX-41's `core/licensing/subscriptions.ts` (`plans/LX-41.md` §2.4, §6.2):
  - `source` is the store;
  - `external_ref_hash` is the purchase's `purchase_key_hash` (Apple's `originalTransactionId`,
    Play's purchase token, hashed as `sha256Hex("<store>:" + key)`, the convention LX-41 also
    uses), so the ledger joins on it;
  - `license_id` is set for a tier target, `grant_id` for an add-on target.
- **Status mapping** onto LX-41's vocabulary and functions:

  | Store fact                                                                                    | LX-41 call and status                                   |
  | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
  | A free trial or introductory offer (Apple `offerType`, Play's free-trial phase)               | `start` → `trialing`                                    |
  | Bought, or renewed                                                                            | `start` or `renew` → `active`                           |
  | Billing retry or store grace (Apple's billing retry or grace period, Play's grace or on hold) | `pastDue(storeGraceEnd)` → `past_due`                   |
  | Auto-renew turned off; turned back on before the end                                          | `cancel` → `canceled`; `resume` → `active`              |
  | Expired, refunded or revoked                                                                  | `expire` → `expired` (refunds through `revokePurchase`) |

- **Grace and renewals** are LX-41's rules, not new ones: a renewal sets the period end plus
  LX-41's 24-hour constant, and `past_due` keeps the target until the latest of that, the
  `dunningGraceDays` setting and the store's own grace end.
- **Deletion.** A live store subscription blocks deleting its licence (`plans/LX-41.md` §6.2 hands
  this blocker to LX-23).
- **Verification.**
  - Apple: `checkTransaction` accepts `Auto-Renewable Subscription` for subscription SKUs only
    (`apple.ts:239` refuses everything but `Non-Consumable` today).
  - Play: subscription SKUs are read with `purchases.subscriptionsv2.get`.
  - Notifications acted on: Apple's `SUBSCRIBED`, `DID_RENEW`, `DID_FAIL_TO_RENEW`,
    `GRACE_PERIOD_EXPIRED`, `EXPIRED`, `DID_CHANGE_RENEWAL_STATUS` and `DID_CHANGE_RENEWAL_PREF`
    join `APPLE_ACTED_TYPES` (`apple.ts:63-68`), and so does Play's `subscriptionNotification`.
- **What LX-23's own plan settles.** LX-23 keeps plan mode, because three store facts decide
  correctness and need their own fixtures:
  1. **Play plan changes.** An upgrade or downgrade issues a new purchase token that names the old
     one in `linkedPurchaseToken`. The new token hashes differently, so it must resolve to the
     chain's existing licence or grant and its Core row. It must never mint a second licence. The
     bound: one licence or grant, and one live Core row, per subscription chain. A change inside an
     Apple subscription group keeps `originalTransactionId` and re-targets the row to the new SKU's
     offer.
  2. **Apple resubscribe after expiry.** It keeps `originalTransactionId`, so it meets the same
     `idx_subscriptions_ref` row. LX-41 allows `expired → active` on the same ref only for
     `external` (`plans/LX-41.md` §2.4). LX-23 widens that transition to `app-store` and `play`,
     inside `core/licensing/subscriptions.ts`.
  3. **The period.** LX-41's `period` is NOT NULL. LX-23 takes it from the store's catalog: App
     Store Connect's `subscriptionPeriod`, mapped to ISO 8601, and Play's base-plan
     `billingPeriodDuration`, which already is. It never takes it from an offer field (§11).
- **No wire member.** The licence document shows renewal through LX-18's `term`. There is no
  transcript, because Apple and Play need signed fixtures, which only the Worker suite holds.
- **Subscriptions without Commerce** need none of this. LX-41's `manual` source (the console's
  Renew…) and its `external` source (a developer's own billing through
  `POST /{product}/license/subscriptions` with a `pkeyci_` token) work with no storefront at all
  (`plans/LX-41.md` §2.4; `integration.md` §1.4).

### 2.5 The storefront: derived identity, four states, activation by itself (A-28, CM-21, A-33)

- **D12. The identity is the app assignment.** A-28 reads the bundle id and Apple ID, the package
  name and the Steam app id from a pin, in today's credential order (`apple.ts:261-279`):
  1. the pin of the product's own active credential;
  2. otherwise, the product's pin on the platform credential
     (`core/platformCredentials.ts:100-131`).

  The typed identity fields of `distribution.commerce` (`settings.ts:30-51`) become optional.
  - **A stored field that differs from the pin still wins.** A-28 raises an identity-mismatch
    attention item until the operator confirms "Use the assigned app", which deletes the typed

## 3. Contract (LX-18 writes it; LX-11 and CM-25 emit it)

### 3.1 `GET /<p>/distribution/commerce/binding`

```jsonc
{ "bindingId": "<uuid>",                    // the device's licence's binding, unchanged (D5)
  "products": [ … ],                        // the frozen legacy projection; shape unchanged
  "offers": [                               // absent from an older Worker: read as []
    { "id": "off_dice-skins", "label": "Dice skins",
      "target": "addon:dice-skins",         // tier:<id> | addon:<id>
      "billing": "one_time",                // one_time | subscription
      "quantity": 1,
      "skus": [ { "store": "app-store", "productId": "gg.acme.djdl.skins" },
                { "store": "steam",     "productId": "1234560" } ] } ] }
```

- **What is listed.** Every on-sale offer with at least one SKU on a Ready or Live store, ordered
  by id. SKUs are ordered by store, then product id. Before CM-21, Ready is the claim's own test
  (§2.5).
- **What is never listed.**
  - App SKUs.
  - Prices: store prices are set in the store, and only CM-01's `polaris-key` SKU would ever carry
    one.
  - Nothing is filtered by outlet, because Godot's App Store 3.1.3(b) rule needs every store's
    SKUs (`sdks/godot/addons/polaris_key/services/commerce.gd:538-547`).
- **`products[]` stays, frozen.**
  - It is projected from add-on offers' SKUs, one row per add-on key, with the stored
    `deliverable_id` while `dist_store_products` exists (D4).
  - It keeps today's block-only store filter (`index.ts:267-272`).
  - For every backfilled mapping it is byte-identical to today's rows, which §6.2's report proves,
    so an old SDK's `hidden_here` keeps working.

### 3.2 `POST /<p>/distribution/commerce/claim`

- **Request.** One new optional member, `kind`:
  - `"product"`, the default, with today's bodies;
  - `"app"` (CM-25), with one of:
    - `{store: "app-store", kind: "app", signedTransaction: <AppTransaction JWS>}`;
    - `{store: "play", kind: "app"}`, answered from the device's latest Play Integrity verdict
      (`core/playIntegrity.ts:20`). Without one, the answer is today's 403
      `attestation_required`, which every SDK already retries;
    - `{store: "steam", kind: "app", ticket}`.

  does not change these names. An SDK sends `kind` only for `"app"`, so a product claim's body
  stays byte-identical to the one `commerce-claim.json` records.

- **Response.** Two new optional members:
  - `offer`, the offer id;
  - `transferred: true`, present only when D7 moved the grant from another holder.

  `"license.tier"` for a tier or app purchase. `deliverable` follows D4. The `productId` of an app
  claim is the store's app identity. Refusal reasons do not change, and there is no new error
  code.

### 6.5 THREAT-MODEL and PRIVACY

**THREAT-MODEL.** A new subsection, "One commerce model (CM-20)", goes after "The commerce bridge
(P6-01)" (`docs/security/THREAT-MODEL.md:3096`):

- **Restore transfer.** Under `transfer`, the binding is a placement hint. A leaked signed
  transaction, or a leaked Play token, can therefore move an add-on to its bearer's licence.
  Bounds:
  - the store re-read still decides whether the purchase is real;
  - a transfer never happens inside one account; there, a purchase moves only off an unusable
    licence (D6.2);
  - the 24-hour interval on `moved_at`;
  - the previous holder's notice and the audit row;
  - `block`, per store;
  - licence-minting purchases never move.
- **Joining a purchase-minted licence (D6.3).** Under `block`, only the buyer's own device and
  devices signed in to the licence's account can join. Under `transfer`, a device presenting the
  store-verified purchase can also join, so a leaked Apple JWS or Play token can add a device to a
  floating minted licence. Bounds:
  - the licence's seats;
  - its device list, from which the holder can remove a device;
  - I-09's `license_owned` for a licence in an account;
  - for Steam, the ticket proves the Steam account itself.
- **Purchase-minted licences.** They are idempotent on `idx_licenses_ext_ref`, so a replay cannot
  mint twice. A refund ends them, even on a Worker rolled back to N (§6.3).
- **Activation by itself.** It needs the operator's audited app assignment, so a repo push still
  cannot aim a key at another app. A stored identity that differs from the pin wins until the
  operator confirms (D12). A storefront with no SKU grants nothing, and the Steam oracle guard
  stays.
- **Test purchases.** `commerce.acceptTestPurchases` is `securityWidening`, with an L2 confirm to
  turn on. The migration only narrows, and production defaults to off.
- **Offers backfill.** The job's report comes before apply, it checks the projection is equal, and
  it never merges purchases.
- **Ledger exposure.** Operators with the Commerce area can search purchases by holder email. No
  purchase key or token is ever shown.
- **`offers[]`.** Reading it needs the licence's device token. It carries store product ids and
  labels the stores already publish, and no prices, holders or counts.

**PRIVACY.** The "Store purchases" section (`docs/PRIVACY.md:120`) gains:

- the offer a purchase matched;
- when a restore moved it, and from which licence. The previous holder is told it moved, never to
  whom;
- for store subscriptions (LX-23): their status, period end and renewal state, keyed by the hashed
  original transaction id or purchase token;
- for app purchases (CM-25): the App Store app transaction id, the Play licensing verdict already
  recorded by P6-02, or a Steam ID with the base app id;
- "Used for: granting, moving, renewing and revoking what was bought, and nothing else."

## 8. Rollout and rollback

1. **Approval.** The brief changes in §14 are applied. LX-18 can then take its corpus-lane slot.
2. **LX-11, release N.** The migrations run first, then the Worker. The Worker still serving
   during that window never names the new tables. The lead runs both jobs: dry run, report,
   apply. The device wire and the transcripts stay byte-identical.
3. **LX-11, release N+1**, once the offers report shows zero diffs and the restore report finds
   nothing missing.
   - Reads switch, and the §3 members appear.
   - It holds the corpus lane for its two transcripts.
   - Release N, still serving during the deploy window, writes both the legacy rows and the
     offers, and N+1 reads the offers.
4. **The rest of the commerce work.** CM-21, CM-22, LX-23, CM-25 and CM-24, as their dependencies
   allow; then CM-23 and CM-26; then the LX-20 wave.
5. **Contract.** LX-16 stops the legacy writes and reads. LX-16b drops the tables after 7 days
   with no read or write.

**Kill switches.** None of these needs a deploy:

- retire an offer;
- set a store's `restore` to `block`;
- unassign the app, which makes the storefront Not set up;
- turn `commerce.acceptTestPurchases` off.

**Rollback ladder** (P0-24):

- **N to its predecessor.** The new tables go unread, and the down script drops them. A Worker
  older than N drops `restore` when an operator saves commerce settings. So after rolling forward
  again, the lead re-runs `commerce.restore` before N+1 ships, and its gate catches any value that
  was lost.
- **N+1 to N.** This is safe:
  - N+1 kept the old rows in step, so N's catch-up re-projects the same grants. One thing
    changes: a grant that N+1 marked `refunded` reads as `revoked`, which is still inactive.
  - Tier and app SKUs exist only in `dist_offer_skus`, so N refuses new purchases of them as
    `unmapped`. Stores do not redeliver:
    - N answers 200 to an Apple notification it cannot place, recording it `unresolved`
      (`index.ts:622-656`);
    - it acknowledges a Play push (`:726-760`).

    Those purchases come back only when a device claims them after the roll-forward: through
    `commerce.sync()`, or when an SDK presents a store transaction again that it has not finished
    (SDKs finish only after a claim answered ok, `commerce.gd:30-31`). A Play purchase that stays
    unacknowledged for three days is refunded by Google. The lead lists the window's `unresolved`
    events in the ledger.

  - A refund in the window still ends a minted licence, because N's `revokeRecordedPurchase`
    disables it by `(source, external_ref_hash)` (§6.3). A reversal in the window leaves the
    licence disabled until the device's next claim, which re-enables it under N+1 (D9).
- **Past LX-16b.** Not supported, as in `plans/LX-01.md`.

## 9. Test strategy

- **Worker (vitest on D1).**
  - The migrations replay twice, the triggers refuse bad values, and the down script runs.
  - **The placement matrix.** It covers new or recorded × same licence, the same account (L
    usable or not) or another holder × `transfer` or `block` × inside or outside the move
    interval. A move inside one account sends no notice and leaves `moved_at` alone.
  - **Notification first.** An Apple `ONE_TIME_CHARGE` or a Play RTDN that arrives before the
    device's claim lands on the buyer's licence B, even when B is not its account's first licence
    by `rankLicenses()`. The device's claim then answers `changed: false`. For a tier SKU, the
    notification mints M with no device, and the buyer's claim joins M.
  - **Steam bindings.**
    - A Steam claim from a licence an account holds succeeds with that licence's binding.
    - A sign-in that moves the licence into an account mid-session leaves the binding unchanged,
      so a cached one still works.
    - A device re-homed to another licence with a stale binding is refused `invalid_ticket`, as
      today.
  - **Joins.**
    - Under `block`, a device on an unrelated licence that presents the purchase is refused
      `bound_elsewhere`.
    - Under `transfer`, that device joins, seat-checked.
    - A licence in an account refuses another account's device (`license_owned`).
  - The `revokePurchase` cause table, row by row.
  - **Tier mints.** A mint is idempotent under replay. It writes `origin = 'admin'` and `source`,
    and provenance reads `store`.
  - **`products[]`.** The projection equals the pre-switch rows, a mapping that is not `app`
    included (a golden test).
  - The offers API refuses a quantity above 1.
  - **Jobs.** Each job dry-runs, applies, resumes after a kill and re-applies as a no-op. A store
    block saved by a pre-N parser loses `restore`, and the restore job's dry run finds it.
  - **Rollback.** Release N's code runs against release N+1's data, with a reconciliation diff of
    `grants` against `license_store_grants`. N's refund of a minted licence disables it.
  - `boundaries` holds: commerce imports `core/licensing` only.
- **Fixtures.** LX-23's Apple and Play subscription fixtures (a Play upgrade chain and an Apple
  resubscribe included), CM-25's AppTransaction and verdict fixtures, and CM-28's consumables. No
  live calls are made; A-18k's live checks remain an owner step.
- **Transcripts.** §5.1. Each SDK replays them in LX-20, and `parity:check` closes the loop.
- **Security review.** Before merge, for LX-11 (transfer and joins), CM-21 (test purchases) and
  CM-25 (app purchase).
