## 1. Summary

- **Two signed documents.** `pkey-feed+jws` is signed by the Worker's product key. It is
  device-less and covers one channel; its TTL is 900 s and its `seq` grows per (product,
  channel). Each platform's target carries its own floor. `pkey-release+jws` is signed by a
  CI-held release key, which is never a product key. It is P2-04's descriptor moved into a signed
  payload, store-only builds included, fetched by its SHA-256 and verified only against the
  release keys the app pins. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` becomes 4 in a new `WIRE-CONTRACT-V4.md`. The
  four v3 documents keep their shapes and bytes.
- **One stricter verifier for all six `typ`s.** Ed25519 gets fixed rules: `S < L`, canonical
  `A` and `R`, no small-order `A` or `R`, and the cofactorless equation. JSON gets an I-JSON
  profile: well-formed UTF-8, no BOM, RFC 8259's grammar with no `NaN` or `Infinity`, no lone
  surrogates, no U+0000 in a member name, member names compared by scalar value, numbers inside
  binary64's range, and at most 64 levels of nesting. An integer claim, v3's included, is
  decided from its token: a plain integer token from the claim's minimum to 2^53 − 1, so `7.0`,
  `17e8` and `1700000000.00000001` are refused in a claim whatever number a parser makes of
  them. A pattern matches the whole string, a length counts bytes, a required member is
  present, and a member that is not a claim never decides a verdict, so Swift's typed decoders
  become total. The probe found 20 JSON and signature vectors on which today's four backends
  disagree, 6 more that all of them accept but should refuse, and 41 v3 claim and member vectors
  that split them or that all of them wrongly accept. 37 new `jwsCases` and 22 v3 claim cases pin
  the strict verdicts. P3-02 adds the missing checks in `shared-jws`, `client-core`, Python,
  Swift and GDScript; P3-12 makes the Worker refuse at write, and never sign, a value a v4
  verifier would refuse (§2.2).
- **Corpus.** It stays in `conformance/corpus/v2/` with `corpusVersion` 2:
  - `cases.json` gains 37 `jwsCases`, 69 `feedCases`, 45 `releaseRecordCases`, and 22 v3 claim
    cases: 7 in `licenseDocCases`, 2 in `configDocCases`, 7 in `trustCases` and 6 in
    `bundleCases`. Every integer claim of every `typ` has a case that breaks its token rule
    alone, and one that breaks its bound alone wherever no other check implies the bound;
  - a new `update-matrix.json` (`updateMatrixVersion` 1) holds 65 decision rows, 25 version
    cases, 10 capability cases, 12 outlet cases and 6 bucket vectors;
  - a new `outlet-matrix.json` (`outletMatrixVersion` 1) holds the per-kind capability defaults,
    the iOS narrowing and listing-URL prefixes, 25 signals and 48 detection rows;
  - `stage-matrix.json` goes to version 2 for boot confirmation;
  - `CORPUS_TARGETS` mirrors every file to Swift and Godot;
  - the generator recomputes every decision, detection and outlet row with its own reference
    implementation, so a row that contradicts §2.8 or §2.9 fails `gen:corpus`.
- **The decision** is a pure, synchronous `decideUpdate` over the verified feed and record, the
  installed build, the outlet (the product's outlet id and its kind, normalised by
  `resolveUpdateOutlet`) and the host's update methods. Versions compare under the product's
  scheme (`semver`, `semver+build` or `4part`, with P2-05's rules and SemVer 2.0's grammar). It
  returns `none`, `code-ready`, `binary`, `store`, `platform` or `blocked`; `packs`,
  `content-floor` and `revoked-content` are reserved names that no v4 list contains, and
  `binary` carries an empty `prestage`. No v4 answer stops play: a
  mandatory offer, or a floor the outlet cannot clear, is a prompt the player cannot dismiss
  (A4 P6). Capabilities default per outlet kind in one table, narrowed for `direct` on iOS, and
  the feed can narrow but never widen them.
- **Routes and order.** P3-03 serves `GET /{product}/update/{channel}/feed.jws?platform=` and
  `GET /{product}/release/records/{sha256}`. The packages follow in this order, enforced as
  graph edges: P3-02; then P3-03, P3-05 and P3-12 side by side; then P3-04, P3-06, P3-07 and
  P3-08 after P3-05; then P3-11. P3-03 imports only the four pure functions P3-02 lands, so it
  does not wait for P3-05 (decision 3). Deployed clients are unaffected once a one-time D1 check
  passes, and v4 SDKs fall back to `check()` on older workers (§7). A signer that fast-forwards
  `seq` is recovered at the `seq` ceiling without a client change (§2.3).

### 2.2 One stricter verifier for every `typ`

**§1.1 Ed25519 (normative, [C]).** A verifier applies these checks to the 64-byte signature
`R ‖ S` and to the trusted key `A` before its backend's verify call:

1. `S`, read as a 256-bit little-endian integer, is less than
   `L = 2^252 + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493`.
2. `A` and `R` are canonical encodings. The 255-bit `y` (bit 255 cleared) is below
   `p = 2^255 − 19`, and the encoding is neither of the two "x = 0 with the sign bit set"
   strings, `01 00…00 80` and `ec ff…ff ff`.
3. Neither `A` nor `R` is one of the eight small-order encodings (`SMALL_ORDER_ENCODINGS`,
   lowercase hex, checked with noble on 2026-10-01):
   `0100…00` (order 1); `ecff…ff7f` (order 2); `0000…00` and `0000…0080` (order 4);
   `26e8958fc2b227b045c3f489f2ef98f0d5dfac05d3c63339b13802886d53fc05`, its `…fc85` twin,
   `c7176a703d4dd84fba3c0b760d10670f2a2053fa2c39ccc64ec7fd7792ac037a` and its `…03fa` twin
   (order 8).
4. The equation is cofactorless, `[S]B = R + [k]A`. Every backend measured does this. No
   prime-subgroup check is made, so a mixed-order key with a cofactorless-valid signature
   verifies.

Checks 1–3 are byte comparisons. A lenient backend therefore never accepts what a strict one
refuses.

**§1.2 Strict JSON (normative, [C]).** These rules apply to the decoded header (before the
signature check) and to the decoded payload (after it), for all six `typ`s:

1. The bytes are well-formed UTF-8 (RFC 3629): no ill-formed sequences, no overlongs, no
   surrogate code points, nothing above U+10FFFF.
2. There is no byte order mark at the start.
3. The text is exactly one JSON object under RFC 8259's grammar: no `NaN`, `Infinity` or
   `-Infinity` literal, no comment, no trailing comma. Python's verifier does not hold this
   today: `json.loads` without `parse_constant` accepts the three literals
   (`sdks/python/src/polaris_key/core/jws.py:120-122`).
4. No string contains an unescaped U+0000–U+001F, as RFC 8259 already requires.
5. No escape is a lone surrogate. Every `\uD800`–`\uDBFF` escape is immediately followed by a
   `\uDC00`–`\uDFFF` escape, and every `\uDC00`–`\uDFFF` escape immediately follows one
   (RFC 7493 §2.1).
6. No object has two members with the same name. Names compare as sequences of Unicode scalar
   values after unescaping (RFC 7493 §2.3), so the NFC and NFD spellings of "é" are two names.
   Swift does not hold this today: `StrictJSON.hasDuplicateKeys` keys a `Set<String>`
   (`sdks/swift/Sources/PolarisKeyCore/JWSVerifier.swift:211`), and Swift's `String` equality is
   canonical equivalence, so it refuses that pair where Node, Python and Godot accept it.
7. No member name contains U+0000. Values keep V3 §10's representation limit and its
   `docNulReplaced` annotation.
8. **Numbers.** Every number token, judged exactly from its decimal digits (no floating point;
   an exponent of more than six significant digits is out of range outright), is zero or has a
   magnitude of at least 10^−307 and below 10^308, so no parser overflows, underflows or flushes
   a subnormal value. So `1e400`, `1e-400` and `5e-324` are refused, and `7`, `-0`, `7.0`, `7e0`,
   `7.5`, `1e-7`, `1e+21`, `1e-307` and `9.99e307` are not. `JSON.stringify` writes a token this
   refuses only for a non-zero value whose magnitude is below 10^−307 or at least 10^308. How a
   number is spelled matters only in an integer claim (below).

9. **Depth.** No object or array nests more than `MAX_JSON_DEPTH` = 64 levels deep, counting
   the top-level object as level 1. Today Foundation's `JSONDecoder` refuses more than 512
   levels, while Node reads 32 000 and Python 5 000 (measured).

**Integers (V4 §3, normative, [C]).** The integer claims are every field typed `integer` in §2.3
and §2.4, and these v3 claims: `issuedAt`, `expiresAt` and `graceUntil` of the licence and
config documents; `schemaVersion` of the config document and of the trust manifest; and
`issuedAt` and `expiresAt` of the trust manifest and of the bundle. A verifier accepts an integer
claim only when both hold:

1. its token is a **plain integer token**, `-?(0|[1-9][0-9]*)`, with no fraction part and no
   exponent part;
2. its value lies from the claim's minimum to `MAX_WIRE_INTEGER` = 2^53 − 1.

Only then does the claim's own check run (`=== 1`, `≥ 1`, the freshness window, …), and `true`
or `false` is never a number. The verifier decides rule 1 from the token, never from the number
a parser made of it: `7.0`, `17e8`, `7.5` and `7.0000000000000001` are all refused in an integer
claim. Every other number in a document (an entitlement or config value, a member of
`requires`, an unknown member) is a value, and rule 8 is its only constraint.

A rule on the parsed value cannot make four parsers agree. A near-integer token such as
`1700000000.00000001` denotes no integer, but binary64 rounding makes it one: Node reads
1700000000 (`Number.isSafeInteger` holds), Swift's `JSONDecoder` decodes it as an `Int`, and
Godot reads a float equal to its floor, while Python's `json.loads` returns a `float`. Godot's
parser is also not correctly rounded. It reads `9007199254740989.5` as 9007199254740989 where
Node and Swift read 9007199254740990, and `9007199254740991.0` as 9007199254740990,
`0.0000000000000000000000000007e28` as 0, `1e23` as 9.999999999999999e22 and
`2.2250738585072014e-308` as 0 (all measured). A rule on the token needs no arithmetic, and
every integer claim the Worker signs is already a plain integer token, because `JSON.stringify`
writes a safe integer as digits.

**How each verifier sees the token.** A prototype in each language ran over four test payloads
on 2026-10-01 and reported the same pointers in all four:

- `shared-jws`'s `scanStrictJson`, Swift's `StrictJSON.validate` and Godot's `PKeyJson` token
  walk keep a path stack and report `nonWireIntegers`: the RFC 6901 pointer (`/issuedAt`,
  `/app/targets/0/release/seq`) of every number token that cannot be a wire integer, because it
  has a fraction or an exponent part, or because its digits exceed 9007199254740991 in magnitude
  (more than 16 digits, or 16 that compare above). Member names are unescaped, then `~` is
  written `~0` and `/` is written `~1`; array indices are decimal. `verifyJws` returns the set
  beside the payload (`VerifiedJws.nonWireIntegers`; Godot adds a `non_wire_integers`
  Dictionary to the results of `PKeyJson.parse_bytes` and `PKeyJws.verify`). The claim helpers
  take the claim's pointer: `isWireInteger(value, pointer, min, nonWire)` in client-core,
  `wireInteger(_:pointer:min:in:)` in Swift and
  `PKeyClaims.is_wire_integer(v, pointer, minimum, non_wire_integers)` in GDScript. Swift keys
  the set by Unicode scalars, as rule 6 keys member names;
- Python needs no set. `json.loads` returns an `int` exactly for a plain integer token and a
  `float` for any other, so `_wire_int(value, minimum)`, an `int` that is never a `bool`, is
  already the token rule, and its comparison with 2^53 − 1 is exact.

So both rules are decided from the token, or from Python's exact `int`. That matters for Godot,
whose parser is not correctly rounded in general, although it read 1700000000, 2^53 − 1 and 2^53
exactly, and 2^53 + 1, 2^54 and 10^20 as 2^53 or more (measured). The value checks stay as a
second line (`Number.isSafeInteger`, Swift's `Int` decode and `≤ MAX_WIRE_INTEGER`). A number
value keeps each language's own reading: Godot's can differ in its last bits, and Python reads
`7.0` as a `float`. V4 §10 records both as representation limits; the verdict agrees.

**Patterns, lengths and presence (V4 §3, normative, [C]).** Three rules make every claim check
of §2.3, §2.4 and §2.8 come out the same in every language:

1. **A pattern matches the whole string.** A value matches only when the pattern matches all of
   it, with nothing after the match, a line terminator included. JavaScript uses the pattern
   with no flags. Python uses `re.fullmatch`, Swift `Regex.wholeMatch(in:)` or an
   `NSRegularExpression` written `\A…\z`, and GDScript a `RegEx` written `\A…\z`. A pattern kept
   as `^…$`, such as the generated `CHANNEL_NAME_PATTERN`, also needs a check that the match
   spans the whole string. Every class is ASCII: `[0-9]`, never `\d`, which matches every
   Unicode decimal digit in Python's `re` and in ICU. Each SDK runs every pattern through one
   helper (Python `_full_match`, Swift `wholeMatches`, GDScript `PKeyClaims.matches_whole`),
   whose unit tests put `\n`, `\r\n`, `\r`, U+0085, U+2028 and U+2029 after a valid value.
2. **A length counts UTF-8 bytes.** Every bounded string in §2.3 and §2.4 is ASCII by its
   pattern, or must be (`listingUrl`), so bytes, code points, UTF-16 units and Swift's
   `Character`s give the same count.
3. **Presence.** A required member must be present. `null` satisfies it only where its type
   includes `null`, so `targets[].floor` and `outlets.*.live` may be `null` but not missing. An
   optional member is absent or of its type, and a present `null` is refused. Python tests
   `key in obj`, never `.get(key) is not None`; Swift checks `container.contains(key)` before
   `decodeNil(forKey:)`, never `decodeIfPresent` alone; GDScript uses `has()`. A payload that
   verifies but does not decode into the SDK's types fails at the claims step with that family's
   claims reason, never at the signature step: Swift calls `JWSVerifier.verify` and decodes
   afterwards.

terminator):

**Keeping the signer total** (P3-12, decision 3; nothing here changes a verifier). Two guards
make the Worker unable to sign what a v4 verifier refuses, and write checks keep stored data
from tripping them:

- `signJws` in `packages/shared-jws` throws `StrictJsonError` when its serialized header or
  payload breaks rule 5, 7, 8 or 9. `JSON.stringify` cannot break the others;
- `signDoc` (`core/signing.ts:29-35`) throws `StrictJsonError` when an integer claim of a v3
  `typ` is not a safe integer of at least the claim's minimum. P3-12 moves `signTrustManifest`
  (`core/trust.ts:98`) onto `signDoc`, with the same bytes, so every v3 document passes this
  guard. For `pkey-feed+jws`, P3-03's composer runs `feedClaims` and `scanStrictJson` before
  `signDoc` (§6), which covers the feed whether P3-12 has landed or not; no Worker signs a
  release record.
  The builders compute each integer claim with integer arithmetic, including
  `graceUntil = now + Math.floor(maxOfflineDays × 86 400)` (`core/documents.ts:78,146`), so the
  guard never fires on stored data.

Without write checks, the first guard would turn stored operator data into failures, because such
values reach signed documents today:

- config, secret and flag values are catalog-typed JSON. An `object` value may have any member
  names (U+0000 in one, or two canonically equivalent ones), any depth and any number, and
  nothing in `shared-catalog` refuses them;
- `JSON.stringify` writes a lone surrogate as the ASCII escape `\ud800`, which survives in D1's
  JSON-text columns;
- several admin handlers store a value with less checking than the manifest validator applies to
  the same value, and some values reach signed bytes only through the manifest sync or OIDC
  sign-in. The inventory below lists every one.

One function decides representability: `representabilityIssue(value) → {rule, path} | null` in
`shared-catalog`, with five rules:

- `lone-surrogate`, in any string, member names included;
- `nul-in-member-name`;
- `equivalent-member-names`: two sibling names that differ but are equal after
  `normalize("NFC")`. The verifier accepts them (rule 6), but Swift's `JSONValue` would keep only
  the first (measured), so no document may carry them;
- `number-out-of-range`: rule 8's range;
- `too-deep`: more than 32 levels inside the value. A document wraps at most three levels around
  an operator value (`entitlements.<key>.value`), so 32 stays inside rule 9.

  - a byte-level `StrictJSON.validate` on the header bytes and on the payload bytes in step 13,
    covering rules 1–9 and JSON syntax, with each object's member set keyed by
    `Array(key.unicodeScalars)` instead of `String` (`JWSVerifier.swift:211`). It reports
    `nonWireIntegers`, keyed by scalars, and `VerifiedJws` carries it. Today step 13 runs only
    the duplicate scan, and `JSONDecoder` skips members it does not decode;
  - `wireInteger(_:pointer:min:in:)` on every integer claim after its `Int` decode
    (`Verify.swift`, `Trust.swift`, `Bundle.swift`);
  - the total decoders of "Members outside the claims" for `ManagedEntry`, `DocProfile`,
    `TrustManifestDoc` and `TrustManifestKey` (`Models.swift`), and a present `profile: null`
    that throws;
  - `inspectBundle` on `JWSVerifier.verify`, with the refusing `BundleDoc` decoder after it, so
    a missing or mistyped member and a present `null` in `docs` are `bundleClaimsRejected`;
  - `wholeMatches`, the pattern helper, with `Semver.parse` on it and `[0-9]` for `\d`.
- **Godot** (in `sdks/godot/addons/polaris_key/core/`: `crypto/ed25519.gd`, `json_strict.gd`,
  `jws.gd`, `claims.gd`, `verify.gd`, `trust.gd` and `bundle.gd`):
  - the `x = 0` sign-bit rule for `A`, and the small-order table for `A` and `R`;
  - `PKeyJson.parse_bytes` refuses ill-formed UTF-8 (the re-encoded text must equal the bytes)
    and a leading BOM (no longer stripped);
  - the token walk refuses a member name whose escape is `\u0000` (before `nul_as_fffd` runs),
    rule 8's out-of-range tokens and nesting past 64, and records the `non_wire_integers`
    pointers;
  - `PKeyClaims.is_wire_integer(v, pointer, minimum, non_wire_integers)` (the pointer is not in the
    set, `v` is a finite float or int, `v == floor(v)` and `minimum ≤ v ≤ 2^53 − 1`) replaces
    `is_number` and the float tests in `verify.gd:19-20` (`_is_valid_schema_version`),
    `verify.gd:56-59`, `trust.gd:45-46,53-55` and `bundle.gd:49-51`;
  - `PKeyClaims.matches_whole`, the pattern helper. `semver.gd` already writes `\A…\z`, and
    `bundle.gd` already refuses a present `null` in `docs`.

### 2.3 The channel feed, `pkey-feed+jws`

Signed by the product key (`signDoc(…, "pkey-feed+jws")`). It carries no device id, so every
caller of a channel gets identical bytes. Payload cap 65 536 bytes. Every `integer` field is an
integer claim (§2.2): a plain integer token from the field's minimum to 2^53 − 1, checked at
its RFC 6901 pointer (`/seq`, `/app/targets/0/release/seq`, …). Every pattern, length and
member below follows §2.2's "Patterns, lengths and presence".

| Field                    | JSON type      | Req. | Verifier check (refusal reason)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------ | -------------- | ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `schemaVersion`          | integer        | yes  | `=== 1` (`claims`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

| `aud`                    | string         | yes  | `===` the product (`claims`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

| `app.targets`            | array          | yes  | at most one item per `platform` value; when `selector.platform` is set, every item has that platform                                                                                                                                                               |

- **Outlet ids and kinds.** P2b-02 lets a product declare outlets under its own ids, each of
  one **kind**. README §3.1's 17 names are the kinds; `altstore-beta` is an id of kind
  `altstore`. The feed keys `targets[].outlets` by the product's outlet id, and every entry
  carries its `kind`. Capability defaults, platforms, listing prefixes and detection are per
  kind. A client's outlet is the pair `{id, kind}` (§2.8). Two outlets of one kind on one
  platform are both listed, and the install's own id picks between them.
- **A floor per platform.** P2-05 resolves each platform separately, so one channel often pins
  different releases on different platforms. Each target therefore carries the floor computed
  for its own platform (§6), never above its own pin. A macOS-only hotfix published with
  `--min-supported-seq` raises the macOS floor and leaves Windows alone.
- **One feed per (product, channel)**, requested as
  `GET /{product}/update/{channel}/feed.jws?platform={platform}`. The Worker answers with the
  channel-wide document (`selector: {}`) while its UTF-8 JSON fits in 65 536 bytes. Otherwise it
  splits the channel into per-platform documents (`selector: {platform}`) and answers with the
  one for the requested platform. A per-platform document that still does not fit is refused
  with a 5xx (P3-03). The SDK always sends `platform`, so a split is invisible to it.
- **`seq` per (aud, channel), shared by every selector of the channel.** The Worker composes the
  whole channel at once. It bumps `seq` when the composed content's SHA-256 changes, and every
  selector document carries the same `seq`. A client keeps one floor per (aud, channel), so
  switching selectors (today platform; in P4 `contentApi`) can never evade it.
- **The `seq` ceiling, and recovering from a fast-forward (V4 §4, [C]).** `seq` never exceeds
  `MAX_WIRE_INTEGER`: at the ceiling, a change of content is signed at the ceiling again with a
  newer `issuedAt`, which §2.5 step 8 accepts. A signer that holds the product key (a
  compromised Worker or KEK) can sign a fresh feed at the ceiling. Every install that fetches it
  commits it, refuses the recovered Worker's lower `seq` as `rollback`, decides from the
  attacker's feed until that is stale, and then freezes (`none {stale}`). The recovery needs no
  client change. The operator runs P3-03's `feed:seq-ceiling` script for every channel of the
  product, which sets the channel's `seq` to `MAX_WIRE_INTEGER` and drops the stored documents,
  so the next request signs at the ceiling with the current `issuedAt`. That is newer than any
  `issuedAt` the attacker's feed can carry, because a client refuses one more than 300 s ahead of
  its effective clock (step 7); a client whose clock runs ahead accepts the recovered feed once
  real time passes the attacker's `issuedAt`. The RUNBOOK runs the script after any suspected
  product-key or Worker compromise, without waiting for evidence, and the threat model's AT-3
  names the attack (§6). A second lever covers a manifest key: a committed feed whose key has
  left the effective trust set no longer verifies, so it is dropped with its floor (step 8). A
  pinned key cannot leave, which is why the ceiling exists. `feed-valid-seq-ceiling-recovery`
  pins the step-8 behaviour at the ceiling. Bounding the step between two `seq` values was
  rejected: a device offline through many releases legitimately sees a large jump, and a bound
  still leaves the recovered Worker needing a `seq` above whatever the attacker signed.
- **TTL.** `expiresAt = issuedAt + FEED_TTL_SECONDS` (900, the top of README §3.9's 5–15 min).
  The Worker stores the signed document per (channel, selector) in D1 and serves it to every data
  centre, re-signing when the content changes or the stored copy is 450 s old (§6). Callers
  therefore see one signing at a time; a cache can still hand a client an older signing of the
  same `seq`, which §2.5 step 8 keeps silently.
- **Stale means freeze.** A feed is stale when `now ≥ expiresAt + CLOCK_SKEW_SECONDS`, with
  `now` the effective clock. The decision then answers `none {reason: "stale"}`: keep running
  and never update automatically (notes/E5 §1.4).
- **No clock floor.** Neither the feed nor the record raises the clock floor, so V3 §4.2 and its
  `clockFloorCases` stay exactly as they are. The feed's freshness is checked against the
  effective clock, so winding the system clock back cannot revive an expired feed.

### 2.4 The release record, `pkey-release+jws`

Signed in CI with a release key; the Worker never holds the private half. Payload cap 65 536
bytes. Every `integer` field is an integer claim (§2.2), and every pattern, length and member
follows §2.2's "Patterns, lengths and presence", as in §2.3. A record has no `iss`:
`key.plrs.im` would be false, and the pinned `kid` already names the signer. `aud` binds the
record to one product even when one release key serves several.

A record built from a descriptor that P2-04's validator accepts passes these claims, except in
three cases: a build holding two `payload` files (P2-04 allows that only when the artifact map
gives the build another role), a record over the 65 536-byte cap, and a string that §2.2's strict
JSON refuses (a lone surrogate in `notes`, say). P3-03's CLI refuses to sign such a record, and
its ingest runs the same claims function every v4 SDK runs (§6), so no stored record fails a
client's claims. A non-empty build with no `payload` file is not refused: it is a build
whose artifact map entry declares another role, which P2-04 accepts, so it verifies and is never
eligible (`record-valid-non-payload-build`).

**From the descriptor to the record** (P2-04's body is moved, not reshaped). P3-03's
implementation of P2-06's `signRecord` seam builds the record this way, and its ingest checks it
field for field:

| P2-04 descriptor                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Record                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `descriptorVersion: 1`                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | dropped; the record has `schemaVersion: 1`                                                                             |
| `product`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | `aud`                                                                                                                  |
| `seq` (optional)                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | `seq`, required: the release's `seq` from the upload answer (§6, §8 item 4)                                            |
| `publishedAt`                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | dropped; `issuedAt` is the signing time                                                                                |
| `artifacts[].locations[]`                                                                                                                                                                                                                | dropped: locations change after signing (README §3.3)                                                                  |
| `deliverable`, `kind`, `version`, `tag`, `channel`, `title`, `notes`, `provenance`, and `builds[]` with `id`, `platform`, `arch`, `format`, `buildNumber`, `minOS`, `requires` and `artifacts[] {name, role, sha256, size, contentType}` | moved unchanged. An optional field the descriptor omits stays absent, and a store-only build keeps its `artifacts: []` |
| —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | `minSupportedSeq` (new, optional; `pkey release publish --min-supported-seq <n>`)                                      |

**Release keys are never product keys.** If a product key were pinned or declared as a release
key, the Worker would hold the private half of a release key and the two-signer property (README
§3.3) would be gone without a trace. Three checks stop it:

- `verifyReleaseRecord` refuses at step `jws` when the selected release key's bytes are also in
  the effective product trust set (§2.5 step 13; `record-release-key-is-product-key`);
- every SDK refuses options whose `pinnedReleaseKeys` and trust pins share a key (§2.6);
- P3-03's `.pkey/release` sync refuses a declared release key equal to any current or retired
  product signing key (§6).

**Bytes.** The record never says where bytes are. Discovery advertises two templates (P2-05,
`services/release/index.ts:67-69`): `release.endpoints.blobs` =
`<bytesBase>/release/blobs/sha256/{sha256}` and `release.endpoints.builds` =
`<bytesBase>/release/builds/{selector}/{buildId}`. After P2b-04, `distribution.endpoints.blobs`
and `.builds` carry the canonical `/distribution/…` spellings, and the release paths stay as
aliases. An SDK reads `distribution.endpoints.*` when distribution's fragment has them, and
`release.endpoints.*` otherwise. It substitutes, percent-encoding each value with
`encodeURIComponent`:

- to install a build, `{sha256}` = the payload's `sha256` in `blobs`;
- to hand a person a download link, `{selector}` = the record's `version` and `{buildId}` = the
  build's `id` in `builds`.

Either way it verifies `size` and SHA-256 against the record before staging. P2-05's blob route
serves R2-held bytes only. P2b-04 extends it to the other locations P2-05's file route serves:
GitHub through `releaseCatalog.openSource`, and an `external` https URL by 302 (§6). P3-03
depends on P2b-04, so no feed pins a record before both exist.

### 2.5 Client verification order

Each step names the corpus family that pins it. `now` is the effective clock,
`max(system, highWaterMark)` (V3 §4.2).

1. Read `update.endpoints.feed` and `release.endpoints.record` from discovery. If either is
   absent (an older Worker, or Update off), refuse with `service-unavailable` before dialling.
   The host falls back to `update.check()` (P3-03's transcripts).
2. `GET …/update/{channel}/feed.jws?platform={platform}`. If the body equals the committed JWS
   for this channel, nothing changed: go to step 10 (each SDK's wiring tests, P3-04 to P3-08).
3. `verifyJws` with the **effective** trust set (pins ∪ manifest keys, as for documents) and `typ`
   `pkey-feed+jws`. This includes §2.2's rules (`jwsCases`; `feedCases` reason `jws`).
4. The claims in §2.3 (`feedCases` reason `claims`).
5. Channel binding (`feedCases` reason `channel`).
6. Selector (`feedCases` reason `selector`).
7. Network path only: freshness at `now` (`feedCases` reason `freshness`).
8. The `seq` floor for (aud, channel), derived from the committed feed while that feed still
   passes steps 3–6 against the current effective trust set (`feedCases`). A committed feed that
   no longer does, because its key left the set, is dropped, and with it the floor (§2.3):
   - `seq > floor.seq`, or `seq == floor.seq` with `issuedAt > floor.issuedAt`: accept;
   - `seq == floor.seq` with `issuedAt ≤ floor.issuedAt`: reason `not-newer`. Below the ceiling
     this is an older signing of the same content, since the Worker bumps `seq` on every change
     of content; at the ceiling it is an older signing. Either way the committed feed stays and
     nothing is reported;
   - `seq < floor.seq`: reason `rollback`. This is README §3.3's "refuse a lower `seq`". Rolling
     back means rolling forward: CI publishes a higher version.
9. Commit the feed: Core's read-modify-write of `feeds[channel]` (§2.6; each SDK's restart
   tests).
10. Take the target for this platform. If there is none, decide without a record (step 18).
11. Use `releaseRecords[sha256]` from the cache, or `GET …/release/records/{sha256}`. Where it
    can (a `Content-Length`, a streaming read), the transport stops reading at 88 845 bytes.
12. **Hash before signature.** A body of more than `MAX_RECORD_JWS_BYTES` = 88 844 bytes, or
    with any byte outside ASCII, cannot be a record any feed pins: the bound is `verifyJws`'s own
    encoded caps, 1 370 + 1 + 87 386 + 1 + 86. It is refused at this step without hashing
    (`record-rejected`, detail `hash`). Otherwise the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the exact body
    bytes must equal `release.sha256`, before any Ed25519 work (`releaseRecordCases` step
    `hash`). Each SDK unit-tests the bound with an 88 845-byte body whose hash is the pin; the
    corpus does not carry one, which keeps to P3-02's size budget, and an SDK that skipped the
    bound would still refuse that body, at step `jws`.
13. Select the key by `kid` from the **pinned release keys only**, never merged with, and never
    added to from, the product trust set. Refuse if its raw 32 bytes equal any key of the
    effective product trust set. Then `verifyJws` with that one key and `typ` `pkey-release+jws`
    (`releaseRecordCases` step `jws`, including the key-separation cases).
14. The record claims in §2.4 (`releaseRecordCases` step `claims`).
15. Cross-check against the pin: `kind` and `deliverable` are `"app"`, and `version` and `seq`
    equal the pin's (`releaseRecordCases` step `cross-check`).
16. Commit the record to `releaseRecords[sha256]` (each SDK's restart tests).
17. If the outlet's entry has a `rollout`: `bucket = rolloutBucket(rollout.salt, installId)`
    (`update-matrix.json` `bucketVectors`).
18. `decideUpdate` (`update-matrix.json` rows), with the outlet and subkind from
    `resolveUpdateOutlet` (`outletCases`), then `bootDecision` (each row's `expect.boot`).
19. When acting, verify the bytes against the record: payload `size` and `sha256`, before
    staging and again before a swap (P3-10 unit tests; host behaviour, not a corpus family).

**After a refusal** (every SDK, the same way). `client.update.decide()` returns an
`UpdateCheck` with four members:

- `decision`, the `UpdateDecision`;
- `feed`: `"network"` when the fetched copy was committed or equals the committed one, and
  `"committed"` when the decision used the earlier copy instead;
- `record`: `"network"`, `"cache"` or `"none"`;
- `errors`: a list of `{code, detail}`, per the table below.

Then:

- It decides from the committed feed whenever the fetched one is not committed: offline, a
  transport failure, `not-newer`, or a refusal at steps 3–8. A committed feed past
  `expiresAt + 300` gives `none {stale}` (§2.8 rule 1), so a stale feed freezes.
- It raises a `PolarisKeyError` only when it has nothing to decide from: no committed feed for
  the channel, and the fetch failed or the feed was refused. It also raises `not-configured` and
  `service-unavailable` as below.
- A record that cannot be fetched or is refused is null for this call (a self-updating outlet
  then answers `none not-available`), unless `releaseRecords` already holds that hash.

| Step    | Outcome                                  | Code                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | `detail`                                            | Surfaces as                                         |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |

### 2.6 Cache, floors and keys

`CacheRecordV3` (`packages/client-core/src/store.ts:34`) gains two optional slices.
`CACHE_VERSION` stays 3, because the change is additive: v3 loaders ignore unknown members, and
a v4 loader treats a record without them as "no floor yet".

```ts
feeds?: Record<string /* canonical channel */, string /* pkey-feed+jws, verbatim */>;
releaseRecords?: Record<string /* lowercase hex sha256 */, string /* pkey-release+jws */>;
```

- **Signed artifacts only** (V3 §4.1). Floors are derived on load, never stored.
- **What is written.** The SDK writes the slice for the channel it fetched and leaves other
  channels' entries alone, so switching back cannot be rolled back. It may drop entries for
  channels it no longer follows. A record is kept only while a committed feed pins it.
- **Release keys.** `pinnedReleaseKeys` (`pinned_release_keys` in Python and GDScript) maps
  `kid` to a raw 32-byte Ed25519 key in base64url, the `TrustSet` encoding. It is configured by
  the host, compiled into the app, and never persisted, merged or extended from the network:
  - an empty map makes `decide()` raise `not-configured`;
  - a key that is also among the trust pins makes construction (Godot: `configure()`) raise
    `invalid-options`;
  - two or more keys are valid at once during a rotation.
- **Fingerprints.** Discovery may advertise `release.releaseKeyFingerprints` (lowercase hex
  SHA-256 of each raw key) for tooling such as `pkey release keys check`. No SDK reads it.

### 2.8 The update decision

**Inputs** (`UpdateDecisionInput`). The brief's "previous channel" is `staged.channel`, and its
"capabilities" are computed inside, from the outlet's kind, the platform, `subkind` and the feed.

**The outlet.** Every SDK builds the decision's `outlet` and `subkind` with one pure function,
`resolveUpdateOutlet({host?, stamp?, detected?}) → {id, kind, subkind}`, pinned by
`update-matrix.json`'s `outletCases`:

1. A host value wins. A host configures a kind, such as `"steam"`, which reads as
   `{id: "steam", kind: "steam"}`, or `{id, kind, subkind?}`. A kind outside `OUTLET_KINDS`, an
   id that does not match `OUTLET_ID_PATTERN`, or a subkind outside `OUTLET_SUBKINDS` raises
   `invalid-options` when the client is constructed (Godot: `configure()`).
2. The stamp's kind is its `outletKind`, or its `outlet` when it has no `outletKind` (P1-11's
   export-time default). A kind outside `OUTLET_KINDS` counts as no kind. Its id is `outlet`
   when that matches `OUTLET_ID_PATTERN`, otherwise null. Its subkind is `outletSubkind` when
   that is in `OUTLET_SUBKINDS`, otherwise null.
3. With a detection result (P3-11): `{id: the stamp's id, kind, subkind: detected.subkind}` when
   the detected kind equals the stamp's kind, and otherwise
   `{id: null, kind: detected.kind, subkind: detected.subkind}`, `unknown` included.
4. Without one: the stamp's `{id, kind, subkind}`, when the stamp has a kind.
5. Otherwise `{id: null, kind: "unknown", subkind: null}`.

So a host with no outlet option and no stamp, which is every Node, Python and Swift host until
P3-11, decides as `unknown` and is never offered an update (row 45). P3-04, P3-06 and P3-07
document `outlet` as the option that turns offers on.

**Versions.** `cmp(a, b)` is `compareVersions(feed.app.versionScheme, a, b)`. It makes P2-05's
rules (`services/release/resolve.ts:88-133`) exact, so that every language gets the same answer
with no floating point. Equality checks on versions (`staged`, `skipVersion`) stay exact string
comparisons. Each grammar below matches the whole string, with ASCII classes (§2.2 "Patterns,
lengths and presence"), so `1.2.3\n` does not parse (version cases 24 and 25).

- **`semver`.** A version parses if it matches SemVer 2.0's own grammar, written with ASCII
  classes:
  `^(0|[1-9][0-9]*)\.(0|[1-9][0-9]*)\.(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(?:-((?:0|[1-9][0-9]*|[0-9]*[A-Za-z-][0-9A-Za-z-]*)(?:\.(?:0|[1-9][0-9]*|[0-9]*[A-Za-z-][0-9A-Za-z-]*))*))?(?:\+([0-9A-Za-z-]+(?:\.[0-9A-Za-z-]+)*))?$`.
  It is stricter than P2-04's `SEMVER_RE` in two ways, both SemVer 2.0 §9's: no identifier is
  empty (`1.0.0-a..b`, `1.0.0-a.`, `1.2.3+`), and a numeric prerelease identifier has no leading
  zero (`1.0.0-01`). Under P2-04's pattern, the empty identifier in `1.0.0-a..b` is non-numeric
  to JavaScript's `/^[0-9]+$/` and numeric to Swift's `allSatisfy(\.isASCIIDigit)`, so two SDKs
  would order `1.0.0-a..b` and `1.0.0-a.0.b` differently. A version P2-04 accepts and this
  grammar refuses is refused when its record is published (`release_record_rejected {scheme}`,
  §6). Precedence is SemVer 2.0 §11:
  - major, minor and patch compare as unbounded integers;
  - a version with a prerelease is lower than the same version without one;
  - prerelease identifiers, split on `.`, compare left to right. An identifier of ASCII digits
    only is numeric and compares as an unbounded integer; a numeric identifier is lower than a
    non-numeric one; non-numeric identifiers compare by ASCII order; a shorter list that is a
    prefix of a longer one is lower;
  - build metadata is ignored.
- **`semver+build`.** It parses as `semver`. When precedence ties, build metadata that is
  `+` followed by ASCII digits only compares as an unbounded integer, and a version without such
  metadata is lower than any version with it (P2-05's `-1`).
- **`4part`.** A version parses if it matches `^(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(\.(0|[1-9][0-9]*)){3}$`, P2-04's
  `FOUR_PART_RE`. The four parts compare left to right as unbounded integers.
- "Unbounded integer" means comparing the digit strings after removing leading zeros, first by
  length and then by ASCII order. If either version does not parse, `compareVersions` answers
  null.

**Bucket.** `bucket = u32_be(SHA-256(UTF-8(salt) ‖ UTF-8(installId))[0..4]) mod 10000`:

- `salt` is the feed's 32-character hex string, hashed as text;
- `installId` is the SDK's device id, the value it sends as `X-PKey-Device`;
- there is no separator;
- the first four digest bytes are read big-endian as an unsigned integer;
- the device is eligible iff `bucket` is not null and `bucket < bp`.

**Eligible builds.** A build of `record` is eligible for self-installation when:

- it has exactly one `payload` artifact;
- its `platform` equals `installed.platform`;
- its arch is the device's, `universal` or `any`.

A **code-pack build** is an eligible build with `format: "pck"`, a string `requires.engine`
equal to `installed.engine` (both non-null), and a `requires.minBinary` that is absent, or that
parses and has `cmp(binaryVersion, minBinary) ≥ 0`. An **eligible binary build** is an eligible
build whose format is not `pck`, and equals `installed.format` when that is known. Among several,
the device's own arch is preferred to `universal`, and `universal` to `any`; ties go to the
build id in ascending order.

**Algorithm.** The first rule that applies decides.

1. **Stale.** If `now ≥ feed.expiresAt + 300`, the answer is `none {reason: "stale"}` and the
   staged update is kept.
2. **Unknown version.** If `installed.version` or `binaryVersion` does not parse under the
   feed's scheme, the answer is `none {reason: "unknown-version"}`.
3. **Setup.**
   - `target` is the feed's target for `installed.platform`, if there is one.
   - `entry` is `target.outlets[outlet.id]` when `outlet.id` is not null and that entry's `kind`
     equals `outlet.kind`. Otherwise it is the one entry of `target.outlets` whose `kind`
     equals `outlet.kind`, if exactly one has it. Otherwise there is no entry. `unknown` never
     has an entry.
   - `caps` is
     `effectiveCapabilities(outlet.kind, {platform: installed.platform, subkind, server: entry?.capabilities})`
     (§2.9).
   - `belowFloor` means `target.floor` exists and
     `cmp(binaryVersion, target.floor.minVersion) < 0`. The floor is this platform's own (§2.3)
     and is judged against the binary (A4 P5, P6). With no target there is no floor.
4. **The offer.**
   - When `caps.binaryUpdates` is `self`, the offer is `target.release` if `entry.live` is not
     null, `entry.live.seq === target.release.seq` and `record` is not null. Otherwise there is
     no offer: `entry.live` is never offered to a self-updating outlet.
   - When `caps.binaryUpdates` is `store` or `none`, the offer is `entry.live`, which may be
     null.
   - With no entry there is no offer. If there is no offer, the answer is
     `blocked {reason: "app-floor"}` when `belowFloor`, else `none {reason: "not-available"}`.
5. **Behind.** If `cmp(offer.version, installed.version) < 0`, the answer is
   `none {reason: "behind", behind: true}`. The floor is suppressed (A4 P1). There is no
   downgrade.
6. **Up to date.** Let `newerRun` be `cmp(offer.version, installed.version) > 0` and `newerBin`
   be `cmp(offer.version, binaryVersion) > 0`. If `!newerRun` and not (`belowFloor` and
   `newerBin`), the answer is `blocked {reason: "app-floor"}` when `belowFloor`, else
   `none {reason: "up-to-date"}`.
7. **Halted.** If `entry.halted`, the answer is `blocked {reason: "app-floor"}` when
   `belowFloor`, else `none {reason: "halted"}`.
8. **Rollout.** Applies when `entry.rollout` is present, the device is not below the floor and
   `target.critical` is false. Unless `bucket` is not null and `bucket < rollout.bp`, the answer
   is `none {reason: "out-of-bucket"}`; a null bucket is out. A critical release and a
   below-floor device bypass the rollout.
9. **Platform.** If `caps.binaryUpdates === "none"`, the answer is
   `platform {release: offer, mandatory: belowFloor}`.
10. **Store.** If `caps.binaryUpdates === "store"`, the answer is
    `store {release: offer, listingUrl: entry.listingUrl ?? null, mandatory: belowFloor}`.
11. **Self-updating outlets.** The answer is the first of these that applies:
    - a. **`code-ready`.** All of: not `belowFloor`, `newerRun`, `caps.codeUpdates`,
      `staged.channel === channel`, `staged.version === offer.version`, and
      `offer.version !== skipVersion`.
    - b. **`binary {method: "sidecar-pck"}`.** All of: not `belowFloor`, `newerRun`,
      `caps.codeUpdates`, `"sidecar-pck"` in `methods`, `offer.version !== skipVersion`, and a
      code-pack build exists.
    - c. **`binary {method: "native"}`, then `{method: "download"}`.** Requires `newerBin`, the
      method in `methods`, and an eligible binary build.
    - d. **Otherwise**, in this order:
      - `blocked {reason: "app-floor"}` when `belowFloor`;
      - `none {reason: "skipped"}` when `offer.version === skipVersion`;
      - `none {reason: "no-build"}` when no eligible binary build exists;
      - `none {reason: "no-method"}` otherwise.

On every answer except `stale`, `discardStaged` is `staged !== null && action !== "code-ready"`.
That covers A4 P11 (a channel switch drops staged code) and E5 §5.2 (a halt discards what was
staged).

**Outputs** (`UpdateDecision`). Each answer has exactly the members below, no others, and the
runners compare decisions by value. `release` is `{version, seq}`, plus `sha256` on `code-ready`
and `binary`; `critical` is `target.critical`; `discardStaged` is set as the paragraph above
says.

| `action`     | Members                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `none`       | `reason` (a `none` reason); `behind`, true only for `reason: "behind"`; `discardStaged`                                                    |
| `code-ready` | `release`, `critical`, `discardStaged` (always false)                                                                                      |
| `binary`     | `method` (a binary method); `release`; `build` (a build id); `mandatory`; `critical`; `prestage: []` (always empty in v4); `discardStaged` |
| `store`      | `release`, `listingUrl` (a string or null), `mandatory`, `critical`, `discardStaged`                                                       |
| `platform`   | `release`, `mandatory`, `critical`, `discardStaged`                                                                                        |
| `blocked`    | `reason` (a `blocked` reason), `discardStaged`                                                                                             |

**Vocabularies** (closed lists, in this order). The protocol constants, the `enums.json` enums
and the matrices' `vocabulary` keys hold the same lists, and every runner asserts them equal. A
reserved value is a name a later package must use, not a member of any v4 list: that package
adds it to the constant, the enum, the vocabulary and the `UpdateDecision` type together, in its

**The iOS narrowing** (`PLATFORM_NARROWING`, also `outlet-matrix.json#/platformNarrowing`). On
`ios`, `direct` narrows to `binaryUpdates: store`, `codeUpdates: false` and
`downloadedScripts: false`:

- an iOS `direct` install is Web Distribution (`AppDistributor.web` maps to `direct`, below).
  Apple's notarization guidelines for apps distributed outside the App Store say they "cannot
  download executable code" (notes/S-07 row 13, a done spike marked "Affects … P3-01 outlet
  capabilities"), so sidecar code packs and downloaded scripts are off. Data-only packs stay
  allowed, as S-07 row 13 says;
- an iOS app cannot install an ipa itself, which is why the parity registry already marks
  `update.driver` N/A on iOS (`outlet`). `store` here means the SDK opens the developer's
  web-distribution page, which the host compiles in, as it does for an AltStore source: `direct`
  has no listing prefixes, so the feed carries no `listingUrl` for it.

The defaults stay per kind, so P2b-02's Worker table needs no platform column: the client applies
the narrowing. Capability case 10 and update-matrix row 52 pin it.

- **What the fields mean.**
  - `binaryUpdates` says who installs a new build:
    - `self`: the SDK downloads it or hands it to a native updater;
    - `store`: the SDK opens a store, a store app or a distribution page that installs it;
    - `none`: the platform installs it without the app (Steam, Flathub, App Installer, and a
      reload on web).
  - `codeUpdates` gates `sidecar-pck` and `code-ready`; `dataUpdates` and `downloadedScripts`
    gate P4's packs.
  - `channelSwitch` gates the dev-menu picker; `commerce` is for P6.
- **Narrowing** (`effectiveCapabilities(kind, {platform, subkind?, server?})`). The defaults for
  the kind are narrowed first by the platform (`PLATFORM_NARROWING[platform][kind]`), then by
  the subkind (`SUBKIND_NARROWING`), then by the feed entry's `capabilities`:
  - booleans are `default && narrowing`;
  - `binaryUpdates` takes the narrower value in the order `none` < `store` < `self`;
  - `commerce` becomes `none` only when a narrowing says `none`; any other value is ignored;
  - nothing widens.
- **Subkinds** (S-06 notes b and c):
  - `homebrew`, `npm`, `pnpm`, `npx`, `scoop`, `chocolatey` and `flatpak` narrow `direct` to
    `binaryUpdates: none` and `codeUpdates: false`, because the package manager or Flatpak updates
    the install;
  - `appimage` narrows nothing, because AppImageUpdate is a native method.

**Detection.** Detection works on kinds. This plan adopts notes/S-06's eight precedence rules
and its signal table, with these decisions on S-06's open points:

- `AppDistributor.web` maps to `direct`, narrowed on iOS as above. A separate `ios-web` kind
  (S-06 note a's alternative) would add an eighteenth kind to README §3.1, P2b-02's enum and
  `.pkey/distribution` for one platform's difference;
- the five rows backed only by documentation (`ios.appDistributor`, and the
  `windows.packageIdentity`, `windows.signatureKind`, `windows.appInstallerUri` and
  `windows.externalLocation` rows) stay `attested`. Lowering them would make every stamp-less
  App Store install `unknown`;
- Android `play` stays `declared` until P5-06 records the Play Store's certificate digest in
  `platformData`;
- the installer–initiator mismatch is its own signal, `android.installerMismatch`, as S-06 has
  it. So the veto never disappears with `android.installSource`, whose identity condition is the
  opposite fact;
- a `subkind` is part of the result, with the narrowing above;
- web runtimes (the React browser adapter, Godot web exports) synthesise
  `stamp.outletKind = "web"`.

The algorithm, made deterministic for
`detectOutlet({stamp, signals}) → {kind, confidence, source, subkind}`. §4.7's signal table
gives each signal its effect: it **names** a kind (with a subkind), **vetoes** kinds, **gates**
other rows (`windows.packageIdentity` only), or is **diagnostic** (`steam.appIdFile`,
`itch.appEnv`). Some signals name or veto depending on their
value (`ios.appDistributor`, `macos.signingLeaf`, `windows.signatureKind`, `linux.snapEnv`).

1. **Filter.** Drop every signal whose identity condition (§4.7's column) does not hold, and the
   two diagnostic signals. A signal whose identity condition is "—" always counts.
2. **Attested naming.** Collect the kinds that attested signals name. The macOS receipt and
   `receiptSandbox` together name one kind; `linux.flatpakInfo` names `direct` + `flatpak` when
   the stamp is `direct` + `flatpak`, and `flathub` otherwise.
   - Two different kinds give `unknown`.
   - One kind gives that kind with confidence `attested`, the first naming attested signal in
     vocabulary order as `source`, and that signal's subkind. Then step 4 runs, and detection
     stops: step 5 never moves attested evidence.
3. **Stamp.** Otherwise, with a stamp, the result is the stamp's `outletKind` and subkind, with
   confidence `stamp` and source `"stamp"`. With no stamp it is `unknown`: declared and
   heuristic evidence never selects a kind on its own (S-06 rule 7).
4. **Vetoes.** If a surviving signal vetoes the current kind, the result is `unknown`.
5. **Restricting signals** (the stamp path only). Take the declared naming signals, then the
   heuristic ones, each group in vocabulary order. The first whose kind, narrowed by its subkind,
   has a `binaryUpdates` no wider than the current kind's, narrowed by the current subkind,
   decides, and detection stops. The result takes that signal's kind, confidence and name (as
   `source`), and its subkind, or the current subkind when it names the current kind without
   one. If no signal qualifies, the stamp's result stands.

`unknown` is always `{kind: "unknown", confidence: null, source: null, subkind: null}`. So
`source` names the one signal that decided, or `"stamp"`, or nothing. When several attested
signals agree, the first in vocabulary order is the source (rows 5 and 6), and a gate never is
(rows 38 and 40). A signal that confirms the stamp's own kind becomes the source and raises the
confidence (rows 11, 21, 22, 25, 30, 46). The SDK passes the result to `resolveUpdateOutlet`
as `detected` (§2.8), and a host value always wins. Async platform calls have a 2 s deadline,
and a timeout is no evidence.
Detection runs at every launch and is never cached. Raw signal values never leave the device.

### 2.10 Boot confirmation (`stage-matrix.json` version 2) and telemetry names

P1-09 left the confirmation criterion to this plan (its decision 5 and risk 1). **A launch is
confirmed** the first time one of these holds. A confirmed launch resets `failedBoots` to 0,
under P1-09's counting rule (§2.3 there).

- The machine's outcome is `waiting`, `blocked` or `offline`. The app is running and waiting
  for the player, so quitting at the activation screen can never roll back a good update.
- The outcome has been `ready` for `BOOT_OK_SECONDS` (10, Diceroll's value) with the process
  alive.
- The game calls `confirmBoot()` while the outcome is `ready`.

`running` and `error` never confirm, so a launch that ends in `error` (for example, a pack that
does not mount) counts as failed.

The pure part is `bootConfirmation(outcome) → "now" | "after-ok-seconds" | "never"`. The timer
and `confirmBoot()` are host-side (P3-10). The stage machine itself is unchanged: no new event,
no new emit, and every existing row, probe and guard case stays byte-identical. The vocabulary
gains `confirmations`, so the version goes to 2 (§4.8).

**Telemetry names** (README §3.6 and P6-03). These travel on the unsigned `devices/report`, so
they are not a wire change. They are fixed as the `updateEvent` enum in
`conformance/parity/enums.json`: `update_offered`, `update_downloaded`, `update_applied`,
`update_confirmed`, `update_reverted`, `pack_failed`, `boot_rolled_back`. The shapes and the
allowlist stay with P6-03, following P1-05's precedent (Worker allowlist and OpenAPI schema
only, with `shared-protocol` untouched).

**The delta rule.** A delta changes only what it names, in the order written. A delta that starts
with `W` starts from `W` instead of `I`, and `as N` starts from row N's delta.

- `installed X` sets `installed.version` and `installed.binaryVersion` to X;

  After `installed X`, a `binary Y` still applies, so `installed 1.6.0, binary 1.3.0` means
  version 1.6.0 and binary 1.3.0;
- a platform with an arch (`linux x86_64`, `android arm64`) sets `installed.platform` and
  `installed.arch`; a platform named alone (`ios`, `macos`) keeps the base's arch, `arm64`;
  `format f` sets `installed.format`;
- `` `x` `` or `outlet x` sets `outlet` to `{id: "x", kind: "x"}`, and `{id, kind}` sets it as
  written; `subkind s`, `methods […]` (`W`'s methods are `[sidecar-pck, download]`),
  `staged {version, channel}`, `skip v`, `bucket n`, `now t`, `engine e`, `channel c` and
  `record null` set those inputs;
- `<Platform> floor V` sets that platform's target `floor` to `{minVersion: V}`;
- `` `direct` halted ``, `rollout {bp, salt}` (or `rollout {bp}`; the salt is always
  `00112233445566778899aabbccddeeff`), a `capabilities` object and `target critical` change the

A runner also asserts its compiled `LISTING_URL_PREFIXES` against

**Evidence (`verified`).** S-06 labels every result `measured` (a real install, launcher or
client produced the value), `emulated` (the value was placed by hand where the real producer
writes it, which proves the reader, not the producer), `source` (read from the producer's source
or reference documentation) or `unmeasured`. `verified` takes S-06's label for the signal, and
these rules settle S-06's compound statuses:

- a combination takes its strongest label, in the order `measured`, `emulated`, `source`,
  `unmeasured`: `source + emulated` is `emulated`, and `source; the device run is unmeasured` is
  `source`;
- a qualified label keeps the label and moves the qualification to `note`: `measured (1 app)`,
  `measured (macOS)`, `measured (realpath)` and `measured (all but Play)` are `measured`;
- `refuted` is `measured`, with a note: the measurement refuted the dev-mode reading, which is why
  `steam.appIdFile` is diagnostic only;
- S-06's `linux.flatpakForeign`, `android.packageSource` and `android.updateOwner` rows are parts
  of `linux.flatpakInfo` and `android.installSource` here, all `measured`.

No SDK branches on `verified`. It tells P3-11 which readers a device run must confirm before it
relies on them, and a device run that changes a label is a plan-mode corpus change. The 25
signals are 13 `measured`, 3 `emulated`, 7 `source` and 2 `unmeasured`.

The Android package names beyond `com.android.vending`, `dev.imranr.obtainium` and
`org.fdroid.fdroid` are [I]: P3-11 verifies them before relying on them.

- **Self-checks** (each one throws, so `gen:corpus` fails):

  - every feed pin equals the SHA-256 of the record vector it names, except in the cases built

  checks that keep them apart from product keys (§2.4), and the strict verifier; P3-12 adds the
  signer guard and the write checks;
- AT-3 gains a branch for the two-signer model, beside P2-02's trusted-publisher branch. A
  compromised Worker or KEK **can** withhold (serve no target), delay (stop re-signing, so
  installs freeze at `expiresAt`), re-target among CI-signed releases newer than the installed
  version, halt or re-bucket a rollout, narrow capabilities, lower a floor, and raise a floor.
  Raising one puts a prompt the player cannot dismiss in front of every install of that platform
  below it, including installs whose outlet has nothing newer (`blocked app-floor`), but play
  continues: no v4 answer maps to `required` (§2.8). It can also point a store prompt at another
  listing on the same store: the prefixes fix the store, not the app. It can **fast-forward**
  `seq` to `MAX_WIRE_INTEGER`: installs that fetch that feed refuse the recovered Worker's lower
  `seq` and freeze once it is stale, until the operator runs the `seq` ceiling script, after
  which the recovered Worker signs at the ceiling and step 8 accepts its newer `issuedAt`
  (§2.3). The freeze therefore ends with the recovery, not with a client release. Through the
  feed it **cannot** ship bytes no release key signed, downgrade, widen a capability, send a
  prompt outside §2.9's prefixes, or stop an install from running. The licence documents it also
  signs are AT-1's subject, and the compatibility window they carry stays the one tool that
  blocks.

   `protocolVersion`, so a Worker built after it deploys as before.
   - **Deployed v3 clients.** No SDK enforces `protocolVersion`: Node and Swift read it from
     discovery, and nothing compares it. The licence, config, trust and bundle documents are
     byte-identical, so deployed clients are unaffected.
   - **New SDK releases** carry the stricter verifier, with the number and depth rules and the
     integer claims decided from the token. Everything the Worker signs passes it once P3-12 is
     deployed: `JSON.stringify` writes a safe integer as plain digits, the corpus and transcripts
     hold no number with a fraction or exponent and no more than 4 levels (§2.2), and the write
     checks and the two signer guards keep it so. So no SDK release built after P3-02 is
     published before P3-12's Worker is deployed with a clean check. In the recommended
     schedule P3-12 is due by week 6.25 and the first wave package, P3-05, by week 6.5, so the
     rule costs nothing planned. A Swift release accepts the member shapes the other SDKs always
     accepted, and a Python or Swift release refuses `profile: null` and a `null` in a bundle's
     `docs`, which the Worker never writes.
2. **P3-12 merges and deploys,** beside P3-03 and P3-05.
   - **Before deploying that Worker,** an operator runs
     `pnpm --filter @polaris-key/worker check:representable` against production D1 and fixes
     every value it flags, in the 8 JSON, 12 text and 1 number columns of §2.2; its warnings
     (fractional or out-of-range offline days) do not block the deploy.
   - **Deploying it** adds §2.2's checks: a value a v4 verifier would refuse is refused at
     write (`422 value_not_representable` or `422 bad_request`, or the manifest validator's
     `value_not_representable`), dropped at OIDC sign-in, dropped from a config document by the
     catalog prune, or answered with `500 document_not_representable` instead of being signed.
     That last answer reaches v3 clients too, so the check is not optional. Console writes that
     the manifest validator would refuse (a tier id, channel or version outside its pattern, a
     product signing kid outside `KID_RE`, a fractional or out-of-range offline-day count) now
     answer `422`.
   - **What changes in documents.** With a clean check and no warnings, no product's documents
     change, with one exception the check cannot see: a sealed secret whose plaintext the rules
     flag is pruned from the config document, because the check cannot open sealed values. A
     warning's fractional day count changes `graceUntil` by under a second (the builders floor).
   - **Capabilities narrow.** The `outletCapabilities` hook answers §2.9's table, so `web`,
     `steam`, `app-installer`, `itch`, `flathub`, `snap` and `winget` outlets narrow from
     P2b-02's proposed values. Before P3-03's composer, only the distribution admin API shows
     them.
3. **P3-03 deploys** the routes, the tables and the record ingest.
   - **Older Workers** have no `endpoints.feed`. A v4 SDK refuses with `service-unavailable`,
     and the host keeps using `check()`.
   - **A rollback** to a pre-P3-03 Worker has the same effect. The new tables just sit unused.
   - **Recovery tooling.** The `feed:seq-ceiling` script and its RUNBOOK section ship with the
     feed itself; before P3-03 there is no feed to fast-forward.
   - **What the feed carries.** A product with no `releaseKeys`, or a release published before
     v4, has no record, so the feed carries no target for it. Installs get signed updates from
     the first CI-signed release onwards.
   - **Ordering moves to `compareVersions`.** Resolution changes only where SemVer 2.0 and
     today's `compareSemver` disagree (mixed prerelease identifiers, integers beyond 2^53, and
     the empty or leading-zero identifiers that SemVer 2.0's grammar refuses, which make a
     channel fall back to `seq` order as any unparseable mix does under P2-05), and for `4part`
     and `semver+build` products, which start being ordered by their scheme.
   - **Floors start to count, without blocking.** P2-05 stores `min_supported` and enforces
     nothing, so no install was ever blocked by it. From P3-03 a floor reaches devices as a
     prompt they cannot dismiss, per platform; play continues (§2.8).
   - **Untouched surfaces.** `/update/version`, the appcasts and `/release/dl` keep their
     behaviour.
4. **The SDK wave:** P3-05, beside P3-03 (which also waits for P2b-03, P2b-04 and P2-06) and
   P3-12; then P3-04, P3-06, P3-07 and P3-08; then P3-11. This order is not free: on the
   recommended graph P3-03 finishes by week 8 and D-03 and D-04 by week 17, against weeks 5.75
   and 14.75 on `main`, and the critical path grows from 18.75 to 21 weeks (§8 decision 3).
   - Cache records stay version 3. A downgraded SDK ignores the new slices; a v4 SDK reading an
     old record has no floor yet, so it accepts the first fresh feed.
5. **Then P3-09, P3-10, and Diceroll's D-03.**

There are no feature flags. The feed answers only where Update, and so Distribution, is enabled.
In the corpus lane, P3-02 and P1b-04 must not be in flight together: whichever lands later
rebases, re-runs `pnpm gen:corpus` and commits the result.

## 8. Decisions, risks and brief changes

**Decisions the lead has approved.** The two choices that change behaviour a reader could expect
otherwise were raised as questions with recommendations, and the lead approved both on
2026-10-01:

1. **A floor never stops play in v4.** Mandatory offers and `blocked {app-floor}` give
   `optional` with a prompt the player cannot dismiss, and no v4 answer gives `required` (§2.8).
   That keeps A4 §5 P6, which the brief carries: Diceroll's floor "prompts non-dismissibly and
   never blocks play". Mapping them to `required`, which P1-09's machine turns into
   `blocked {update-required}`, would let a compromised Worker, a mistaken `min_supported` or a
   stray `--min-supported-seq` stop every below-floor install of a platform from launching, a
   stronger lever than withholding. An operator who needs a hard stop keeps License's
   compatibility window. `required` stays in the boot vocabulary, unused in v4.
2. **The outlet matrix stays in this plan.** Every part of it gates the same P3-02, and the
   generator recomputes every detection row, so the matrix cannot drift from §2.9.

**Risks.**

1. **The verifier fixes touch the v3 path in four languages,** the token-level integer rule, the
   number rules and Swift's total decoders included. Mitigation: the transcripts and the corpus
   already hold real Worker-signed documents, and all of them must still verify; a scan found
   none that breaks the new rules.
2. **The D1 check is a human step.** It reads production D1 before P3-12's Worker is first
   deployed. Skipped, a stored value that trips the new rules becomes a pruned config value or a
   `500` on that product's licence documents, for v3 clients too (§7).
3. **The Play Store's digest and AltStore PAL's marketplace id are unknown.** Until P5-06 and
   P5-05 record them, Play is `declared` and a marketplace install vetoes only. Adding either is
   a plan-mode corpus change. The same holds for the listing prefixes, which are [I] until P3-10.
4. **The feed split** is exercised only by `feed-valid-platform-selector` and P3-03's synthetic
   test until P4's pack sets make feeds large.
5. **Windows signals are documentation only** (S-06). They stay attested, and a device run that
   contradicts them is a plan-mode amendment.
6. **The scheme bug is live on `main`** (§6). Until P3-03 lands, P2-05 orders `4part` products
   by `seq` alone and refuses their `min_supported`. It could be fixed sooner as its own
   change.
7. **The corpus lane.** P3-02 is the largest corpus change since v3. Its own Node sections
   prove the version and claim cases, and P3-05 proves the rest before the other SDKs start, so
   a corpus mistake surfaces once.
8. **Godot reads some numbers inexactly.** A fractional config value can differ in its last
   bits in Godot, and an exotic spelling can read as 0; every verdict still agrees (V4 §10). A
   number value that must be exact belongs in a plain integer token or a string; an integer
   claim is always exact, because its token is checked.
9. **Five packages own the update replayers.** Whichever of P3-03 and a wave package lands
   second fixes the other's replayer mismatch in its own PR (§5).
10. **Floors only prompt.** An operator who expects a floor to lock old builds out has to use
    License's compatibility window; P3-03's operator docs say so.
11. **Admin writes get stricter.** Console and API writes that the manifest validator would refuse
    now answer `422`. A stored value that only fails a pattern (a channel `Beta Channel`) still
    signs, so the D1 check does not list it, and it is refused the next time a write touches it.
12. **A fast-forward freezes installs until the operator acts.** The recovery is a RUNBOOK step;
    until it runs, affected installs answer `none {stale}` and keep running. Nothing in the
    client can tell a fast-forward from a legitimate jump, so the RUNBOOK runs the script after
    any suspected compromise.
13. **The manifest still accepts 0 and more than 365 offline days.** `defaultMaxOfflineDays` is a
    non-negative integer to the validator, and either edge gives a `graceUntil` every client
    already refuses (below `expiresAt`, or past the 365-day ceiling). That predates v4 and changes
    no verdict, so it is a follow-up rule-9 change; the admin paths refuse it from P3-12 on.
14. **The schedule** (decision 3). Even as recommended, the critical path grows by 2.25 weeks,
    and D-03 and D-04 move from week 14.75 to week 17. A further overrun in P3-02 or P3-03
    moves them one for one; one in P3-04 to P3-08 does not until it passes 1.25 weeks.
15. **Two packages share the Worker side of v4.** P3-12 and P3-03 both touch `core/signing.ts`
    callers and the threat model. They are in the same worker lane and rebase on each other;
    the composer's own `scanStrictJson` keeps the feed safe whichever lands first.
