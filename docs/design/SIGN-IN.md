# Polaris Key sign-in: the canonical experience

**Status:** canonical. **Scope:** every way a person signs in to Polaris Key, or to an app through
Polaris Key. That covers the hosted login card (portal and console), the Worker's no-JS pages, the
sign-in emails, and the sign-in screens of every UI kit. The owner's decisions are §0 and the
lead's delegated choices are §7. **Mockups:** [sign-in/](sign-in/): frames 01–22 and 48 at desktop
and phone, frames 23–46 as desktop-only scenes (app windows, the desktop browser and terminals) and
frame 47 at phone width, each in dark and light, rendered by `sign-in/render.cjs`, plus an animated,
clickable prototype of the integrated web flow in [sign-in/prototype/](sign-in/prototype/) (§9).

> **This document is the single source of truth for sign-in.** [PORTAL.md](PORTAL.md) §3.3–§3.4
> and §4.1–§4.11, §4.18–§4.19, §4.23–§4.25 and §4.29; [EXPERIENCE.md](EXPERIENCE.md) §0.6 P1, §8 and
> §11.1; [ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md) §2.7 (Sign in row), T8 (Session expired) and §6.10.1; and
> [UI-KITS.md](UI-KITS.md) §4.3 (Welcome and Activate, SignIn and SignInHandoff, DeviceLimit) point
> here. Measures, colours and type stay [BRAND.md](BRAND.md)'s and EXPERIENCE.md §8's. The wire is
> the approved plans' in `docs/research/2026-09-29-godot-omniplatform/program/plans/`:
> `plans/I-27.md` §2.1 for the authorization server (`authorize`, `token`, `request`), and
> `plans/I-04.md` §A–§G, as `plans/I-27.md` amends them, for license choice, Replace a device and
> the one sign-in form (§6). A change no approved plan carries is a plan-mode change (CLAUDE.md).

**Owner request (verbatim, 2026-10-05):** "Let's ensure all our UX systems are aligned on what this
will all look like as we're making several UX changes around the login flow."

**Naming.** The product is **Polaris Key**, never "Polaris Key Portal", "the portal" or "PK" in UI
copy or email. UI copy uses **license** (US spelling; glossary, AGENTS.md rule 4), so the owner's
"Choose a licence for this device" reads **"Choose a license for this device"** (D-01).
Banned UI words: claim, redeem, merge, OIDC, deauthorize, activation (for an entry), portal.

## 1. Principles

1. **Authenticate once, then decide what this installation runs on.** Sign-in proves who the
   person is. Binding an installation to a license is a separate choice the person makes. It is
   never a silent side effect (O-1, O-3).
2. **One card, one step, one primary.** Every browser step renders inside the same `AuthCard`
   (EXPERIENCE §8 measures), and every in-app step inside the kit's one form (§3.17). Each step has
   exactly one primary button. Each step replaces the last in place, with no page hop and no modal
   layer (O-13, O-16). The header and footer persist across the steps of one flow.
3. **Credentials are entered only in Polaris Key's card or the platform's own sheet.** An app never
   hosts an email code, a passkey or a provider password. Kits render natively only what carries no
   credential: the form's sign-in step, the hand-off and the code view, the license choice and
   Replace a device (through the grant, I-04 §G), key entry and the device limit.
4. **Same names, same strings, everywhere.** A step has one component name and one set of copy keys
   (§5.2) across the web card, the Worker pages and every kit.
5. **No enumeration, no dead ends.** Unknown and known emails get the same response. Every refusal
   names the next step. A failure never shows an HTTP status or an internal code.
6. **The product is the hero of an app's sign-in.** The app header (a 56 px icon and the app's
   name) is the most prominent thing above the step. Polaris Key's lockup shrinks to a quiet brand
   row, the trusted frame rather than the subject. In kits the product owns the screen.
7. **Nothing changes until the last primary.** The license choice, a Replace and consent are
   recorded on the flow and applied together when the person presses the flow's final primary.
8. **Desktop is not a big phone.** A desktop app signs in in the person's default browser, waits
   in its own form, in place, with a way out (Cancel, Open browser again, Use a code instead), ends
   the tab with "You can close this tab", and comes back to the front by itself (§3.17). Desktop
   sizes, controls and button order are the platform's (UI-KITS §2.1).
9. **Motion explains the flow.** Steps morph; what stays, stays still; lists arrive in order; the
   success moment is brief. Under reduced motion every step swaps instantly (§3.18).

### 2.2 What a sign-in flow carries

One server-held flow record per sign-in (I-07's flow, PX-W13's request handle for passthrough). It
holds the context, `returnTo`, the app request handle and binder cookie (`__Host-pk_req`), the
device label and user code, the email-gate state (G31), and, after LicenseChoiceStep, the **chosen
binding** (§6.2). Display data never comes from a query parameter (PX-W13 §2.2).

### 2.3 Bindings: what "associate the installation" means

An installation is a `devices` row (`X-PKey-Device`). Signing in through Identity sets
`devices.subject`. That makes the account the device's entitlement holder (S-19 decision 4). It also
binds the device to one **anchor** license (`bound_by = 'signin'`). LicenseChoiceStep is where that
anchor is chosen. Nothing else binds a signed-in device:

- key entry binds to the entered license (`bound_by = 'key'`, D24 unchanged); in a passthrough the
  KeyStep confirm is itself the choice (I-04 delegated decision 10);
- attach adds the device's license to the account and never re-anchors (I-09 Q2). After **Keep**,
  the device still offers it (P1-07 `confirm.attachable`, I-04 delegated decision 4);
- sign-out releases a sign-in-bound seat (I-09 §2.2, I-04 Q3).

### 2.4 Two ways to integrate

The owner asked for two complete, equal paths over one contract (O-15). Every step and state of
§3 exists on both paths, with the same copy keys (§5.2) and the same server answers
(`plans/I-04.md` §A–§G).

- **Path A, hosted.** The card at `key.plrs.im` runs the whole flow in one continuous card:
  sign-in, the license key, the license choice, Replace a device, consent and the return (§4.16).
  An app needs no UI code. It opens the `authorize` URL (for a desktop or mobile app, built from the pushed
  request's `request_uri`; for a web app, the redirect) or shows a device code, and receives an activation. The kits'
  `presentation: "browser"` and the terminals use this path.
- **Path B, native.** The app runs the flow in its own window. The card still authenticates (D17:
  credentials only on the card or a platform sheet) and asks consent when it is due. Everything
  after that comes back to the app through a pending sign-in grant (`licenseChoice: "app"`, I-04
  §G). Path B comes in two layers: the kit's drop-in **one sign-in form** (§3.17; `presentation:
"inline"` by default, `"sheet"` on request), and the **headless primitives** for an app that
  draws its own UI.

**Mixing is per step** (I-04 delegated decision 33):

- a native sign-in with the choice on the card (`exchange({…, licenseChoice: "card"})` answers
  `interstitial_required` at the choice step);
- an in-app choice whose **Replace a device…** opens the card (`replace: "browser"` on the kit, or
  the row's `freeDeviceUrl` in a headless app, re-reading `choice.licenses` when the app is active
  again);
- the browser presentation for the whole choice (`presentation: "browser"`), with the form showing
  only steps 1, 2 and Done.

**What stays on the card on both paths:** credentials (D17), consent (D-83), device approval, and
the license choice of every device-code sign-in (D-82).

## 3. The step model

### 3.1 The card

`ui/auth/AuthCard` (UX-40 promotes the built `LoginCard`). The measures are EXPERIENCE §8's: a
28.5 rem card, radius 22 px, elevation 3, 48–52 px controls, a 24 px h1, top-aligned at 10 vh on the
static star field, and edge to edge under a 56 px lockup row on phones. Help · Privacy · Terms sit
below the card. **No "Polaris Key · key.plrs.im" line.** The star field stays. Nothing draws a
hairline or tint under the brand row on phones.

**The primary is neutral ink** in every context (portal, app sign-in, Worker pages): near-white
fill on dark, near-black on light, label in the page colour (`action-neutral`; disabled is the same
shape at 40 % contrast with its reason line). Violet stays on the focus ring, the selected radio
and row border, links and the brand row mark. Danger stays red.

**Provider buttons** take the host theme's scheme, height (44 px or more) and radius, and a fill
from the provider's allowed set for that scheme (PORTAL §4.1); never a lavender or accent tint.

**Layout: the product passport.** When the card has an App request or Product context header and
the viewport is landscape (width of at least 60 rem / 960 CSS px and a width to height ratio of at
least 1.2), the header leaves the top of the card and becomes a start pane 17.5 rem (280 px) wide
beside the form pane, which keeps every measure above (28.5 rem, 48 to 52 px controls, a 24 px h1);
the card is about 46 rem wide. Portrait, tablet portrait (834 px) and phones keep the stacked header
strip: never two narrow panes in portrait (UI-KITS DL1). The pane is a neutral `surface-sunken` in
both themes, never a violet wash and never a dark pane in the light theme, with `text-strong` and
`text-muted` text. Top to bottom, top-aligned with the form's h1, each fact once:

1. Optional product art from Core Presentation (HA-12), an `<img>` with `object-fit: cover`, 16:10,
   at most 40 % of the pane height, `alt=""`. No art: icon and text only, no generated fill.
2. The 56 px icon (radius 14).
3. "<App> wants you to sign in" (18/24, name semibold). No eyebrow.
4. The developer on its own line.
5. The device or origin glyph and its label on its own line, with no trailing separator.
6. The device-code panel, when present.

Product context (developer link, `identity_disabled`) shows the icon, "<Product> · <Developer>" and
"Your license, downloads and devices". The **trust receipt** ("Polaris Key signs you in for
<App>...") spans the full width of the form pane. The pane carries `view-transition-name: pk-head`
and never moves between steps (§3.18). The brand row stays the 18 px muted lockup; Help · Privacy ·
Terms stay under the card. On the Worker's no-JS pages (I-38) the art is a same-origin `<img>` (or the
image origin the Worker CSP allows through `cspImageOrigin()`, exactly as the app policy does),
with no inline style or script: the layout is classes and container queries in the hashed
stylesheet and the pane uses neutral tokens only.

**The key on-ramp header.** On KeyStep reached from "Have a license key?" before authentication,
and on its Done, the header uses the product-context form, "<Product> · <Developer>" with the
device line, not "wants you to sign in": the key works without an account. Every other app step
keeps the persistent "wants you to sign in", ReturnStep included.

The header persists on every step of one app sign-in, including ReturnStep and any Worker page that
carries the request handle (§3.13). Branding is data only: name, icon, developer, origin and device label, from the client record

### 3.5 FinishStep: a new account, EmailGateStep and profile import

A new account finishes in one step, **FinishStep** (I-27 §2.4; API I-33, screens PX-21). One finish
API serves both entries: **RegisterStep** (after an email code, §3.4) and **EmailGateStep** (the
first provider sign-in of an account). It asks, in this order, only what the account lacks:

1. **Email**, with a code unless the provider or connection vouches for the address (below).
2. **Screen name** (`accounts.display_name`), with a chip for each source that offered one. An
   account has one public name, and it is not unique; apps receive it as `name`.
3. **Picture**.
4. **Birth date**, only when a source offered one. It is stored only when the person accepts it
   here, can be changed or removed in the account profile, and never reaches an app, a consent
   item or any export but the person's own.
5. **Terms**: the product's (`identity.terms`, with its privacy notice linked and not accepted) and
   Polaris Key's (`identity.platformTerms`). The platform step shows only once the owner has
   published reviewed terms; the setting is unset until then.

**EmailGateStep** is step 1 for a provider sign-in (PORTAL §4.29, O-8):

- **Required, once per account.** No skip path. **Cancel sign-in** abandons the sign-in. No token,
  session or binding is issued before the gate passes.
- **Layout:** the "Signed in with <Provider> · <identity> · Done" strip; h1 "Confirm your email";
  lede (in an app: "One step before <App>. We send sign-in codes, receipts and security notices
  here."); **ProfileImport**; the email radio cards; Terms when the product requires them; the
  primary; the Polaris Key terms line.
- **Fast path:** a provider-verified address needs **no code**. That means any Apple address,
  private relay included, or a Google address with `email_verified: true` that is either
  `@gmail.com`/`@googlemail.com` or carries a signed `hd` claim equal to its domain (a Workspace
  account). The primary is **Continue** (in passthrough, **Continue to <App>**). Google's
  `email_verified` on any other address only says Google verified it once, so it gets a code like
  a typed address (lead decision under the owner's delegation, 2026-10-06; PX-W15).
- **Code path:** a typed or unverified address, or a Google address outside that rule, shows six
  cells, and the primary is **Verify and continue**.
- **Steam** has no email. The field starts empty, with the help "Steam doesn't share an email. Add
  one so you can get back in without Steam." The primary is **Send code** (frame 04).
- **Email already in another account** (known only after proof): **Join into one account** (PORTAL
  §4.11, proof of both in this session, 72 h undo) or **Use a different email**. Never joined
  silently and never by email match, with one exception: a platform connection on a DNS-verified
  domain links an address verified on exactly one account; the person is emailed and the link is
  audited (I-27 Q1).
- **ProfileImport:** Google gives name and picture. Apple gives the name on first consent only, and
  no picture ("Apple doesn't share a picture. Add one"). Steam gives persona and avatar. Game Center
  gives an alias. Imported values follow their source until the person chooses. Explicit choices
  stick. Pictures are copied, re-encoded and served from the same origin (G33).
- **After FinishStep**, a first platform sign-in shows the **Add another way to sign in** nudge (PORTAL
  §4.10) once. It shows again after 30 days only if the account still has a single method. In
  passthrough the nudge waits until the next portal visit; it never stands between a person and the
  app.

### 3.6 LicenseChoiceStep

**Entry condition.** An app sign-in (passthrough, kit native, or the I-26 legacy page, §4.14) on a
product with the License service on, whose completion will **bind or re-bind this installation**.
It shows on **every** such sign-in, even with one license. It is skipped only for silent
re-authentication of an installation already bound to this account on a usable license (token
refresh, an I-13 exchange after the grant exists, a device-code re-poll), after a KeyStep confirm
in the same flow, because the key's license is the choice (I-04 delegated decision 10), and when
the first automatic license is due (rule 4). Portal direct and console sign-ins never show it: no
installation is involved.

**Layout (frame 05).** The person row (avatar, name, email, **Not you?**, which ends this account's
part of the flow and returns to MethodsStep with the app header intact). h1 "Choose a license for
this device", lede "<Product> will use it on <device label>." (web: "…in this browser."). Then one
`radiogroup` "Licenses for <Product>", the primary **Use this license and continue**, and a quiet
row with **Use a license key instead** (when the product accepts keys) and **Cancel**, which
returns `access_denied` to the app on every surface (on device code it denies the code; D-51).

**Row anatomy** (O-11). Every license row has three lines:

1. the **title**: the license's own name when it has one, else the product name ("Tidewater
   Studio"), then the row's tag;
2. the **tier and devices**: the tier as a neutral pill ("Pro", "Standard"), then the device
   counter "{used} of {limit} devices", for every license. No license is labelled by type: every
   license is account-bound (owner decision, 2026-10-05; O-17);
3. the **meta**: "{origin} · {term}". The origin says in plain words how the license came to be:
   "From signing in" (issued at sign-in, `access: "account"`, §6.2), "Key ending 3WPLDA" (the key's
   last six characters, when kept; G7) else "Added with a key", "{store} key ending 3WPLDA" (or
   "{store} key") when the key came from a store purchase, "From {store}" for a store-bound
   license with no key, "From {developer}", "Free", "Gift" or "Included with {org}" (`LicenseChoice.origin`, `signin.choice.origin.*`). Only the store's name
   is shown, never an order id. The term is "Lifetime", "Until 12 Jun 2027", "Renews 3 Mar" or
   "Yearly, until 2 Feb 2027" (never "For life").

Every license with a device limit, sign-in licenses included, adds a seat meter under line 3, and a
full one is a **Full** row with **No free devices** and its Replace. **When the account holds a
sign-in license for the product, its key and seat licenses hide the counter and the meter** (D-54);
line 2 is the pill alone, and a full one keeps its **No free devices** tag. The sign-in row
keeps "{used} of {limit} devices" and its meter. Two rows with the same title are
told apart by the pill, origin and term.

1. **Candidates** are the account's `base` licenses for this product that pass `licenseUsable`,

2. **Preselection**, first match wins: (a) **On this device** or **Keep**; (b) the first free row in

   sign-in row takes its place in the same order; it is not preferred for its origin.
3. **One license:** the same step with one row preselected: a one-tap confirm (O-1).
4. **No usable license, the product auto-issues** (frame 08; D-95, I-27 §2.2): the card skips

   token response and the form goes to Done (§3.17). Frame 08 draws the same license as a row.
5. **Every license full** (frames 09, 19): a neutral callout on `surface-sunken` (UI-KITS DL6: a
   refusal the person can resolve has no warning colour or glyph), "Your licenses are on all their
   devices. Replace a device to use one here." (with a Create row: "…Replace a device, or create a
   new free license."). Nothing is preselected and there is no disabled primary: the rank-first
   full row's **Replace a device** is the filled primary and takes focus, other rows' Replace stay
   secondary, and picking Create restores **Use this license and continue** as the primary. No
   license is created without that pick (O-3). `warning` stays for real risk only: the unsynced

### 3.7 ReplaceDevice

A step of its own (frames 06, 37, 30; D-81), opened by **Replace a device** on a full row. The
license list morphs into that license's devices in place (§3.18), under the heading "Replace a
device" and the license's title, pill and counter; the header and the person row stay. Focus moves
to the heading. The confirm expands under the list and is the step's only primary. In a kit it is
the system confirmation where the platform expects one (D-80).

- **The list:** that license's seat-holding devices (authorized and not dormant), least recently
  used first. The least recent is preselected and tagged **Least recent**. **Active now** marks a
  device seen in the last 10 minutes, and **This browser** the browser's own web device. Each row
  has a form-factor glyph, the device label and "<platform> · last used <when>". This installation
  is never in the list.
- **One confirm that names the device.** It sits under the list and follows the selection. There is
  no second dialog (an L1 inline confirm, EXPERIENCE §7):
  - title "Replace <device>?";
  - "<device> signs out of <Product> and <this device> takes its seat. <device> can sign in again
    later if a seat is free. We'll email you about it.";
  - when the selected device is **Active now**, a `warning` line: "<device> is in use right now.";
  - **Replace and continue** (primary) and **Back**, which returns to the license list with focus
    on that row's **Replace a device**.
- **Two taps** from a full license: **Replace a device**, then **Replace and continue**. The least
  recent device is already chosen.
- **Nothing is written until the flow's final primary.** "Replace and continue" records
  `{license, replaceDeviceId}` on the flow. When Consent follows, its license line reads "<license>
  · replaces <device>" with **Change**, and the replacement happens on **Continue to <App>**.
  Otherwise "Replace and continue" is the final primary.
- **Effect** (I-04 §B): the server frees the device through `freeAccountDevice()`, the portal's
  Remove operation (account ownership, `portal_enabled`, the `portalDeviceDisconnect` budget
  shared with the portal, audit `portal.device.disconnect` with "to sign in <new label>", the
  device-removed email), then binds this installation into the freed seat with
  `bound_by = 'signin'`. The free and the bind commit in one batch where the claim can join it (I-04 §F.3).
- **Safety.** The fresh sign-in (under 5 minutes) is the step-up. In the device-code context the
  code panel stays on screen and the confirm names both devices, so a phished code cannot quietly
  trade one of the person's devices for the attacker's. A Replace never counts as a key entry (I-04
  decision 11).
- **Not offered** when the product's customer portal is off and on seat-holder licenses (I-24a:
  **Free a device** only). The I-26 legacy page offers it without JS (D-59). Sign-in licenses get it like any
  other: they keep a device limit and can be full (D-53). **Free a device** opens
  `#/p/<slug>/free-device?license=<id>&for=<this device label>&return=/signin?request=<handle>`
  (PX-10's focused flow), which returns to this step (I-04 decision 14).
- **Failures:** "That device was already removed. Choose again." (the list refreshes); "Too many
  device changes. Try again in {seconds} seconds." (`429 rate_limited` with `retryAfter`); "Someone
  took that seat first. Choose again." (a `device_limit` race with a fresh view). If the free had
  already committed: "<device> was removed, but someone took the seat first. Choose again."

**Device vocabulary** (D-08, D-49): **Replace a device** opens the device list in place (in a kit
set to `replace: "browser"` it opens the card). **Replace <device>?** with **Replace and continue** confirms it wherever the
action also seats this device: the card, the kit `LicenseChoice` and the kit `DeviceLimit`.
**Remove <device>** only frees a seat (on a sign-in license it also signs that device out of
the product) (the portal product page, FreeDevicePage). **Free a device**
is the link to FreeDevicePage from the card and the portal's quick actions. "Free up a device",
"Manage devices in Polaris Key" and "Disconnect" are retired. The console keeps "Deauthorize".

### 3.8 ConsentStep

PORTAL §4.8 Confirm, always as its own step **after** LicenseChoiceStep (frame 07; I-04 delegated
decision 3):

- Person row, h1 "Continue to <App> as <first name>?".
- **License for this device**: the chosen license ("Tidewater Studio Pro · Lifetime · this Mac
  becomes device 3 of 3", "Storytime Standard · Lifetime · this iPad becomes device 3 of 3", "…·
  replaces Work laptop" or "Keep the license this device uses"), with **Change**, which goes back
  to LicenseChoiceStep with the selection kept. The automatic license reads "New: Saltwind Free
  license, created when you continue" and has no **Change**: no choice precedes it (§3.6 rule 4).
- "<App> will also get": the items inside the requested scope (`identity.claims {required,
optional}` over name, picture, email and Cloud Sync; I-27 §2.4). Required items are plain lines.
  Each optional item is a toggle, on by default. **Cloud Sync** shows only when the product has it
  on, with what it syncs. The pairwise line: "It gets its own id for you, and won't see your other
  products or how you sign in." A declined item is not asked again and never reaches the app
  (S-16 D19); declining Cloud Sync leaves sync off for that app.
- **Continue to <App>** and **Cancel**, the same width and height, listed after what the app gets
  (scopes come before the buttons); on phones they stack full width with the primary last.
  Continue posts the granted subset and applies the choice, any Replace and the grant together.

### 3.14 Accessibility and motion (every step, every surface)

- **Focus and announcements.** Each step change moves focus to the new h1, and the card's
  `aria-live="polite"` region announces it. Errors use `role="alert"`. A refreshed list (seat
  taken, device removed) is announced and keeps focus in the list.
- **Targets** are at least 44 × 44 px (controls are 48–52 px). Logo-only provider buttons carry
  their accessible name and show it as a tooltip on hover and focus.
- **License rows** are native radios in a `radiogroup` "Licenses for <Product>". Full and blocked
  rows are not radios: each is a labelled `role="group"` whose tag and reason are text, tied to it with
  `aria-describedby`. It never carries `aria-disabled`, which ARIA applies to every focusable
  descendant and would disable its **Replace a device** button (D-94).
  Its **Replace a device** button and **Free a device** link stay in Tab order. Status is never
  colour alone: tags and "2 of 3 devices" are text, and the meter is `aria-hidden`. Disabled rows
  keep body-text contrast (4.5:1). Only the missing radio and the meter colour mark them; their
  text is never dimmed.
- **Code entry** is one input drawn as six cells (§3.4), never six inputs.
- **Time limits.** The ReturnStep timer pauses on focus and has **Stay here**. Device and approval
  codes show the time left and announce it once at one minute. No step ends without warning (WCAG
  2.2.1).
- **Motion** (§3.18). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` every step swaps instantly, there is no burst,
  the Replace confirm appears at once and skeletons do not shimmer.
- **Reflow.** At 320 CSS px, 200 % zoom and 400 % zoom nothing scrolls sideways (EXPERIENCE §7.1).
- **Forced colours.** In Windows high contrast, radios, meters, focus and the passport render with
  system colours; nothing depends on a tint or a fill.
- **Custom accent.** A product's custom accent is checked in light and dark: contrast holds and
  the accent never fills a provider button. RTL mirrors
  arrows and chevrons, never logos.
- **Kits** use the platform equivalents: VoiceOver and TalkBack labels from the same copy keys,
  Dynamic Type and font scale up to the largest accessibility size, and on TV the D-pad's first
  focus is the preselected row, then the primary.
- **Desktop** (D-77): focus moves to the form's new h1 after each morph (§3.18); Esc is Cancel and Return the primary; the
  waiting status is a polite live region announced once per change and its spinner is
  `aria-hidden`; VoiceOver, Narrator and Orca read the same copy keys; terminals honour `NO_COLOR`
  and `TERM=dumb` (no spinner, plain lines).

### 3.17 Apps: the one sign-in form (desktop, mobile, Godot and terminals)

Every in-app step happens inside one form whose content morphs in place (O-13, O-14).

**The form.** Each kit has one sign-in component that owns every in-app step: `SignIn` in React,
`<pk-sign-in>` in the elements, `SignInView` in SwiftUI, `SignIn(state)` in Compose, `PKeySignIn`
in Godot and `SignIn.qml` in Qt. Its headless model is `SignInModel` (§5.1 kit layers). The app
header (the product icon and name) and, once known, the person row persist. Only the body
underneath morphs (§3.18):

1. **Sign in** (frame 23): the product as the hero, **Continue in browser**, the logo-only provider
   row as shortcuts, **Have a license key?** and `signin.footer`.
2. **Finish in your browser** (frames 24, 28, 31, 38), or **Sign in with a code** (frames 29, 35),
   replacing step 1's body in place.
3. **Choose a license** (frames 36, 18, 39, 40), and **Replace a device** in place of the list
   (frames 37, 30). Also the key field (**Use a license key instead**) and the no-license state.
4. **Done** (frame 41) when a license was added or issued now; otherwise the app opens with the
   toast (frame 27). With License off there is no license and no tier to name, so the form ends on
   Done with `signin.return.signedInShort` alone. Errors and expiry render in place too.

**No sheet, dialog or popup is stacked on the form.** The one exception is the destructive
confirm of a Replace, which uses the system confirmation where the platform's guidelines expect one
(D-80). The app's menus stay live while the form waits, so **Quit** works, and quitting cancels.

**Presentation** (D-79). One option with the same name and values in every kit:

| `presentation`       | Where the form lives                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | License choice (I-04 §G)             |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `"inline"` (default) | In the app's own surface: the Welcome window's pane on desktop, the gate's full screen on phones and TVs, the game's panel in Godot, the page in the web kits                                                                                                                            | In the form (`licenseChoice: "app"`) |
| `"sheet"`            | One platform modal that holds every step, for an app that opens sign-in from a menu: a macOS or iOS sheet, a Windows `ContentDialog`, a GNOME `AdwDialog`, an Android modal bottom sheet, a web `<dialog>`, the Godot `PKeySheet`. The steps morph inside it; a second sheet never opens | In the form (`"app"`)                |
| `"browser"`          | The form shows only steps 1, 2 and Done; the hosted card runs everything else (path A, §2.4)                                                                                                                                                                                             | On the card (`"card"`)               |

A second option, `replace: "inline" | "browser"` (default inline), sends **Replace a device…** to
the card for an app that wants the in-app choice but the browser for device management. The
terminals have one presentation, the browser one. Device-code sign-ins (TV, console, **Use a code
instead**) always choose on the card (D-82), so for them the form goes from step 2 to Done.

**Native controls stay native** inside the form: system buttons and text fields, the platform's
progress indicator, `SignInWithAppleButton`, Credential Manager, the passkey sheet, and the system
confirmation for a destructive Replace. Only the layout, the copy and the order are Polaris Key's

**Start** (D-62). The app pushes the request (`POST /<p>/identity/request`, RFC 9126; I-15) with
the computer's label (D-73) and `licenseChoice` (I-04 §G.2), and gets a `request_uri`. It opens
`authorize` with it in the default browser (`NSWorkspace.open`, `Desktop.browse` or `xdg-open`, `shell.openExternal`,
Tauri's opener, `OS.shell_open`; a CLI uses `open`, `start` or `xdg-open`). The redirect is, in
this order:

1. **Loopback** (I-15, RFC 8252 §7.3): `http://127.0.0.1:<ephemeral port>/pkey/callback`, the IP
   literal and never `localhost`. The listener binds 127.0.0.1 only, accepts one request whose
   `state` matches, and closes on that request or when the flow expires (10 minutes). PKCE S256
   throughout. This is the default in every desktop kit.
2. **A registered scheme** (`tidewater://pkey/callback`) when the app registers one and cannot
   listen: a policy that blocks loopback, or an App Sandbox build without
   `com.apple.security.network.server`. A registered scheme also lets ReturnStep's **Return to
   <App>** bring the app forward (D-64).
3. **Device code** (I-08) when neither works, when the browser does not open, when the person picks
   **Use a code instead**, or in a headless terminal (D-68).

Claimed HTTPS stays a mobile redirect class. On phones the form hands off to
`ASWebAuthenticationSession` or Custom Tabs, the platform's own browser sheet, which the kit does
not draw; when it closes, the form morphs to step 3.

**Step 1, Sign in** (frame 23, D-69). The signed-out Welcome window (UI-KITS §2.1: a split window
with the product's art) shows the 64 px hero icon, h1 "Sign in to <Product>", the lede

an outbound glyph, "or", the logo-only provider row, **Have a license key?** and `signin.footer`.

- **Steam** runs the Steam session ticket when the app runs under Steam (I-14), with no browser.
  Otherwise it opens the card on Steam.
- **Google** opens the card on Google.

The desktop step 1 has no **Sign in on your phone or computer** row (that is the TV and console
path, §4.2) and no passkey row: the card offers the passkey, with conditional UI, in the browser

- **Use a license key instead** morphs the body into the key field; its confirm **Add and use on

- **Seat taken since load** (`device_limit` with a fresh view): the list refreshes in place and
  announces `signin.choice.raced`. The form re-reads the view when the app becomes active again.

### 3.18 Motion

> **Owner, verbatim (2026-10-05):** "Also, animations are going to be key here. I want this
> experience to feel modern, especially the web one." (O-16)

Motion explains **where you are in the flow**: what stayed, what changed, and which way you went.
It never decorates. The patterns are the shared motion system's (S-23,
`notes/S-23-motion-system.md` §5–§6), applied to sign-in. **The canonical tokens live in
`packages/brand`** (`tokens.json`, the generated `--pk-*` custom properties and
`src/generated/kit.ts` `motion`, MO-01); token names and values are S-23's.

**Tokens** (S-23 §5). Durations: `--pk-duration-instant` (0), `--pk-duration-micro` (80 ms),
`--pk-duration-fast` (120 ms, exits), `--pk-duration-base` (200 ms), `--pk-duration-moderate`
(260 ms, morph and expand), `--pk-duration-slow` (320 ms, sheets), `--pk-duration-deliberate`
(480 ms, the success burst) and `--pk-duration-shimmer` (1600 ms, the skeleton sheen). Easing:
`--pk-ease-standard` `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, `--pk-ease-enter` `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0, 1)`,
`--pk-ease-exit` `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 1, 1)` and `--pk-ease-emphasized`. Distances:
`--pk-motion-distance-sm` (4 px, the stagger rise), `-md` (8 px, exits) and `-lg` (12 px, enters).
Stagger: `--pk-stagger-step` (30 ms), at most `--pk-stagger-max` (6) steps. There is no `quick`
token: exits run at `fast`. Press feedback at `micro`/`fast` is the one quicker response, and
sheets keep `slow`.

**Rules.**

- **Direction of travel.** Forward steps enter from the inline end; Back (Back, Change, Not you?,
  Cancel to step 1) enters from the inline start. The browser's Back button is a step back: each
  card step pushes a history entry (`history.pushState`), and a reload restores the step from the
  server flow record.
- **Focus follows the morph.** Focus moves to the new h1 when the enter finishes, or at once under
  reduced motion. The live region announces the step once.
- **One thing moves at a time.** A morph never runs while a list is staggering; a late answer waits
  for the running transition to end and then morphs once.
- **No layout shift.** A busy button keeps its width, skeletons hold the final size, and the card's
  width never animates.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the in-app `data-motion="reduce"`,
  `accessibilityReduceMotion`, `ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE` 0, Godot `ui_reduce_motion`): every step
  is an instant swap with no fade, slide, scale or spark (S-23 D3); the success mark is a static
  check and skeletons do not shimmer. `motion:
"none"` in a kit's theme forces the same.
- **CSP-safe on the web.** The Worker serves the card under its strict CSP (no inline scripts or
  styles). Motion lives in the external stylesheet as keyframes and `::view-transition-*` rules,
  driven by class changes from the external script. Sizes are set only through the CSSOM or the
  Web Animations API, which `style-src 'self'` allows. There are no `style=""` attributes, inline
  handlers, `eval` or injected `<style>`. Without the View Transitions API the same classes run
  CSS keyframes, and without JS the Worker pages swap with a full page load and no motion.
- **The star never animates** (BRAND §7.5); the success burst takes FLOWS F20's spark shape and
  never the Polaris mark.
- **One celebration.** The burst runs only on "<Product> is yours". Never on ConsentStep,
  DeviceConfirm, Replace, sign-out or Disconnect. The passport and brand row never animate beyond
  the shared-element morph. Under reduced motion the check is static.

**In the kits** (UI-KITS §4.8 maps each platform; a kit uses its platform's native motion, and the
web numbers above apply to the web):

### 4.16 The integrated web flow (one card, start to finish)

> **Owner, verbatim (2026-10-05):** "And for the web-based experience, let's see what it looks
> like for it to be fully integrated into the sign in flow instead of being disparate." (O-16)

Path A is one card at one address, `key.plrs.im/signin?request=<handle>` (and `/signin` or
`/activate?product=<slug>` for portal-direct visits, which open the same card on its first step).
Sign-in, the email code, the license key, the license choice, Replace a device, consent and the
return are **steps of that one card**. The card never hops to another page for activation, device
management or confirmation, and it never opens a modal. Each step replaces the last with the morph
of §3.18; the app header and the person row stay put.

**Storyboard** (Tidewater Studio, a returning person with a key from a friend's machine; the
[prototype](sign-in/prototype/) runs it, and [the frame strip](sign-in/prototype/strip-dark.png)
shows it in both themes):

1. **Sign in** (MethodsStep): the app header, the email field, **Continue**, the provider row,
   **Have a license key?**.
2. **Check your email** (CodeStep): six cells; the code submits on the sixth digit. The header
   stays; only the body slides.
3. **Choose a license** (LicenseChoiceStep): the person row slides in under the header and the rows
   stagger in. Pro is preselected; Edu is full.
4. **Use a license key instead**: the body becomes the key field (KeyStep) in place, with the live
   verdict "Key for Tidewater Studio". **Back** returns to the list. (The same step is the on-ramp
   from **Have a license key?** before sign-in: one component, one place.)
5. **Replace a device** on the full Edu row: the list becomes Edu's devices, Work laptop
   preselected, and the confirm "Replace Work laptop?" expands under the list.
6. **Replace and continue** → **Continue to Tidewater Studio as Mara?** (ConsentStep) with the
   license line "Tidewater Studio Edu · replaces Work laptop" and **Change**.
7. **Continue to Tidewater Studio** → the success moment: the check draws, "You're signed in to
   Tidewater Studio", "You can close this tab and return to Tidewater Studio." (desktop ReturnStep)
   or the 3 s return on a phone.

**What is not a separate place:**

- the license key entry (`/activate`): a step of the card, reachable before or after sign-in;
- **Free a device** (FreeDevicePage): a full row shows only **Replace a device** in the card. The
  link remains only where Replace is not offered but the portal is on (seat-holder licenses, I-24a)
  (D-87);
- the Worker's device-code pages: the card with the app header (UX-43), and the no-JS Worker twins
  render the same steps with full page loads and no motion.

**History and reload.** Each step is a history entry, so the browser's Back goes back one step,
and a reload restores the step from the server flow record (§3.18). Nothing is written until the
flow's final primary (principle 7), so going back never undoes anything.

(§A–§F, delegated decisions 1–24), as `plans/I-27.md` amends it. This document is the experience
authority, and they say the same thing. In brief:

- `GET /api/signin/requests/:handle/licenses` answers `LicenseChoiceView` `{state: "choose" |
"autoIssue" | "none", choices, keep, preselected, create: {tierName, access} | null, getLicense:
{activateUrl, purchaseUrl, keyEntry} | null}`. A `LicenseChoice` is `{id, tierName, name, origin,
access: "seats" | "account", seats: {used, limit | null}, current, expiresAt, state: "free" |
"full" | "blocked", blockedReason?, replace, freeDeviceUrl}`. `state: "autoIssue"` is the first
  automatic license: the card skips the step and Continue creates the license (D-95).
- `GET /api/signin/requests/:handle/licenses/:licenseId/devices` answers `ReplaceView` (label,
  platform, device type, last seen, `leastRecent`, `activeNow`, `thisBrowser`). A sign-in
  license answers it like any other: it keeps a device limit (D-53).
- I-08's Continue `POST` carries `choice`: `{kind: "license", licenseId, replaceDeviceId?}`,
  `{kind: "keep"}` or `{kind: "create"}`. It answers `409 license_choice_required` (registered by
  I-08 in `errors.json`) when the choice is missing, `404 not_found`, `device_limit` for a race,
  and `429 rate_limited` with `retryAfter`. The free and the bind of a Replace commit in one batch.
- The token exchange (I-13: `token` with the token-exchange grant) answers `200 {"status":
"choose", "grant", "choices": …}` when a choice is due and `licenseChoice` is `"app"`;
  `choice/complete` takes the choice (§6.5).
- Portal summaries (`GET /api/licenses`, `GET /api/library`) gain `access`, the fact behind the origin
  "From signing in" and the mixed rule (never a displayed type); the library's seats keep the real
  limit for sign-in licenses (I-04 §F.6, D-53). The store named in an origin comes from the product
  view's `purchase.store` (PX-W6): the store's name only.
- **Device wire: none.** `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 4, `corpusVersion` 2. The device-code poll stays
  `pending` while the person chooses. P1-07's `confirm.attachable` stays, and is `false` unless the
  person picked **Keep**. **Cancel** returns `access_denied` on every surface.
- I-26 covers the legacy product-OIDC path until I-08 with PX-14 replaces it (§4.14).

**Conformance:** the `LicenseChoice` rows of `ui-matrix.json`'s `signIn` family (UK-02b: loading,
many, one, current, keep, new, create, all-full, sign-in, mixed, replace-open, raced, none-keys,
none-no-keys, none-replaceable, grant-expired), and I-04 §C's transcripts plus §F.6's `devicecode-sign-in.json`, with
their Swift and Godot mirrors (`gen transcripts`).

### 6.3 Changes to planned contracts

- **PX-W13 consent:** the route takes `?choice=` and reports the **chosen** license as `anchor`; it
  does not run a dry-run anchor (I-04). `more: n` gives way to the combined line (§3.6 rule 1).
  I-34 adds `required` and `granted` to its items (§3.8).
- **I-08 device code:** the device-side `confirm` with `attachable` stays, offered only after
  **Keep** (I-04 delegated decision 4). `verification_uri` is `<origin>/device`; `/tv` is an alias
  for TV and console screens.
- **PX-W8 Q5:** the sign-in seat refusal is not the main path. ReplaceDevice handles a full
  license in the card, and LX-18's `device_limit` + `manageUrl` covers only the bind-time race on surfaces
  without the card. Cancel answers `access_denied` (D-51).

### 6.4 Desktop: what the wire needs

The desktop experience rides on planned routes and three additions from I-15: the loopback class,
one browser-facing page and bind-on-redemption. **No device-wire change**: `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 4 and `corpusVersion` 2.

1. **Loopback and scheme redirects:** I-15 (planned). The loopback class must accept
   `http://127.0.0.1:<any port>/pkey/callback` (RFC 8252 §7.3), compared without the port.
2. **The hosted desktop ReturnStep:** `GET /signin/return?request=<handle>[&cancelled=1]`, a
   browser-facing, same-origin, `no-store` page. It renders §3.10's desktop variant from the
   request handle (the app header, the chosen license, and **Return to <App>** only when the client
   record has a registered scheme). Planned in I-15 (OpenAPI and `routeCoverage`, rule 10).
3. **Bind on redemption:** for loopback and scheme redirects the choice, any Replace and the grant
   apply when the app redeems the code at `token` (the redirect code record carries the choice,
   I-04 §2.5), so an app-side Cancel or a crash before redemption changes nothing. I-15 builds this
   for the native classes. In path B nothing binds before `choice/complete` (§6.5).
4. **The computer's label:** the SDK default changes for Windows, JVM, Node and Python (D-73).
   That is display data under PX-W13's normalisation; the wire is unchanged.
5. **Device code fallback:** I-08's `device/start` and `poll` (planned), with
   `verification_uri_complete` for **Open browser**.

### 6.5 The one form: what the wire needs (I-04 §G)

Path B rides on `plans/I-04.md` §G ("One sign-in form, licence choice in the app"), with the routes
of `plans/I-27.md` §2.1. In brief:

- **The declaration** `licenseChoice: "app" | "card"` on the pushed request (default `"card"`), on
  `authorize` as `license_choice=app` (default card), and on the native exchange (default `"app"`).
  Device code has none: the card always chooses there (D-82).
- **The card in app mode** skips LicenseChoiceStep, keeps Consent with
  `signin.consent.licenseInApp`, and ends on the desktop ReturnStep variant
  `signin.return.chooseInApp` (§3.10).
- **The completion** (`token`) answers `200 {status: "choose", grant, expiresIn: 300, choices:
LicenseChoiceView}` when a choice is due. With no choice due (silent re-authentication, a key
  chosen on the card, a first automatic license) it answers the token response, and
  `choice/complete` answers that same shape.
- **Four grant routes**, device-facing and body-only: `POST /<p>/identity/choice/licenses`,
  `…/choice/devices`, `…/choice/complete` and `…/choice/cancel`. `LicenseChoiceInput` gains
  `{kind: "key", key}`. Errors reuse `invalid_grant`, `license_choice_required`, `not_found`,
  `device_limit` and `rate_limited`.
- **Security:** the grant is bound to the redeeming device and the PKCE verifier, lives 300 s,
  lists devices only for a replaceable license in its own view (one `404` for everything else) and
  never looks anything up by license key. A Replace is `freeAccountDevice()` with the portal's
  rules, budget, audit and email.
- **Wire:** additive and opt-in. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` 4, `DISCOVERY_VERSION` 2, `corpusVersion` 2;
  no corpus change; new transcripts and the parity rows `identity.choice` and `ui.signin` (the
  one form's row, UK-02b D1).
- **Deployed:** old SDKs keep card behaviour; a new SDK on an old Worker falls back to card mode;
  grants in flight are dropped on a rollback.

**The motion of §3.18 needs no wire.** The card's history entries per step restore from the server
flow record that I-07 and PX-W13 already keep.

### 6.6 Sign-in hints

S-24's **Add your name and email** (§3.17) needs three optional hints, planned in I-08: `login_hint`
(an email; prefills MethodsStep and opens CodeStep, never verifies), `name_hint` (prefills
FinishStep's screen name for a new account only) and `purpose: "signin" | "attach"` (copy only: the
header line "Add <Product> to your account", Consent and ReturnStep). They travel on the pushed
`request` and on `authorize`, and on `auth/device/start` as `loginHint`, `nameHint` and `purpose`.
Invalid hints are ignored. **No signed document or activation response changes**:
`PROTOCOL_VERSION` 4, `DISCOVERY_VERSION` 2, `corpusVersion` 2, new transcripts only, and I-10a and
I-10b add the members to Node, React, Python, Swift, Kotlin and Godot.
