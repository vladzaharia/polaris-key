## 1. Principles

1. **Authenticate once, then decide what this installation runs on.** Sign-in proves who the
   person is. Binding an installation to a license is a separate choice the person makes. It is
   never a silent side effect (O-1, O-3).
2. **One card, one step, one primary.** Every browser step renders inside the same `AuthCard`
   (EXPERIENCE §8 measures), and every in-app step inside the kit's one form (§3.17). Each step has
   exactly one primary button. Each step replaces the last in place, with no page hop and no modal
   layer (O-13, O-16). The header and footer persist across the steps of one flow.
3. **Credentials are entered only in Polaris Key's card or the platform's own sheet.** An app never
   hosts an email code, a passkey or a provider password. Kits render natively only what carries no
   credential: the form's sign-in step, the hand-off and the code view, the license choice and
   Replace a device (through the grant, I-04 §G), key entry and the device limit.
4. **Same names, same strings, everywhere.** A step has one component name and one set of copy keys
   (§5.2) across the web card, the Worker pages and every kit.
5. **No enumeration, no dead ends.** Unknown and known emails get the same response. Every refusal
   names the next step. A failure never shows an HTTP status or an internal code.
6. **The product is the hero of an app's sign-in.** The app header (a 56 px icon and the app's
   name) is the most prominent thing above the step. Polaris Key's lockup shrinks to a quiet brand
   row, the trusted frame rather than the subject. In kits the product owns the screen.
7. **Nothing changes until the last primary.** The license choice, a Replace and consent are
   recorded on the flow and applied together when the person presses the flow's final primary.
8. **Desktop is not a big phone.** A desktop app signs in in the person's default browser, waits
   in its own form, in place, with a way out (Cancel, Open browser again, Use a code instead), ends
   the tab with "You can close this tab", and comes back to the front by itself (§3.17). Desktop
   sizes, controls and button order are the platform's (UI-KITS §2.1).
9. **Motion explains the flow.** Steps morph; what stays, stays still; lists arrive in order; the
   success moment is brief. Under reduced motion every step swaps instantly (§3.18).

### 2.2 What a sign-in flow carries

One server-held flow record per sign-in (I-07's flow, PX-W13's request handle for passthrough). It
holds the context, `returnTo`, the app request handle and binder cookie (`__Host-pk_req`), the
device label and user code, the email-gate state (G31), and, after LicenseChoiceStep, the **chosen
binding** (§6.3). Display data never comes from a query parameter (PX-W13 §2.2).

### 2.3 Bindings: what "associate the installation" means

An installation is a `devices` row (`X-PKey-Device`). Signing in through Identity sets
`devices.subject`. That makes the account the device's entitlement holder (S-19 decision 4). It also
binds the device to one **anchor** license (`bound_by = 'signin'`). LicenseChoiceStep is where that
anchor is chosen. Nothing else binds a signed-in device:

- key entry binds to the entered license (`bound_by = 'key'`, D24 unchanged); in a passthrough the
  KeyStep confirm is itself the choice (I-04 delegated decision 10);
- attach adds the device's license to the account and never re-anchors (I-09 Q2). After **Keep**,
  the device still offers it (P1-07 `confirm.attachable`, I-04 delegated decision 4);
- sign-out releases a sign-in-bound seat (I-09 §2.2, I-04 Q3).

### 2.4 Two ways to integrate

The owner asked for two complete, equal paths over one contract (O-15). Every step and state of
§3 exists on both paths, with the same copy keys (§5.2) and the same server answers
(`plans/I-04.md` §A–§G).

- **Path A, hosted.** The card at `key.plrs.im` runs the whole flow in one continuous card:
  sign-in, the license key, the license choice, Replace a device, consent and the return (§4.16).
  An app needs no UI code. It opens a URL (the pushed request's `authorizeUrl`, or the web
  `authorize` redirect) or shows a device code, and receives an activation. The kits'
  `presentation: "browser"` and the terminals use this path.
- **Path B, native.** The app runs the flow in its own window. The card still authenticates (D17:
  credentials only on the card or a platform sheet) and asks consent when it is due. Everything
  after that comes back to the app through a pending sign-in grant (`licenseChoice: "app"`, I-04
  §G). Path B comes in two layers: the kit's drop-in **one sign-in form** (§3.17; `presentation:
"inline"` by default, `"sheet"` on request), and the **headless primitives** for an app that
  draws its own UI.

- a native sign-in with the choice on the card (`exchange({…, licenseChoice: "card"})` answers

## 3. The step model

### 3.1 The card

`ui/auth/AuthCard` (UX-40 promotes the built `LoginCard`). The measures are EXPERIENCE §8's: a
28.5 rem card, radius 22 px, elevation 3, 48–52 px controls, a 24 px h1, top-aligned at 10 vh on the
static star field, and edge to edge under a 56 px lockup row on phones. Help · Privacy · Terms sit
below the card. **No "Polaris Key · key.plrs.im" line.**

The header persists on every step of one app sign-in, including ReturnStep and any Worker page that
carries the request handle (§3.13). Branding is data only: name, icon, developer, origin and device label, from the client record
(PX-W13 §2.2), checked against reserved names (warn first, then enforce). A failing name renders the
neutral frame (`nameVerified: false`).

**Steps replace each other in place** (O-16, §4.16). Within one flow the card never navigates to
another page and never opens a modal. The body morphs (§3.18) while the brand row, the header, the
person row and the footer stay. **Replace a device**, the key field and Consent's **Change** are
steps of the same card.

### 3.5 EmailGateStep (first provider sign-in) and profile import

PORTAL §4.29 stands, with these rules made explicit (O-8):

- **Required, once per account.** No skip path. **Cancel sign-in** abandons the sign-in. No token,
  session or binding is issued before the gate passes.
- **Layout:** the "Signed in with <Provider> · <identity> · Done" strip; h1 "Confirm your email";
  lede (in an app: "One step before <App>. We send sign-in codes, receipts and security notices
  here."); **ProfileImport**; the email radio cards; Terms when the product requires them; the
  primary; the Polaris Key terms line.
- **Fast path:** a provider-verified address needs **no code**. That means Google with
  `email_verified: true`, or any Apple address, private relay included. The primary is **Continue**
  (in passthrough, **Continue to <App>**).
- **Code path:** a typed or unverified address shows six cells, and the primary is **Verify and
  continue**.
- **Steam** has no email. The field starts empty, with the help "Steam doesn't share an email. Add
  one so you can get back in without Steam." The primary is **Send code** (frame 04).
- **Email already in another account** (known only after proof): **Join into one account** (PORTAL
  §4.11, proof of both in this session, 72 h undo) or **Use a different email**. Never joined
  silently, never by email match.
- **ProfileImport:** Google gives name and picture. Apple gives the name on first consent only, and
  no picture ("Apple doesn't share a picture. Add one"). Steam gives persona and avatar. Game Center
  gives an alias. Imported values follow their source until the person chooses. Explicit choices
  stick. Pictures are copied, re-encoded and served from the same origin (G33).
- **After the gate**, a first platform sign-in shows the **Add another way to sign in** nudge (PORTAL
  §4.10) once. It shows again after 30 days only if the account still has a single method. In
  passthrough the nudge waits until the next portal visit; it never stands between a person and the
  app.

### 3.6 LicenseChoiceStep

**Entry condition.** An app sign-in (passthrough, kit native, or the I-26 legacy page, §4.14) on a
product with the License service on, whose completion will **bind or re-bind this installation**.
It shows on **every** such sign-in, even with one license. It is skipped only for silent
re-authentication of an installation already bound to this account on a usable license (token
refresh, an I-13 exchange after the grant exists, a device-code re-poll), and after a KeyStep
confirm in the same flow, because the key's license is the choice (I-04 delegated decision 10).
Portal direct and console sign-ins never show it: no installation is involved.

**Layout (frame 05).** The person row (avatar, name, email, **Not you?**, which ends this account's
part of the flow and returns to MethodsStep with the app header intact). h1 "Choose a license for
this device", lede "<Product> will use it on <device label>." (web: "…in this browser."). Then one
`radiogroup` "Licenses for <Product>", the primary **Use this license and continue**, and a quiet
row with **Use a license key instead** (when the product accepts keys) and **Cancel**, which
returns `access_denied` to the app on every surface (on device code it denies the code; D-51).

**Row anatomy** (O-11). Every license row has three lines:

1. the **title**: the license's own name when it has one, else the product name ("Tidewater
   Studio"), then the row's tag;
2. the **tier and devices**: the tier as a neutral pill ("Pro", "Standard"), then the device
   counter "{used} of {limit} devices", for every license. No license is labelled by type: every
   license is account-bound (owner decision, 2026-10-05; O-17);
3. the **meta**: "{origin} · {term}". The origin says in plain words how the license came to be:
   "From signing in" (issued at sign-in, `access: "account"`, §6.2), "Key ending 3WPLDA" (the key's
   last six characters, when kept; G7) else "Added with a key", "Steam key ending 3WPLDA" (or
   "Steam key") when the key came from a store purchase, "From Steam" for a store-bound license
   with no key, "From {developer}", "Free", "Gift" or "Included with {org}". Only the store's name
   is shown, never an order id. The term is "Lifetime", "Until 12 Jun 2027", "Renews 3 Mar" or
   "Yearly, until 2 Feb 2027" (never "For life").

Every license with a device limit, sign-in licenses included, adds a seat meter under line 3, and a
full one is a **Full** row with **No free devices** and its Replace. **When the account holds a
sign-in license for the product, its key and seat licenses hide the counter and the meter** (D-54);
line 2 is the pill alone, and a full one keeps its **No free devices** tag. The sign-in row
keeps "{used} of {limit} devices" and its meter. Two rows with the same title are
told apart by the pill, origin and term.

### 3.7 ReplaceDevice

A step of its own (frames 06, 37, 30; D-81), opened by **Replace a device** on a full row. The
license list morphs into that license's devices in place (§3.18), under the heading "Replace a
device" and the license's title, pill and counter; the header and the person row stay. Focus moves
to the heading. The confirm expands under the list and is the step's only primary. In a kit it is
the system confirmation where the platform expects one (D-80).

- **The list:** that license's seat-holding devices (authorized and not dormant), least recently
  used first. The least recent is preselected and tagged **Least recent**. **Active now** marks a
  device seen in the last 10 minutes, and **This browser** the browser's own web device. Each row
  has a form-factor glyph, the device label and "<platform> · last used <when>". This installation
  is never in the list.
- **One confirm that names the device.** It sits under the list and follows the selection. There is
  no second dialog (an L1 inline confirm, EXPERIENCE §7):
  - title "Replace <device>?";
  - "<device> signs out of <Product> and <this device> takes its seat. <device> can sign in again
    later if a seat is free. We'll email you about it.";
  - when the selected device is **Active now**, a `warning` line: "<device> is in use right now.";
  - **Replace and continue** (primary) and **Back**, which returns to the license list with focus
    on that row's **Replace a device**.
- **Two taps** from a full license: **Replace a device**, then **Replace and continue**. The least
  recent device is already chosen.
- **Nothing is written until the flow's final primary.** "Replace and continue" records
  `{license, replaceDeviceId}` on the flow. When Consent follows, its license line reads "<license>
  · replaces <device>" with **Change**, and the replacement happens on **Continue to <App>**.
  Otherwise "Replace and continue" is the final primary.
- **Effect** (I-04 §B): the server frees the device through `freeAccountDevice()`, the portal's
  Remove operation (account ownership, `portal_enabled`, the `portalDeviceDisconnect` budget
  shared with the portal, audit `portal.device.disconnect` with "to sign in <new label>", the
  device-removed email), then binds this installation into the freed seat with
  `bound_by = 'signin'`. The free and the bind should commit in one batch (§6.2 delta 3).
- **Safety.** The fresh sign-in (under 5 minutes) is the step-up. In the device-code context the
  code panel stays on screen and the confirm names both devices, so a phished code cannot quietly
  trade one of the person's devices for the attacker's. A Replace never counts as a key entry (I-04
  decision 11).
- **Not offered** when the product's customer portal is off and on seat-holder licenses (I-24a:
  **Free a device** only). The I-26 legacy page offers it without JS (D-59). Sign-in licenses get it like any
  other: they keep a device limit and can be full (D-53). **Free a device** opens
  `#/p/<slug>/free-device?license=<id>&for=<this device label>&return=/signin?request=<handle>`
  (PX-10's focused flow), which returns to this step (I-04 decision 14).
- **Failures:** "That device was already removed. Choose again." (the list refreshes); "Too many
  device changes. Try again in {seconds} seconds." (`429 rate_limited` with `retryAfter`); "Someone
  took that seat first. Choose again." (a `device_limit` race with a fresh view). If the free had
  already committed: "<device> was removed, but someone took the seat first. Choose again."

**Device vocabulary** (D-08, D-49): **Replace a device** opens the device list in place (in a kit
set to `replace: "browser"` it opens the card). **Replace <device>?** with **Replace and continue** confirms it wherever the
action also seats this device: the card, the kit `LicenseChoice` and the kit `DeviceLimit`.
**Remove <device>** only frees a seat (on a sign-in license it also signs that device out of
the product) (the portal product page, FreeDevicePage). **Free a device**
is the link to FreeDevicePage from the card and the portal's quick actions. "Free up a device",
"Manage devices in Polaris Key" and "Disconnect" are retired. The console keeps "Deauthorize".

### 3.14 Accessibility and motion (every step, every surface)

- **Focus and announcements.** Each step change moves focus to the new h1, and the card's
  `aria-live="polite"` region announces it. Errors use `role="alert"`. A refreshed list (seat
  taken, device removed) is announced and keeps focus in the list.
- **Targets** are at least 44 × 44 px (controls are 48–52 px). Logo-only provider buttons carry
  their accessible name and show it as a tooltip on hover and focus.
- **License rows** are native radios in a `radiogroup` "Licenses for <Product>". Full and blocked
  rows are not radios: each is a labelled `role="group"` whose tag and reason are text, tied to it with
  `aria-describedby`. It never carries `aria-disabled`, which ARIA applies to every focusable
  descendant and would disable its **Replace a device** button (D-94).
  Its **Replace a device** button and **Free a device** link stay in Tab order. Status is never
  colour alone: tags and "2 of 3 devices" are text, and the meter is `aria-hidden`. Disabled rows
  keep body-text contrast (4.5:1). Only the missing radio and the meter colour mark them; their
  text is never dimmed.
- **Code entry** is one input drawn as six cells (§3.4), never six inputs.
- **Time limits.** The ReturnStep timer pauses on focus and has **Stay here**. Device and approval
  codes show the time left and announce it once at one minute. No step ends without warning (WCAG
  2.2.1).
- **Motion** (§3.18). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` every step swaps instantly, there is no burst,
  the Replace confirm appears at once and skeletons do not shimmer.
- **Reflow.** At 320 CSS px and 200 % zoom nothing scrolls sideways (EXPERIENCE §7.1). RTL mirrors
  arrows and chevrons, never logos.
- **Kits** use the platform equivalents: VoiceOver and TalkBack labels from the same copy keys,
  Dynamic Type and font scale up to the largest accessibility size, and on TV the D-pad's first
  focus is the preselected row, then the primary.
- **Desktop** (D-77): focus moves to the form's new h1 after each morph (§3.18); Esc is Cancel and Return the primary; the
  waiting status is a polite live region announced once per change and its spinner is
  `aria-hidden`; VoiceOver, Narrator and Orca read the same copy keys; terminals honour `NO_COLOR`
  and `TERM=dumb` (no spinner, plain lines).

this order:

1. **Loopback** (I-15, RFC 8252 §7.3): `http://127.0.0.1:<ephemeral port>/pkey/callback`, the IP

   `state` matches, and closes on that request or when the flow expires (10 minutes). PKCE S256
   throughout. This is the default in every desktop kit.
2. **A registered scheme** (`tidewater://pkey/callback`) when the app registers one and cannot
   listen: a policy that blocks loopback, or an App Sandbox build without
   `com.apple.security.network.server`. A registered scheme also lets ReturnStep's **Return to
   <App>** bring the app forward (D-64).
3. **Device code** (I-08) when neither works, when the browser does not open, when the person picks
   **Use a code instead**, or in a headless terminal (D-68).

`ASWebAuthenticationSession` or Custom Tabs, the platform's own browser sheet, which the kit does
not draw; when it closes, the form morphs to step 3.

### 3.18 Motion

> **Owner, verbatim (2026-10-05):** "Also, animations are going to be key here. I want this
> experience to feel modern, especially the web one." (O-16)

Motion explains **where you are in the flow**: what stayed, what changed, and which way you went.
It never decorates. The patterns and token names below are generic on purpose. The motion spike
S-23 (`spike/S-23-motion`) is designing one motion system for the portal, the console and the kits,
and it adopts them. **The canonical tokens live in `packages/brand`** (`tokens.json`, the
generated `--pk-*` custom properties and `src/generated/kit.ts` `motion`). Where S-23 renames or
retunes a token, S-23 wins and this section follows.

**Tokens.** The existing tokens are `--pk-duration-instant` (0), `--pk-duration-fast` (120 ms),
`--pk-duration-base` (200 ms), `--pk-duration-slow` (320 ms), and `--pk-ease-standard`
`cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, `--pk-ease-enter` `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0, 1)` and `--pk-ease-exit`
`cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 1, 1)`. Sign-in proposes three more for S-23 to adopt:
`--pk-duration-quick` (160 ms), `--pk-duration-moderate` (260 ms) and `--pk-stagger` (30 ms per
item). Every transition runs 150–300 ms; press feedback at `fast` is the one quicker response, and
sheets keep `slow`.

**Rules.**

- **Direction of travel.** Forward steps enter from the inline end; Back (Back, Change, Not you?,
  Cancel to step 1) enters from the inline start. The browser's Back button is a step back: each
  card step pushes a history entry (`history.pushState`), and a reload restores the step from the
  server flow record.
- **Focus follows the morph.** Focus moves to the new h1 when the enter finishes, or at once under
  reduced motion. The live region announces the step once.
- **One thing moves at a time.** A morph never runs while a list is staggering; a late answer waits
  for the running transition to end and then morphs once.
- **No layout shift.** A busy button keeps its width, skeletons hold the final size, and the card's
  width never animates.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, `accessibilityReduceMotion`,
  `ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE` 0, Godot `ui_reduce_motion`): every step swaps instantly with no slide,
  scale or particle; the success mark is a static check and skeletons do not shimmer. `motion:
"none"` in a kit's theme forces the same.
- **CSP-safe on the web.** The Worker serves the card under its strict CSP (no inline scripts or
  styles). Motion lives in the external stylesheet as keyframes and `::view-transition-*` rules,
  driven by class changes from the external script. Sizes are set only through the CSSOM or the
  Web Animations API, which `style-src 'self'` allows. There are no `style=""` attributes, inline
  handlers, `eval` or injected `<style>`. Without the View Transitions API the same classes run
  CSS keyframes, and without JS the Worker pages swap with a full page load and no motion.
- **The star never animates** (BRAND §7.5); the success particles are separate small stars.

### 4.14 Legacy product sign-in (I-26)

Until I-08 retires it on Identity-on products, the legacy `/<p>/identity/auth/*` sign-in on
`provider: platform` products renders a no-JS Worker LicenseChoice page whenever the linked account
holds a usable license, instead of minting a second, `sub`-keyed license. It uses the same copy keys
and rows as §3.6: Free, Sign-in (the identity's own `sub` license, "From signing in"), Full (with **Replace a
device** as a no-JS disclosure and a "Replace <device>?" confirm page, plus the **Free a device**
link; D-59) and Create; **Use this license and continue** and **Cancel**. It has no Keep row, because it never names the
device's own license. Only the browser holding the flow's `__Host-` binder sees it, and without
the binder it refuses rather than mint (I-04 delegated decision 13). Its look follows
`renderAuthCard()` (§3.13) once UX-43 lands.

### 4.16 The integrated web flow (one card, start to finish)

> **Owner, verbatim (2026-10-05):** "And for the web-based experience, let's see what it looks
> like for it to be fully integrated into the sign in flow instead of being disparate." (O-16)

Path A is one card at one address, `key.plrs.im/signin?request=<handle>` (and `/signin` or
`/activate?product=<slug>` for portal-direct visits, which open the same card on its first step).
Sign-in, the email code, the license key, the license choice, Replace a device, consent and the
return are **steps of that one card**. The card never hops to another page for activation, device
management or confirmation, and it never opens a modal. Each step replaces the last with the morph
of §3.18; the app header and the person row stay put.

**Storyboard** (Tidewater Studio, a returning person with a key from a friend's machine; the
[prototype](sign-in/prototype/) runs it, and [the frame strip](sign-in/prototype/strip-dark.png)
shows it in both themes):

1. **Sign in** (MethodsStep): the app header, the email field, **Continue**, the provider row,
   **Have a license key?**.
2. **Check your email** (CodeStep): six cells; the code submits on the sixth digit. The header
   stays; only the body slides.
3. **Choose a license** (LicenseChoiceStep): the person row slides in under the header and the rows
   stagger in. Pro is preselected; Edu is full.
4. **Use a license key instead**: the body becomes the key field (KeyStep) in place, with the live
   verdict "Key for Tidewater Studio". **Back** returns to the list. (The same step is the on-ramp
   from **Have a license key?** before sign-in: one component, one place.)
5. **Replace a device** on the full Edu row: the list becomes Edu's devices, Work laptop
   preselected, and the confirm "Replace Work laptop?" expands under the list.
6. **Replace and continue** → **Continue to Tidewater Studio as Mara?** (ConsentStep) with the
   license line "Tidewater Studio Edu · replaces Work laptop" and **Change**.
7. **Continue to Tidewater Studio** → the success moment: the check draws, "You're signed in to
   Tidewater Studio", "You can close this tab and return to Tidewater Studio." (desktop ReturnStep)
   or the 3 s return on a phone.

**What is no longer a separate place:**

- the license key entry (`/activate`): a step of the card, reachable before or after sign-in;
- **Free a device** (FreeDevicePage): a full row shows only **Replace a device** in the card. The
  link remains only where Replace is not offered but the portal is on (seat-holder licenses, I-24a)
  (D-87);
- the Worker's device-code pages: the card with the app header (UX-43), and the no-JS Worker twins
  render the same steps with full page loads and no motion.

**History and reload.** Each step is a history entry, so the browser's Back goes back one step,
and a reload restores the step from the server flow record (§3.18). Nothing is written until the
flow's final primary (principle 7), so going back never undoes anything.

1. **Kit `SignIn` chooser**: the same logo-only provider row (Apple, Google, Steam, as the
   product ships). On Apple platforms Apple uses native `ASAuthorization` behind a logo-only button
   drawn to Apple's logo-only guidelines. On Android, Google goes through Credential Manager. Steam
   goes through the hosted card. Below the row: **Sign in with a passkey** (the platform passkey
   sheet), **Continue with email** (opens the card, which is identifier-first), **Sign in on your
   phone or computer** (device code), and the footnote `signin.footer`. **On desktop** the chooser
   leads with **Continue in browser** and has no passkey or device-code row (§3.17, D-69).
2. **Terminal:** `login` opens the default browser with a loopback redirect, and falls back to
   device code (the code in reverse video, **no QR**) when headless or with `--device-code`
   (§4.15, D-67, D-68). "Use a license key" is the `activate` verb.

**Kit layers** (UI-KITS §1.3): the one **`SignIn`** form is the drop-in, inside `PolarisKeyGate`
or alone with its `presentation`. Its steps, `SignInHandoff`, `LicenseChoice` (with
`ReplaceDevice`), `Activate` and `Done`, are the styled parts; `DeviceLimit` (titled **Replace a
device**) stays the key path's screen. The headless layer is one model, `SignInModel` (`useSignIn`,
`SignInModel`, `rememberSignInState`, `PKeySignInController`, `SignInViewModel`), with the step
models inside it, over the SDK primitives of §2.4. All take their states from the conformance UI
fixtures (UK-02b `signin-form`).

### 6.4 Desktop: what the wire needs

The desktop experience rides on planned routes. It adds one browser-facing page and two rules for
I-15's plan. **No device-wire change**: `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 4 and `corpusVersion` 2.

1. **Loopback and scheme redirects:** I-15 (planned). The loopback class must accept
   `http://127.0.0.1:<any port>/pkey/callback` (RFC 8252 §7.3), compared without the port.
2. **The hosted desktop ReturnStep:** `GET /signin/return?request=<handle>[&cancelled=1]`, a
   browser-facing, same-origin, `no-store` page. It renders §3.10's desktop variant from the
   request handle (the app header, the chosen license, and **Return to <App>** only when the client
   record has a registered scheme). **Not planned:** it goes into `plans/I-15.md` (OpenAPI and
   `routeCoverage`, rule 10).
3. **Bind on redemption:** for loopback and scheme redirects the choice, any Replace and the grant
   apply when the app redeems the code at `redirect/token` (the redirect code record carries the
   choice, I-04 §2.5), so an app-side Cancel or a crash before redemption changes nothing. The I-15
   plan confirms this for the native classes. In path B nothing binds before `choice/complete`
   (§6.5).
4. **The computer's label:** the SDK default changes for Windows, JVM, Node and Python (D-73).
   That is display data under PX-W13's normalisation; the wire is unchanged.
5. **Device code fallback:** I-08's `device/start` and `poll` (planned), with
   `verification_uri_complete` for **Open browser**.

### 6.5 The one form: what the wire needs (I-04 §G)

Path B rides on one approved amendment, `plans/I-04.md` §G ("One sign-in form, licence choice in
the app"). In brief:

  `authorize` as `license_choice=app` (default card), and on the native exchange (default `"app"`).
  Device code has none: the card always chooses there (D-82).
- **The card in app mode** skips LicenseChoiceStep, keeps Consent with
  `signin.consent.licenseInApp`, and ends on the desktop ReturnStep variant
  `signin.return.chooseInApp` (§3.10).
- **The completion** (`redirect/token`, `token`) answers `200 {status: "choose", grant, expiresIn:
300, choices: LicenseChoiceView}` when a choice is due.
- **Four grant routes**, device-facing and body-only: `POST /<p>/identity/choice/licenses`,
  `…/choice/devices`, `…/choice/complete` and `…/choice/cancel`. `LicenseChoiceInput` gains
  `{kind: "key", key}`. Errors reuse `invalid_grant`, `license_choice_required`, `not_found`,
  `device_limit` and `rate_limited`.
- **Security:** the grant is bound to the redeeming device and the PKCE verifier, lives 300 s,
  lists devices only for a replaceable license in its own view (one `404` for everything else) and
  never looks anything up by license key. A Replace is `freeAccountDevice()` with the portal's

- **Deployed:** old SDKs keep card behaviour; a new SDK on an old Worker falls back to card mode;
  grants in flight are dropped on a rollback.

flow record that I-07 and PX-W13 already keep.

## 9. Mockups

Static HTML (`sign-in/card.html`, `frames.js`, `card.css`) on the generated `@polaris-key/brand`
tokens and the kit fonts. They are rendered with Playwright (`sign-in/render.cjs`, the same harness
as the UI-kit boards) into `sign-in/shots/<frame>-<desktop|phone>-<dark|light>.png`. Re-render with:

```sh
NODE_PATH=packages/admin/node_modules node docs/design/sign-in/render.cjs
```

The script fails on a console error, a font that does not load, or horizontal overflow in a phone
frame. **Desktop renders.** The desktop render of frames 01–22 is the desktop browser's viewport
