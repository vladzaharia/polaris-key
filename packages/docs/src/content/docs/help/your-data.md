This page says in plain words what an app that uses Polaris Key reads from your device, what its
developer sees, and what you can ask for.

## What an app reads from your device

- **Computer identifiers**, such as your computer's ID and serial number, its network card address
  and its disk ID. The app scrambles each one on your device. Polaris Key never receives the
  original values.
  - An app uses them to count your devices against your license, to give a free license to one
    computer only once, and to notice a changed computer.
  - Apps that run in a web browser don't read them.
- **System details**: your operating system and its version, your computer's model, processor,
  number of processor cores and memory, your language and time zone, and the version of the app.
  Polaris Key receives these as they are, and the app's developer can see them.
  - They let the developer see which systems the app runs on, check that it works on yours and send
    it the right settings.
  - Apps that run in a web browser send the kind of operating system, processor cores, memory,
    language, time zone and the version of the app.
- **Your computer's name**, as set in your system settings. It can include your own name, and you
  can change it there. Apps you install usually send it, so you can tell your devices apart in your
  library. The developer sees it too. Apps that run in a web browser send none.
- **A few named apps**: a developer can ask whether specific apps, such as a companion app, are
  installed. The app can't list the apps you have.

An app doesn't read your user name on the computer, your files or your browsing. A developer can
turn the computer identifiers off. The system details are still sent.

## What the developer sees

- A random ID that exists for that app only. Another app sees a different ID for you.
- Your licenses for the app, its devices with their system details and names, and the kind of
  sign-in you used, such as Steam.
- Your screen name and picture, only if you agree to share them.
- Your email, only if the developer already has it from your purchase or you agree to share it.

A developer never sees your Polaris Key account ID, your other sign-in methods, your birth date or
anything from another app.

## What Polaris Key holds

Polaris Key holds the details you give your account: email addresses, sign-in methods, name,
picture and an optional birth date. It also keeps a list of the browsers signed in to your account
and a record of changes to your sign-in methods.
For each device, it keeps the scrambled computer identifiers, never the originals, until the device
is removed. It keeps your computer's name and the last system details your device sent for as long
as the license exists. You can change your account details in
[Your account and sign-in methods](/docs/help/account/).

## Ask about or erase your data

- Polaris Key doesn't publish a privacy address. [Get help with an app](/docs/help/contact/) says
  who to ask about your Polaris Key account.
- To ask what an app holds about you, such as your licenses and devices, ask the app's developer.
  Ask the developer too if you want it to erase its records.
- To erase your Polaris Key account, see [Delete your account](/docs/help/delete-account/).
  Deleting it doesn't erase a developer's own records.
